## The draft is more formalizable than it looks — and can be made Bernstein–Widder-free

The proofs in §§4–7 were written unusually elementarily — the null-array argument via the inequality |u − (1−e⁻ᵘ)| ≤ u²/2 plus cone-closure, uniqueness via a scalar ODE, Bochner-FTC instead of Hille–Yosida, Bohr–Mollerup instead of general functional-equation theory — and that profile is exactly what formalizes well. But the decisive move is definitional. Don't define Bernstein functions by derivative signs; define admissibility **representation-first**:

$$\texttt{HasLevyRep}\ F\ :\iff\ \exists\, b_0 \ge 0,\ \nu,\ \ F(s) = b_0 s + \int (1 - e^{-st})\,\nu(dt),$$

with k and ν built on Mathlib's `StieltjesFunction` (which exists and is good). Then, walking the paper: the synthesis direction of Theorem 7.3 constructs the kernel measures as explicit weak limits of compound-Poisson-with-drift laws (truncate ν), needing only a Laplace continuity theorem, not Bernstein–Widder; the analysis direction's key step, Theorem 5.2, produces the limit exponent ∫(1−e⁻ˢᵗ)Πₙ(dt) with ∫(1∧t)dΠₙ uniformly bounded, so the Lévy measures live in a compact set of measures on the compactified [0, ∞] — escaping mass at 0 becomes the drift b₀, at ∞ the killing term excluded by g(0+) = 0 — and the closure needed is a Prokhorov/vague-compactness argument, again not Bernstein–Widder. The two transform facts you do need are cheap by Mathlib standards: **injectivity** of the Laplace transform for finite measures on [0,∞) falls out of Stone–Weierstrass (which Mathlib has) applied on the one-point compactification, and the **continuity theorem** on the half-line comes from tightness plus Prokhorov (in Mathlib) or, if the characteristic-function Lévy continuity work has merged (it was in flight around my horizon — verify `ProbabilityTheory.charFun` and its convergence theorems), by restriction. The classical CM ⟺ representation equivalence then becomes an optional appendix theorem rather than a load-bearing import. That single restructuring probably halves the project.

## Inventory, hedged

Solidly there: measure theory with Bochner integration, Fubini/Tonelli; convolution of functions and of measures; `StieltjesFunction`; Banach–Alaoglu and Riesz–Markov (for the Wendel-style Lemma 4.1, together with Mathlib's bump-function approximate identities); Prokhorov and the portmanteau API; FTC for interval Bochner integrals and Grönwall/Picard–Lindelöf (enough for Theorem 10.4's structure); `iteratedDeriv` and the convexity API; the Gamma function with — importantly for you — **Bohr–Mollerup already formalized** (`Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Gamma.BohrMollerup`), which is most of what the order-2 case of Theorem 12.5 needs, with the Krull–Webster instance for g(z) = c(z+a) an adaptation of that file rather than new theory; Gamma and exponential distributions; and — a genuine gift from the analytic-number-theory formalizations — the **Mellin transform with an inversion theorem**, which means §11 is less hopeless than one would guess if you ever extend the project there.

Solidly absent: Bernstein–Widder and any completely-monotone/Bernstein-function theory; Lévy–Khintchine, infinite divisibility, subordinators, self-decomposability; Wendel/multiplier theory; Hille–Yosida; one-sided stable laws; Bessel-K. Uncertain, worth a Zulip/Loogle check before you build: a named Laplace transform API (there's `mgf`, and defining your own via `∫⁻` first, real-valued second, is trivial anyway), the merged state of Lévy continuity, Gauss's multiplication formula beyond Legendre duplication, and Laguerre polynomials. Also worth asking on the Zulip probability stream whether anyone has Lévy-process or infinite-divisibility formalization in progress — that community would likely welcome a `BernsteinFunctions` / Lévy–Khintchine-on-the-half-line contribution, and the half-line case (Laplace, no oscillation) is markedly easier than the ℝ case, so you'd be landing the easiest genuinely-missing piece.

## A milestone ladder

M0: definitions (measures on ℝ supported in [0,∞) rather than on a bespoke half-line type, so `Measure.conv` and the convolution API apply directly; exponents via `lintegral` first), Laplace transform, injectivity via Stone–Weierstrass. M1: the vanishing Lemma 2.4 and Lemma 7.1 — small, self-contained, and they stress-test the definitions. M2: Theorem 7.3 ⇐, giving an end-to-end formalized construction of the scale spaces — I'd make this the first public artifact, since it's the statement "these kernels exist and satisfy the axioms," which is what a formalization skeptic wants certified. M3: Lemma 4.1 (Alaoglu + approximate identity). M4: Theorem 5.2 with the compactification argument. M5: the §6 rigidity (order-topology and monotone-bijection lemmas; fiddly but Mathlib's order API is strong). M6: assemble ⇒, completing Theorem 7.3 as an iff. M7, stretch: §12's order-2 classification on top of the BohrMollerup file — which would be a lovely headline, "the Bessel classification, formally verified." Honest sizing: M0–M2 feels like weeks for someone fluent; the full ladder is a real multi-month project, and the gauge construction in M5 is where I'd predict the most unglamorous effort.
