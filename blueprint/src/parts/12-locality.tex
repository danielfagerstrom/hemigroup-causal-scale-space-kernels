Theorem~5 of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} singles out $\alpha = \tfrac12$ --- the heat
signaling equation --- as the unique member of the stable family whose memory-line evolution is
a local, positivity-respecting differential equation. This chapter proves the hemigroup
analogue.

The structure is genuinely two-layered: locality \emph{alone} opens a countable ladder of
admissible local media of every order, and it is the positive maximum principle --- the
requirement that the medium be a physical transport medium --- that closes the ladder at order
two, where it leaves exactly the Bessel family. Throughout we assume (H), work in the
canonical gauge, write $m(z) := \Ex[T_1^{-z}]$, and take $A$ and $B$ as in Chapter~11.

% draft: Definition 12.1
\begin{definition}[locality; positive maximum principle]\label{def:locality-pmp}
\uses{def:inversion-operator}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.IsLocalOfOrder}\leanok
The inversion operator $A$ is \emph{local of order $n$} if it agrees on
$C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ with a differential expression $\sum_{j=0}^{n} c_j(x)\,\partial_x^j$
with $c_j \in C((0,\infty))$ and $c_n \not\equiv 0$. It satisfies the \emph{positive maximum
principle} (PMP) if $(Ag)(x_0) \le 0$ whenever $g \in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ attains a
nonnegative maximum at $x_0$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Formalised}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.IsLocalOfOrder} and \texttt{SatisfiesPMP} in
\texttt{LocalOperator.lean}. Three readings had to be fixed, and each is a choice rather
than a transcription.
\textbf{Locality is bundled as data, not as an existential.} ``Agrees with \emph{some}
differential expression'' would make the coefficients inaccessible, and
\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}'s conclusion is a statement \emph{about} them --- that
$c_j(x) = \gamma_j x^{j-1}$. As a structure with a \texttt{coeff} field the lemma says what it
says here; as a \texttt{Prop} every use would re-obtain coefficients with no way to say they are
the same ones. The price is that locality is then data, so the ($\Leftarrow$) direction of
\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol} concludes \texttt{Nonempty (IsLocalOfOrder c n)} --- which is
the propositional reading, and the one \ref{thm:locality} quantifies over.
\textbf{$C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ means support inside the open half-line}, not merely compact
support in $\mathbb{R}$. The distinction is load-bearing rather than pedantic: it is exactly what
kills the boundary term at the origin in the integrations by parts below, where the weight
$x^{z-1}$ is singular.
\textbf{The maximum principle needs a real-valued reading.} $A$ is complex-valued here, and
$(Ag)(x_0) \le 0$ is not a statement about a complex number. The Lean version quantifies over
real-valued test functions and asserts $\operatorname{Re}(Ag)(x_0) \le 0$ --- weaker than first
proving that $A$ preserves realness, and therefore the safer of the two.)}
\end{definition}

% draft: Lemma 12.2
\begin{lemma}[covariant local = polynomial symbol]\label{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}
\uses{def:locality-pmp,def:inversion-operator,lem:mellin-data}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.exists_symbol_eq_of_isLocalOfOrder}\notready
$A$ is local of order $n$ if and only if $B$ is a polynomial of degree $n$; in that case the
covariance built into the Mellin class forces the coefficients
\[
  c_j(x) \;=\; \gamma_j\, x^{\,j-1}, \qquad
  A \;=\; \frac{1}{x}\sum_{j=0}^n \gamma_j\,\theta(\theta-1)\cdots(\theta-j+1)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{x}\,B(\theta).
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{One direction proved, one stated}, in two halves ---
\texttt{isLocalOfOrder\_of\_symbol\_eq} and
\texttt{exists\_symbol\_eq\_of\_isLocalOfOrder} --- since the two directions need different
things and only one of them needs covariance.
\textbf{($\Leftarrow$) is machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.isLocalOfOrder\_of\_symbol\_eq}, on A17 and nothing
else. \textbf{And it does not use (H).} The chapter states everything under the standing
hypothesis, and this direction turns out not to need it: the symbol identity is a hypothesis, and
everything else --- the Euler factors, the vertical decay, the inversion --- is a fact about test
functions that says nothing about $F$. (H) still does something here, but something different: it
is what makes the statement non-vacuous, the strip $0 < c < z_* - 1$ being inhabited only when
$z_* > 1$. Being used by a proof and being needed for a statement to have content are different
roles, and this node now distinguishes them.
\textbf{The ($\Leftarrow$) direction needed one analytic fact, and it is now proved}: the Mellin
symbol of the Euler operator,
$\mathcal{M}\bigl[x^{j} g^{(j)}\bigr](z) = \bigl(\prod_{i<j}(-z-i)\bigr)\,\tilde g(z)$ for test
functions $g$, which is $j$ integrations by parts against the weight $x^{z-1}$ with no boundary
terms. Mathlib carries no Mellin/derivative interface at all --- \texttt{MellinTransform.lean} and
\texttt{MellinInversion.lean} between them prove nothing about derivatives --- so it is written
from scratch, as \texttt{Hemigroup.mellin\_pow\_mul\_iteratedDeriv}, and \texttt{\#print axioms}
gives Lean core alone. Everything else in that direction is linearity.
\textbf{Where the support hypothesis is spent}, since it is the reason the statement is true:
Mathlib's integration by parts on the whole line asks for differentiability of each factor only on
the \emph{other's} support. The weight $x \mapsto x^{w}$ is differentiable away from the origin,
and $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ says exactly that the origin is not in the region where its
derivative is needed. Compact support alone would leave a boundary term at $0$, where the weight
is singular, and the identity would be false as stated --- which is why \ref{def:locality-pmp}'s
test class is rendered with support inside $(0,\infty)$ and not with compact support in
$\mathbb{R}$.
The factor is isolated as \texttt{mellinEulerFactor} rather than inlined, because the sign
convention is where this chapter's bookkeeping errors would live: Lean's
\texttt{inversionSymbol z} is this chapter's $B(-z)$.
\textbf{The ($\Rightarrow$) direction needs one thing more}, and it is the harder half: that a
differential expression is determined by its coefficients, so that comparing coefficients of
$g^{(j)}(x/\sigma)$ is licensed. In Lean that is a prescribed-jet test-function construction, and
it is the reason this node is the chapter's largest remaining item rather than its
next one.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) $x^{j}\partial_x^{j} = \theta(\theta-1)\cdots(\theta - j + 1)$, so a polynomial
$B$, expanded in falling factorials, gives $x^{-1}B(\theta) = \sum \gamma_j x^{j-1}\partial_x^j$,
a differential expression of order $\deg B$.

($\Rightarrow$) Every operator of \ref{def:inversion-operator} satisfies the covariance
$A\Delta_\sigma = \sigma^{-1}\Delta_\sigma A$, where $(\Delta_\sigma g)(x) = g(x/\sigma)$.
Imposing this on $\sum c_j(x)\partial_x^j$ and comparing coefficients of $g^{(j)}(x/\sigma)$
gives $c_j(x)\sigma^{-j} = \sigma^{-1}c_j(x/\sigma)$, i.e.\ $c_j(x) = c_j(1)x^{j-1}$.
Rewriting via falling factorials, $A$ agrees on $C_c^\infty$ with $x^{-1}P(\theta)$ for a
polynomial $P$; Mellin-transforming on a line and using injectivity gives $B = P$.
\end{proof}

% draft: Lemma 12.3
\begin{lemma}[the moment recursion]\label{lem:moment-recursion}
\uses{lem:local-polynomial-symbol,lem:mellin-data}
Suppose $A$ is local of order $n$, i.e.\ $B$ is a polynomial of degree $n$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B(0) = 0$; write $B(-z) = z\,Q(z)$ with $Q$ a polynomial of degree $n - 1$;
\item $z_* = \infty$: all negative moments of $T_1$ are finite;
\item $m(z+1) = Q(z)\,m(z)$ for all $z > 0$, and $Q > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$.
\end{enumerate}
\statusA\quad\ledger{A13}\quad\emph{(the singularity of a transform with monotonic integrand
at the real point of its axis of convergence; \sscite{widder1941laplace}, Ch.~II, \S5,
Thm.~5b, pp.~58--59. Cited, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A13} carries clause~(2) alone, and only its final step: that a
finite $z_*$ would be a singularity of $\tilde H$, contradicting the analytic continuation the
recursion supplies. Clauses~(1) and~(3) are \textbf{[T]}, proved below from
\ref{lem:mellin-data} by letting $z \downarrow 0$; clause~(1) is machine-checked, as
\ref{lem:symbol-vanishes-at-origin}, which also records that the polynomial hypothesis plays no
part in it. Two cautions recorded in the ledger. First,
the anchor is the theorem number: Widder's own footnote credits the result to Hamburger
(1921), and ``Pringsheim--Landau'' --- the name the draft uses, and the received one --- is
attached to the Dirichlet- and power-series forms rather than to this page. Second, the
hypothesis is monotonicity of the integrand, which holds here because $H = e^{-F}$ is the
transform of a positive random variable; Widder gives on the same page the counterexample
showing a general Laplace integral need have no singularity on its axis.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) By \ref{lem:mellin-data}, $B(-z) = z\,m(z+1)/m(z)$ on the strip. As $z \downarrow 0$,
$m(z) \to 1$ by dominated convergence and $m(z+1) \to m(1) < \infty$ by (H), so $B(0) = 0$;
since $B$ is a polynomial, $Q(z) := B(-z)/z$ is one of degree $n-1$.

(2) On $0 < z < z_* - 1$ we have the identity $\tilde H(z+1) = B(-z)\tilde H(z)$ between
functions analytic on the strip $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$. If $z_* < \infty$, the right
side is analytic at $z = z_* - 1 + \varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$, which extends
$\tilde H$ analytically through the real point $z_*$. But $\tilde H$ is the Mellin transform
of the nonnegative $H$, and \ledger{A13} makes the real endpoint of its convergence strip a
singularity when finite. Hence $z_* = \infty$.

(3) The recursion is now \ref{lem:mellin-data} on all of $(0,\infty)$, and positivity of $Q$
follows from $m > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[log-convexity]\label{lem:log-convexity}
\uses{lem:mellin-data}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.convexOn_log_negMoment}\leanok
$z \mapsto m(z)$ is log-convex on $(0, \infty)$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.convexOn\_log\_negMoment}, together with the
$[0,\infty]$-valued form \texttt{negMoment\_le\_rpow\_mul\_rpow} from which it is read off.
\texttt{\#print axioms} shows A17 and nothing else --- A17 only because $T_1$ is a kernel of the
construction, so every statement about it inherits the entry. None of this chapter's own cited
interfaces is touched.
\textbf{Two readings, and the difference matters.} The $[0,\infty]$ form is
\emph{unconditional}: no standing hypothesis, no finiteness, no strip, since divergence is a
value there rather than a failure. The real-valued form stated above needs $m$ finite and
positive, so the formal statement carries the domain $(0, z_*)$ rather than $(0,\infty)$ ---
which is the same interval once $z_* = \infty$, but that is \ref{lem:moment-recursion}(2), the
one clause of that lemma \ledger{A13} carries, and this node does not presuppose it. Positivity
of $m$ is where (H)'s first clause enters: the integral runs over $(0,\infty)$, so an atom at the
origin would be invisible to it.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$m\bigl(\tfrac{z_1+z_2}{2}\bigr) = \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z_1/2}\,T_1^{-z_2/2}\bigr]
\le m(z_1)^{1/2} m(z_2)^{1/2}$, which is midpoint log-convexity; with continuity of $m$ this
gives log-convexity.

The appeal to continuity is avoidable, and the formalisation does without it: H\"older at the
exponent pair $\bigl(\tfrac1\theta, \tfrac1{1-\theta}\bigr)$, applied to $T_1^{-z_1}$ and
$T_1^{-z_2}$, gives
\[
  m\bigl(\theta z_1 + (1-\theta) z_2\bigr)
    \;=\; \Ex\bigl[(T_1^{-z_1})^{\theta}\,(T_1^{-z_2})^{1-\theta}\bigr]
    \;\le\; m(z_1)^{\theta}\,m(z_2)^{1-\theta}
\]
at every $\theta \in [0,1]$ at once, Cauchy--Schwarz being the case $\theta = \tfrac12$. Since
that inequality is the definition of log-convexity, nothing remains to upgrade.
\end{proof}

% draft: Theorem 12.5
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 5$'$: the locality theorem]\label{thm:locality}
\uses{def:locality-pmp,lem:local-polynomial-symbol,lem:moment-recursion,lem:log-convexity,lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness,prop:bessel-family,ex:bessel-quadratic,cor:semigroup-case,thm:signaling-form,rem:drift-boundary}
Assume (H). The inversion operator $A$ of \ref{thm:signaling-form} is local \emph{and}
satisfies the positive maximum principle \textbf{iff} one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Order 1; pure transport, degenerate.)} $B(\theta) = -c'\theta$ with $c' > 0$: then
$A = -c'\,\partial_x$, the kernels are the deterministic delays $\mu_{0,x} = \delta_{x/c'}$,
and the signaling problem is the transport equation. This member has no smoothing and is
excluded by requiring absolutely continuous kernels (\ref{rem:drift-boundary}).
\item \emph{(Order 2; the Bessel family.)} After normalizing the unit of time so that
$c_2 = 2$: $B(\theta) = 2\,\theta(\theta - a)$ for some $a > 0$; the kernels are the
inverse-gamma family $T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(2\gamma_a)$ of \ref{prop:bessel-family}; and in
the parabolic gauge the signaling problem is the classical degenerate-parabolic PDE
\[
  \partial_t u \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial_x^2 u + \frac{\beta}{x}\,\partial_x u,
  \qquad \beta = \tfrac12 - a, \qquad u(t,0) = f(t),\quad u(0,x) = 0 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
Within case (2), the depth-homogeneous (semigroup) members are exactly $a = \tfrac12$, i.e.\
$\beta = 0$ --- the heat signaling equation --- recovering
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~5, as the zero-drift point of the family. The kernel
moments in case (2) satisfy $\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} < \infty$ iff $n < a$.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A14}\quad\emph{(Courrège's classification of operators satisfying the
positive maximum principle, \sscite{courrege1966sur}, Corollaire~2, p.~2-10. Cited, not
reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A14} carries one step of the ($\Rightarrow$) direction and
nothing else: the order bound, that a local operator satisfying the PMP is a pure second-order
diffusion operator with no jump part --- the locality clause specifically, not the general
integro-differential structure theorem. Note its scan paginates by exposé, so the anchor reads
p.~2-10 and not p.~10.
\textbf{\ledger{A15} used to appear here and no longer does.} It carried the uniqueness of the
log-convex solution of the recursion, which is what pins $m$ to the gamma form. In the order-2
case the recursion has a \emph{single} Gamma factor, and uniqueness is then the classical
Bohr--Mollerup theorem rather than the general Krull--Webster theorem; that case is now proved
in-repo as \ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}, so this node is \textbf{[T]} at that step. The
general entry stays alive at \ref{prop:local-ladder}, whose order-$n$ ladder has $n-1$ Gamma
factors and for which Bohr--Mollerup is not enough. The order-1 case never used the entry:
there $\log m$ is convex with constant unit increments, hence affine, which is elementary and
given below. Everything in the ($\Leftarrow$) direction is \textbf{[T]}: admissibility of the
Bessel family is \ref{prop:bessel-family}, locality is \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}, and the PMP
verification is two lines of calculus. The homogeneity claim is \textbf{[T]} through
\ref{cor:semigroup-case}.)}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) A local operator satisfying the positive maximum principle has order at most
$2$: this is the local case of Courrège's classification, \ledger{A14} --- the jump measure
vanishes by locality, leaving $c_2(x)\partial^2 + c_1(x)\partial + c_0(x)$ with $c_2 \ge 0$ and
$c_0 \le 0$. By \ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}, $\deg B \le 2$, and $B(0) = 0$ by
\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1) --- equivalently, there is no killing, which is (A5).

\emph{Order 1.} Here $Q \equiv c'$ is constant, so $m(z+1) = c'm(z)$ with $m(0)=1$. By
\ref{lem:log-convexity}, $\log m$ is convex with constant unit increments, hence affine; so
$m(z) = c'^{\,z}$. Thus $T_1 = 1/c'$ a.s., $B(-z) = c'z$, i.e.\ $B(\theta) = -c'\theta$ and
$A = -c'x^{-1}\theta = -c'\partial_x$; positivity of $Q$ gives $c' > 0$.

\emph{Order 2.} Here $Q(z) = c_2 z + q_0$ with $c_2 > 0$, by positivity of $Q$ as
$z \to \infty$ in \ref{lem:moment-recursion}(3), and $q_0 = Q(0) = m(1) > 0$; write
$Q(z) = c_2(z + a)$ with $a := q_0/c_2 > 0$. The recursion $m(z+1) = c_2(z+a)\,m(z)$ with
$m(0{+}) = 1$ --- the normalisation being \ref{lem:symbol-vanishes-at-origin}'s first limit ---
has a \emph{unique} log-convex solution by \ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}, namely
\[
  m(z) \;=\; c_2^{\,z}\,\frac{\Gamma(a + z)}{\Gamma(a)}
  \;=\; \Ex\Bigl[\bigl(c_2\,\gamma_a\bigr)^{z}\Bigr],
\]
and $m$ is log-convex by \ref{lem:log-convexity}, hence equals it. Thus
$T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(c_2\gamma_a)$; the time normalization $c_2 = 2$ gives
\ref{prop:bessel-family}, the profile is the Bessel-K function, and the operator is computed
in \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}: $B(\theta) = 2\theta(\theta - a)$, with the parabolic-gauge form
as displayed.

($\Leftarrow$) \emph{Case (1).} Admissibility is the pure-drift member $F(s) = s/c'$ of (7.1),
and a first-order operator satisfies the PMP trivially, since $g'(x_0) = 0$ at an interior
maximum. \emph{Case (2).} Admissibility of the inverse-gamma family is
\ref{prop:bessel-family}; locality is \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}; and a second-order operator
$\tfrac12\partial_x^2 + \tfrac{\beta}{x}\partial_x$ with nonnegative leading coefficient
satisfies the PMP, because at an interior nonnegative maximum $g'(x_0) = 0$ and
$g''(x_0) \le 0$, so $(Ag)(x_0) = \tfrac12 g''(x_0) \le 0$.

\emph{The homogeneity claim.} By \ref{cor:semigroup-case}, depth-homogeneous members have
$F(s) = c\,s^{\alpha}$; within the inverse-gamma family the exponent is a pure power only for
$a = \tfrac12$, where $K_{1/2}$ is elementary and $H(y) = e^{-\sqrt{2y}}$ --- the
$\tfrac12$-stable, i.e.\ heat, case. The moment statement is \ref{prop:bessel-family}.
\end{proof}

% draft: Proposition 12.6
\begin{proposition}[the ladder: locality without the maximum principle]\label{prop:local-ladder}
\uses{thm:main-characterization,lem:local-polynomial-symbol,lem:moment-recursion,lem:log-convexity,thm:locality}
Fix $n \ge 2$, $c' > 0$ and $a_1, \dots, a_{n-1} > 0$, and let
\[
  T_1 \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; \frac{1}{c'\,\gamma_{a_1}\gamma_{a_2}\cdots\gamma_{a_{n-1}}}
  \quad (\text{independent factors}), \qquad
  m(z) = c'^{\,z}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(a_i + z)}{\Gamma(a_i)} .
\]
Then the family is admissible in the sense of \ref{thm:main-characterization} and satisfies
(H), and its inversion operator is \emph{local of order $n$}, with polynomial symbol
\[
  B(-z) \;=\; c'\,z\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z + a_i);
\]
the kernels are Meijer $G$-function densities. Conversely, every local admissible family of
order $n$ has $m$ equal to the unique log-convex solution of $m(z+1) = Q(z)m(z)$ for its
polynomial $Q$; when the roots of $Q$ are real --- necessarily $\le 0$, giving
$Q(z) = c'\prod(z+a_i)$ with $a_i > 0$ --- this is the family above. Whether
complex-conjugate root pairs can support an admissible family is left open; we conjecture not.

For the Meijer $G$ identification: the product $\gamma_{a_1}\cdots\gamma_{a_{n-1}}$ has a
$G^{\,n-1,0}_{\,0,n-1}$ density, for arbitrary distinct shapes, by
\sscite{springer1970distribution}, Thm.~1, p.~722; the reciprocal and the scale factor $c'$ are
then absorbed by the $G$-function transformation rules (\sscite{samko1993fractional},
eqs.~(1.96) and (1.97), p.~22).
\statusA\quad\ledger{A16}\quad\ledger{A15}\quad\emph{(the HCM tower,
\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}, Thm.~5.1.1 p.~69 and Thm.~4.3.1 p.~57 with its hypotheses
on p.~56; and Krull--Webster, \sscite{webster1997log}, Thm.~3.1. Cited, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A16} carries admissibility of the displayed family, in two steps:
that $\HCM$ is closed under products of independent variables and under reciprocals, and that
$\HCM \subset \GGC \subset \SDclass$. A caution the ledger records and the proof must respect:
$\HCM$ is \emph{not} closed under convolution, so only the multiplicative structure is
available here --- and the second inclusion, $\GGC \subset \SDclass$, is asserted by Bondesson
in a single sentence on p.~30 rather than as a numbered theorem, which makes it the thinnest
link in this node. \ledger{A15} carries the converse direction only, exactly as in
\ref{thm:locality}. The Meijer $G$ identification is carried by neither entry and needs
neither: nothing later uses it, and it is cited in the statement to a stated theorem plus two
printed transformation rules. Two things about it are deliberate. It takes \emph{two} sources
because \sscite{springer1970distribution} covers products of gamma variables but not
reciprocals of them --- its only quotient result is for two factors --- so the inversion
routes through \sscite{samko1993fractional}. And the $G$-parameters of $T_1$ itself are not
written out: Springer--Thompson use the density convention
$M\{f\}(s) = \Ex[x^{s-1}]$, under which the parameters are the shapes minus one, whereas the
$m$ of this proposition is the moment convention $\Ex[X^{z}]$, under which they are the shapes;
and the reciprocal shifts them again. The order stated above is for the \emph{product}, where
it is unambiguous. The conjecture about complex-conjugate roots is flagged as open and is
claimed by neither entry.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Admissibility.} Gamma densities are hyperbolically completely monotone, and by
\ledger{A16} the $\HCM$ class is closed under products of independent variables and under
reciprocals, while $\HCM$ laws are generalized gamma convolutions and hence self-decomposable.
So $-\log \Ex e^{-sT_1}$ is of the form (7.1), and (H) holds because all negative moments are
finite and $\mathbb{P}(T_1 = 0) = 0$.

\emph{The symbol.} $B(-z) = z\,m(z+1)/m(z) = c'z\prod(z+a_i)$, a polynomial, so $A$ is local
of order $n$ by \ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}.

\emph{The converse.} This is \ref{lem:moment-recursion} and \ref{lem:log-convexity} together
with \ledger{A15}, as in the proof of \ref{thm:locality}. A real root $-a_i \ge 0$ of $Q$
would contradict $Q > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$ together with $Q(0) = m(1) > 0$, so real roots give
$a_i > 0$, and the Krull--Webster solution is the displayed gamma product.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the stable slice; Gauss multiplication]\label{rem:stable-slice}
\uses{prop:local-ladder,prop:stable-family,cor:semigroup-case}
Depth-homogeneity intersects the ladder at the equally spaced parameters $a_j = j/n$ and
$c' = n^n$: by the Gauss multiplication formula,
\[
  m(z) \;=\; n^{nz}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(j/n + z)}{\Gamma(j/n)}
  \;=\; n\,\frac{\Gamma(nz)}{\Gamma(z)}, \qquad
  \tilde H(z) = n\,\Gamma(nz), \qquad H(y) = e^{-y^{1/n}},
\]
the $\tfrac1n$-stable laws --- recovering the classical identity that represents the
$\tfrac1n$-stable subordinator as a scaled reciprocal product of $n-1$ gamma variables. Thus
the local cases $\alpha = 1/n$ of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} are precisely the
homogeneous slice of the ladder, and each unfolds under the hemigroup axioms into an
$(n-1)$-parameter inhomogeneous family of local media. The maximum principle eliminates
$n \ge 3$ in both settings; what remains at $n = 2$ is the single point $\alpha = \tfrac12$
there, and the Bessel line here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what the theorem settles]\label{rem:trilemma}
\uses{thm:locality,rem:heavy-tails,prop:gamma-family,prop:bessel-family}
\ref{thm:locality} completes the resolution of the practicality problem of
\ref{rem:heavy-tails} \emph{within the local class}: the drift weight $\beta = \tfrac12 - a$
is a design parameter, and any prescribed number of finite kernel moments is achievable by a
local, positivity-respecting, continuously scale-covariant, time-causal medium, namely
$n < a$. The residual trade-off is the tail: inverse-gamma kernels have polynomial tails
$t^{-1-a}$ for every finite $a$, so \emph{all} moments --- exponential localization --- still
require leaving the local class for the Gamma-type corner of \ref{prop:gamma-family}.

Homogeneity, locality and moment control thus interact as follows: homogeneity together with
covariance forces stable kernels, which have no mean; locality together with covariance and
the PMP allows any finite number of moments but polynomial tails; full moment control requires
nonlocal delay accrual.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[the symbol vanishes at the origin]\label{lem:symbol-vanishes-at-origin}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.tendsto_inversionSymbol_nhdsGT_zero}\leanok
Assume (H). Then, along the reals,
\[
  m(z) \;\longrightarrow\; 1
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  B(-z) \;=\; \frac{z\,m(z+1)}{m(z)} \;\longrightarrow\; 0
  \qquad (z \downarrow 0).
\]
Consequently $B(0) = 0$ for any $B$ continuous at the origin --- in particular whenever $B$ is a
polynomial, which is \ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1).
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.tendsto\_inversionSymbol\_nhdsGT\_zero}, with the
first limit as \texttt{tendsto\_negMoment\_nhdsGT\_zero}. \texttt{\#print axioms} shows A17 and
nothing else; \ledger{A13} is not touched.
\textbf{Why this is separate from \ref{lem:moment-recursion}.} That lemma states clause~(1) under
the hypothesis that $B$ is a polynomial of degree $n$, and gets $B(0) = 0$ from this limit by
continuity. \textbf{The polynomial hypothesis is inert in the argument}: what the proof uses is
that $m(z) \to 1$, that $m(z+1)$ stays bounded, and that $z \to 0$ --- none of which mentions
$B$'s form. Stating the limit without it is what makes the machine-checked content visible, and
keeps a node that spends \ledger{A13} on clause~(2) from appearing to spend it on clause~(1) as
well. This is the same distinction the article draws for \ref{lem:mellin-vertical}: what a proof
cites is an upper bound on what its statement needs.
\textbf{Where (H) enters, in both clauses and differently.} Its first clause, no atom at zero
delay, is what makes every $m(z)$ positive, the integral defining $m$ running over $(0,\infty)$;
its second, $z_* > 1$, is what makes the dominating function of the first limit integrable and
the interval $(1, z_*)$ --- from which the bound on $m(z+1)$ is taken --- nonempty.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\emph{The first limit.} Dominated convergence. For $t > 0$ the integrand $t^{-z} = e^{-z\log t}$
tends to $1$ as $z \to 0$; for $0 < z \le 1$ it is dominated by $1 + t^{-1}$, which is
$\mu_{0,1}$-integrable because (H) places $1$ strictly below $z_*$. Hence
$m(z) = \Ex\,T_1^{-z} \to \Ex\,1 = 1$.

\emph{The second.} Fix $\zeta \in (1, z_*)$, nonempty by (H). For $0 < z \le \zeta - 1$ we have
$z + 1 \le \zeta$, and splitting the integral at $t = 1$ --- below it $t^{-(z+1)} \le t^{-\zeta}$,
above it $t^{-(z+1)} \le 1$ --- gives $m(z+1) \le m(\zeta) + 1$, a bound independent of $z$. So
$z\,m(z+1) \to 0$, while $m(z) \to 1 \ne 0$ by the first limit, and the quotient tends to $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the shifted Gamma recursion; Krull--Webster at a linear $Q$]
\label{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}
\uses{lem:log-convexity}
\lean{Hemigroup.eq_gamma_form_of_logConvex_of_recursion}\leanok
Let $c, a > 0$ and let $m : (0,\infty) \to (0,\infty)$ be log-convex and satisfy
\[
  m(z+1) \;=\; c\,(z + a)\,m(z) \quad (z > 0),
  \qquad m(z) \to 1 \ \ (z \downarrow 0).
\]
Then
\[
  m(z) \;=\; c^{\,z}\,\frac{\Gamma(a + z)}{\Gamma(a)} \qquad (z > 0).
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.eq\_gamma\_form\_of\_logConvex\_of\_recursion}. \texttt{\#print axioms} gives
\textbf{Lean core alone} --- not even A17, since the statement names no kernel. That is the check
that the ledger entry is discharged rather than relocated.
\textbf{What this replaces.} \ref{thm:locality}'s order-2 case applied \ledger{A15}, the
Krull--Webster uniqueness theorem, to exactly this recursion. With $Q$ linear the recursion has a
single Gamma factor, and Krull--Webster is then the classical Bohr--Mollerup theorem, which
Mathlib carries. \ref{prop:local-ladder} is a different matter: its order-$n$ ladder has $n-1$
Gamma factors, log-convexity alone does not reduce to Bohr--Mollerup there, and \ledger{A15}
remains.
\textbf{The one point of technique.} Bohr--Mollerup pins a function on $(0,\infty)$ satisfying
$f(y+1) = y f(y)$; ours carries the shift $a$. Substituting $f(y) = m(y-a)\cdots$ would need $m$
below the interval it is given on, and extending it downward through the recursion is an
induction on $\lceil a \rceil$ that also leaves the domain where log-convexity was established.
Shifting the other way costs nothing, which is the proof below.
\textbf{An honest note on the trade.} This is \emph{longer} than the citation it removes ---
unlike the five substitutions recorded after \ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}, where the elementary
route was also the shorter one. What is bought is the trust base, not the exposition.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Choose an integer $n \ge a$ and put $d := n - a \ge 0$. Define, for $y > 0$,
\[
  f(y) \;:=\; \frac{m(y + d)\; c^{-(y+d)}}{P(y)},
  \qquad P(y) := \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(y + j),
\]
which uses $m$ only on $(d, \infty) \subseteq (0,\infty)$. Two shifts cancel: the recursion gives
$m(y + 1 + d) = c\,(y + n)\,m(y+d)$, since $d + a = n$, while $P(y+1)\,y = P(y)\,(y+n)$; together
with $c^{-(y+1+d)} = c^{-(y+d)}c^{-1}$ this leaves
\[
  f(y+1) \;=\; y\,f(y) \qquad (y > 0).
\]
$f$ is positive, and $\log f(y) = \log m(y+d) - (y+d)\log c - \log P(y)$ is convex on $(0,\infty)$:
the first term is \ref{lem:log-convexity} precomposed with a translation, the second is affine, and
the third is convex because $-\log(y+j)$ is, $\log$ being concave. Bohr--Mollerup applied to
$f/f(1)$ therefore gives $f = f(1)\,\Gamma$ on $(0,\infty)$.

Write $C := f(1)$ and $G(z) := c^{\,z}\Gamma(z+a)$. Unwinding the definition of $f$ at
$y = z - d$ and using $P(y)\,\Gamma(y) = \Gamma(y+n)$ with $y + n = z + a$ gives $m(z) = C\,G(z)$
for every $z > d$. Both $m$ and $G$ satisfy the same recursion on $(0,\infty)$, so
$m(z + k)\,G(z) = m(z)\,G(z+k)$ for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$; choosing $k$ with $z + k > d$ and
cancelling $G(z+k) > 0$ extends $m = C\,G$ to all of $(0,\infty)$. Finally $z \downarrow 0$ gives
$C\,\Gamma(a) = 1$, since $c^{\,z} \to 1$ and $\Gamma$ is continuous at $a > 0$.
\end{proof}
