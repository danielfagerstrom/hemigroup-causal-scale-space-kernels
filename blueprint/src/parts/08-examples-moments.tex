\begin{proposition}[the extremal stable family: the exponent]\label{prop:stable-family}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,prop:admissibility-criterion,lem:memory-kernel}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.stableExponent_toRealExponent}\leanok
For $0 < \alpha < 1$ the exponent $F(s) = s^\alpha$ is of the form (7.1), with Lévy density
factor
\[
  k(t) = \frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\, t^{-\alpha},
\]
which is nonincreasing.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.stableExponent\_toRealExponent}, on the kernel
\texttt{stableExponent}; \texttt{\#print axioms} reduces to Lean core. The proof is the same
antiderivative argument as \ref{prop:gamma-family}, at the Gamma integral's exponent $1-\alpha$
in place of $1$: $F'(s) = \frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^\infty t^{-\alpha}e^{-st}dt =
\alpha s^{\alpha-1}$, which is the derivative of $s^\alpha$, and both sides vanish at $0{+}$ ---
the second by $F(0{+}) = 0$ and the first because $\alpha > 0$. So the ``standard integral
representation'' the draft cites is not assumed here but derived, and $\Gamma(1-\alpha)$ enters
as the constant Mathlib's Gamma integral produces rather than as a normalisation chosen to make
the answer come out.}

\emph{\textbf{This kernel is what separates the two halves of
\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion}.} $k$ is \emph{unbounded} at the origin and integrable there
only because $\alpha < 1$; it is integrable against $dt/t$ at infinity only because
$\alpha > 0$. Each side of the hypothesis $0 < \alpha < 1$ is used exactly once, on a different
half, which is why the criterion could not have been stated with a single condition.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$s^\alpha = \frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,t^{-\alpha-1}\,dt$ is
the standard integral representation, which is (7.1) with $b_0 = 0$ and
$k(t)/t = \alpha t^{-\alpha-1}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)$; $k$ is a negative power, hence
nonincreasing, and both integrability conditions of \ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} hold
($t^{-\alpha}$ integrable at $0$, $t^{-\alpha-1}$ integrable at infinity), so $F$ is finite and
of the form (7.1).
\end{proof}

\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.gammaExponent_toRealExponent}\leanok
For $\gamma > 0$ the exponent $F(s) = \gamma\,\log(1 + s)$ is of the form (7.1) with
$k(t) = \gamma\, e^{-t}$, which is nonincreasing.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.gammaExponent\_toRealExponent}, on the kernel
\texttt{gammaExponent} of \ref{prop:admissibility-criterion}; \texttt{\#print axioms} reduces to

\emph{\textbf{The proof is not the draft's.} The draft quotes Frullani's integral, which Mathlib
does not have. What it does have --- once \ref{lem:memory-kernel} is proved --- is the
antiderivative reading of the same fact: $F' (s) = \gamma\!\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+s)t}dt =
\gamma/(1+s)$ by the $a=1$ case of the Gamma integral, and $\log(1+s)$ has the same derivative
and the same limit $0$ at the origin. The limit is the clause that needs an argument, and it is
supplied once for the whole class as $F(0{+}) = 0$
(\texttt{tendsto\_toRealExponent\_nhdsGT\_zero}), by dominated convergence against the $s=1$
integrand. That is exactly the classical proof of Frullani's integral, so nothing is smuggled in;
what changes is that the citation becomes a computation.}

\emph{One normalisation is not free: the Lean structure fixes $k(0) = 0$, so the kernel is
$\gamma e^{-t}$ guarded by $\mathbf{1}_{t>0}$. Without the guard the field is unprovable rather
than merely inelegant, and $\{0\}$ being null costs nothing downstream.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The Frullani integral gives
$\log(1+s) = \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,e^{-t}\,t^{-1}\,dt$, so $F$ has the form (7.1) with
$b_0 = 0$ and $k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$, nonincreasing, and both integrability conditions of
\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} hold ($e^{-t}$ bounded near $0$, integrable at infinity), so
$F$ is finite and of the form (7.1); hence $F$ is admissible by
\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the Bessel-K family; preview of the memory line]\label{prop:bessel-family}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,thm:main-characterization,lem:criterion-converse,prop:admissibility-criterion}
For $a > 0$ let $T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(2\gamma_a)$ be the scaled inverse-gamma law, where
$\gamma_a$ has the Gamma density of shape $a$, and let $F$ be its Laplace exponent. Then
\[
  e^{-F(s)} = \frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma(a)}\,\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)^{a} K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr),
\]
and $T_1$ is self-decomposable, so $F$ is of the form (7.1) and the family is admissible. The
moments satisfy $\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ if and only if $a > n$.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A8}\quad\emph{(self-decomposability of the generalized inverse Gaussian
laws; \sscite{halgreen1979self}. A cited interface, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A8} carries exactly one clause: that $T_1$ is self-decomposable,
which is what makes $F$ admissible and puts the family inside
\ref{thm:main-characterization}. Everything else is \textbf{[T]} and proved below --- the
evaluation of the transform in terms of $K_a$ is a classical integral representation, and the
moments are a direct Gamma-integral computation from $T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$, not routed through
\ref{prop:moment-criterion}: the edge to that node is dropped below along with the sentence that
used it (2026-08-29). Note also that Halgreen proves the
stronger statement that the law is a generalized Gamma convolution; self-decomposability
follows through $\GGC \subset \SDclass$, which is a clause of \ledger{A16} --- so A8 and A16
rest on the same tower and should be reviewed together.}

\emph{\textbf{Two sentences moved out of the statement (2026-08-23).} The parabolic-gauge
identification of these kernels with the Bessel-type media
$\tfrac12\partial_x^2 + \tfrac{\beta}{x}\partial_x$, $a = \tfrac12 - \beta$, is not this node's
content --- it is \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}'s, in Chapter~9, which already depends on this one
and states the identification precisely, in its own gauge notation
(\ref{rem:gauge-freedom}'s parabolic gauge $\xi$); restating it here used the older, now-superseded
symbol $\tilde x$ for it and said the same thing a second time. Likewise the closing pointer to
the memory-line theorems is prose, not a mathematical clause of this proposition, and now lives
at the point of citation instead.}
\end{proposition}

% CORRECTED 2026-08-29: the proof's closing sentence asserted a Lévy-density asymptotic,
% "k(t) ~ a as t -> 0", that contradicts the exponent's own growth. k(0+) < infty with b_0 = 0
% forces F(s) = O(log s) as s -> infty (a bounded density integrates against dt/t to a
% logarithmically divergent tail at best); but the display two lines above gives, via the K_a
% asymptotic at infinity, F(s) ~ sqrt(2s), which is not O(log s). The true small-t behaviour is
% the stable-1/2 singularity k(t) ~ (2 pi t)^{-1/2} (inverse-gamma laws are heavy-tailed at their
% own origin), not a finite limit; the tail claim "decays like t^{-a}" was also never cited.
% Nothing downstream used the clause: the moment criterion route consumed only the tail exponent,
% and the direct computation below needs no Levy-density asymptotics at all, at either end.
% Librarian-verified against halgreen1979self, printed p. 15 (2026-08-29): the Thorin density
% g_v there has g_v(x) ~ x^{-1/2} as x -> infty and ~ x^{v-1} as x -> 0, which by a
% Watson's-lemma step give exactly k(t) ~ c t^{-1/2} at 0 and ~ c' t^{-a} at infinity -- so both
% ends are now anchored should the Levy-asymptotics route ever be wanted; ledger A8's pp. 13-15
% anchors were re-verified against the page images in the same pass.
\begin{proof}
Write $\gamma_a$ for a Gamma$(a,1)$ variable and $T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$. Substituting
$u = 1/(2t)$ in the Gamma density gives the density of $T_1$,
\[
  \phi_1(t) = \frac{t^{-a-1}\,e^{-1/(2t)}}{2^{a}\,\Gamma(a)}, \qquad t > 0 .
\]
The classical integral representation
$\int_0^\infty t^{\nu-1}\exp(-A/t - Bt)\,dt = 2\,(A/B)^{\nu/2}K_\nu\bigl(2\sqrt{AB}\bigr)$,
taken at $\nu = -a$, $A = \tfrac12$, $B = s$, together with $K_{-a} = K_a$, gives
\[
  \Ex\,e^{-sT_1}
  = \frac{1}{2^{a}\Gamma(a)}\int_0^\infty t^{-a-1}e^{-st - 1/(2t)}\,dt
  = \frac{2\,(2s)^{a/2}\,K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)}{2^{a}\Gamma(a)}
  = \frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma(a)}\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)^{a}K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr).
\]
Self-decomposability of $T_1$ is \ledger{A8}; by
\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} its exponent is of the form (7.1), and both integrability
conditions of \ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} hold for the resulting $(b_0,k)$ by
\ref{lem:criterion-converse}, since $F(1) = -\log\Ex\,e^{-T_1} < \infty$; so $F$ is finite and
\ref{thm:main-characterization} makes the family admissible. Finally, since $T_x \stackrel{d}{=}
x\,T_1$ in the canonical gauge, for every $x > 0$ and $n \in \NN$,
\[
  \Ex\,T_x^n = x^n\,\Ex\,T_1^n = \frac{x^n}{2^n}\,\Ex\bigl[\gamma_a^{-n}\bigr],
  \qquad
  \Ex\bigl[\gamma_a^{-n}\bigr]
  = \frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_0^\infty u^{a-n-1}e^{-u}\,du
  = \frac{\Gamma(a-n)}{\Gamma(a)},
\]
the last integral (hence the moment) finite exactly when $a - n > 0$; so $\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ iff
$a > n$. This is a direct computation from the defining substitution $T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$ and
cites no Lévy-density asymptotics.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the mean delay and the influence curve]\label{prop:moments}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,thm:main-characterization}
\lean{Hemigroup.moments}\leanok
$\Ex\,T_x = x\,F'(\zp) = x\bigl(b_0 + \int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt\bigr)$, finite if and only if $k$
is integrable at infinity.
\statusT\quad\emph{(monotone convergence in (7.1) and nothing else --- no citation is needed and
none is made. \textbf{The influence-curve identification} (\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S7)
moved out of the statement into prose at the point of citation (2026-08-23): it restates this
node's linearity in different language rather than adding a further fact, and the statement of
record should carry only the latter. The higher-moment criterion, which \emph{does} need one, was
split off as
\ref{prop:moment-criterion}; before the split this node reported that entry's cost for a result
that does not incur it. What is \emph{not} claimed here is anything about the mode, which is the
delay measure the stable family forces one to use --- see \ref{prop:stable-family}.
\textbf{Proved in Lean} (2026-08-14). The mean rate is
$[0,\infty]$-valued, so the ``finite if and only if'' clause is
\texttt{meanRate\_ne\_top\_iff} with both sides allowed to fail and no finiteness hypothesis
anywhere. \textbf{And the linearity of the influence curve does not depend on the identity}, which
is the reverse of the order used above: $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $x T_1$, so every moment scales
by a change of variables whether or not it is finite, and the identity is needed only at $x = 1$.
That one instance, $\Ex T_1 = F'(\zp)$, is reached without differentiating anything: the
difference quotient $\bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)/s$ is \emph{nonincreasing} in $s$, so the proof is
monotone convergence twice --- once on the law, once inside (7.1) --- joined by the squeeze
$we^{-w} \le 1 - e^{-w} \le w$ at $w = F(s_n) \to 0$. The lower bound is the upper one times a
factor tending to $1$, which in $[0,\infty]$ multiplies through with no side condition, so the
infinite case needs no separate treatment --- and it is exactly the case a real-valued argument
would have had to exclude.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Differentiating the transform at the origin,
$\Ex\,T_x = -\partial_s e^{-F(xs)}\big|_{s=\zp} = x F'(\zp)$, and monotone convergence in
(7.1) gives $F'(\zp) = b_0 + \int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt$, finite exactly when $k$ is integrable at
infinity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[heavy tails are the semigroup's doing]\label{rem:heavy-tails}
\uses{thm:main-characterization,cor:semigroup-case,prop:moment-criterion,prop:moments,prop:gamma-moments,prop:bessel-family}
The heavy tails of the 2005 kernels are an artifact of the semigroup axiom, not of causality
together with covariance. Within \ref{thm:main-characterization} the semigroup sub-case
(\ref{cor:semigroup-case}) is the subset with $k$ a pure power $c\,t^{-\alpha}$,
$0 < \alpha < 1$, or the pure delay, $k \equiv 0$ with $b_0 > 0$ --- the only data compatible
with homogeneity. A pure power is never integrable at infinity, so by
\ref{prop:moments} no stable member has a mean. Any other nonincreasing $k$ is
admitted, and its moments are governed by \ref{prop:moment-criterion} alone:
$\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ exactly when $\int_1^\infty t^{n-1}k(t)\,dt < \infty$. For
$k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$ every moment is finite (\ref{prop:gamma-moments}); for
the Bessel family with shape $a$ exactly the moments of order below $a$ are finite
(\ref{prop:bessel-family}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[the moment criterion]\label{prop:moment-criterion}
\uses{prop:moments,lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,thm:main-characterization}
$\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} < \infty$ if and only if $\int_1^\infty t^{\,n-1} k(t)\,dt < \infty$.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A7}\quad\emph{(the moment criterion for infinitely divisible laws;
\sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~25.3. A cited interface, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A7} carries the equivalence itself and nothing else --- that a
moment of the law is finite exactly when the corresponding integral against the Lévy measure
over $\{t > 1\}$ is finite. Translating that integral into the stated one is \textbf{[T]}: the
Lévy measure of $T_x$ is $\nu(dt) = k(t)t^{-1}dt$ up to the scaling by $x$, so
$\int_{t>1}t^n\,\nu(dt) = \int_1^\infty t^{\,n-1}k(t)\,dt$. The $n = 1$ case is \emph{not}
carried by the ledger --- it is \ref{prop:moments}, proved by monotone convergence --- which is
the reason for the split.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Lévy measure of $T_x$ is $\nu(dt) = k(t)t^{-1}dt$ up to the scaling by $x$, so
$\int_{t>1} t^n\,\nu(dt) = \int_1^\infty t^{\,n-1}k(t)\,dt$; \ledger{A7} equates finiteness of
that integral with finiteness of $\Ex\,T_x^n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[admissibility criterion for a memory kernel]\label{prop:admissibility-criterion}
\uses{def:levy-exponent}
\lean{Hemigroup.levyExponentD_ne_top_of_integrableOn}\leanok
Let $b_0 \ge 0$ and let $k : (0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ be nonincreasing with
\[
  \int_0^1 k(t)\,dt < \infty
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \int_1^\infty \frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt < \infty .
\]
Then $F(s) = b_0 s + \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,k(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}$ is finite for every
$s \ge 0$.

Consequently $F$ is of the form (7.1), and if $F \not\equiv 0$ \ref{thm:main-characterization}
applies to it.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.levyExponentD\_ne\_top\_of\_integrableOn}; \texttt{\#print axioms} reduces to
Lean core. Elementary, and stated separately because it is the \emph{only} clause with content
in exhibiting a concrete admissible exponent: the other five fields of the Lean structure ---
nonnegativity of $b_0$ and of $k$, monotonicity of $k$, and the normalisation $k(0)=0$ --- are
immediate for every kernel in this chapter, and this one is not.}

\emph{The closing reference to \ref{thm:main-characterization} is a consequence and not part of
the statement, which is why this node's dependency list records only \ref{def:levy-exponent}:
convergence is all the estimates below establish, and admissibility is what
\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} then reads off it.}

\emph{The two-sided split is forced by the integrand rather than chosen for convenience.
Near the origin $1 - e^{-st}$ is comparable to $st$, which cancels the $1/t$ and leaves $k$
itself; at infinity it is bounded by $1$, which leaves $k(t)/t$. The two halves therefore test
genuinely different things, and the examples separate them: the stable family's $k$ is
\emph{unbounded} at the origin and integrable there all the same
(\ref{prop:stable-family}), while a Dickman ray's $k$ has compact support and so passes the
second test trivially.}

\emph{\textbf{Two instances are built in Lean from it}, as non-vacuity checks on remarks that
would otherwise assert the existence of members of the class without exhibiting one:
\texttt{Hemigroup.gammaExponent}, giving the leaky integrator $k = e^{-t}$ of
\ref{rem:heavy-tails} at $\gamma = 1$, and \texttt{Hemigroup.dickmanExponent}, giving the
Dickman ray $k = \mathbf{1}_{(0,\tau)}$ that \ref{rem:extreme-rays} identifies as an extreme ray
of the admissible cone. Each is a witness that the cone in question is inhabited where the
remark says it is; neither yet carries that remark's closed form, which is
\ref{prop:gamma-family}'s business.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Split the integral at $t = 1$. On $(0,1]$ use $1 - e^{-st} \le st$, which is $1 + x \le e^x$ at
$x = -st$; the integrand is then at most $s\,k(t)$, integrable by the first hypothesis. On
$(1,\infty)$ use $1 - e^{-st} \le 1$; the integrand is at most $k(t)/t$, integrable by the
second. The drift term is finite for every real $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the criterion is characteristic]\label{lem:criterion-converse}
\uses{prop:admissibility-criterion,def:levy-exponent}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.integrableOn_of_ne_top}\leanok
Let $b_0 \ge 0$ and let $k : (0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ be nonincreasing. If the $[0,\infty]$-valued
exponent (7.1) at $s = 1$,
\[
  b_0 + \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-t})\,k(t)\,\frac{dt}{t} ,
\]
is finite, then $\int_0^1 k(t)\,dt < \infty$ and $\int_1^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.integrableOn\_of\_ne\_top}; \texttt{\#print axioms}
reduces to Lean core. \textbf{Restated (2026-08-23) from the Lean hypothesis directly}, rather
than through ``$F$ is of the form (7.1)'': that phrase already packages the two integrability
conditions in as part of Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}(3), which made the previous
wording tautological. What has content is that finiteness of the exponent at the single point
$s = 1$ --- nothing about $s$ elsewhere, and no appeal to admissibility already holding --- is
enough to force both integrals; that is what changes \ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} from a
convenient sufficient condition into a characterisation, so that the two integrals are the right
invariants of the kernel and not an artifact of how the estimate was arranged. It also has a
practical consequence for the Lean development --- every admissible $F$ carries both
integrability facts \emph{already}, so the analysis of the appendix needs no hypothesis beyond
membership of the class, and in particular $F'$ may be differentiated under the integral sign
without one.)}

\emph{The proof is the same inequality read in the other direction. Where
\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} used $1 - e^{-u} \le u$, this uses
$1 - e^{-u} \ge u/(1+u)$, which is $e^u \ge 1+u$ rearranged; the two together pin $1 - e^{-u}$
between $u/(1+u)$ and $u$, and every estimate on either side is one of them. A single value of
$s$ suffices, and $s = 1$ is taken.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Take $s = 1$ in (7.1), so that $\int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-t})k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$. For $t > 0$,
$1 - e^{-t} \ge t/(1+t)$, so the integrand is at least $k(t)/(1+t)$. On $(0,1]$ that is at least
$k(t)/2$, and on $(1,\infty)$ it is at least $k(t)/(2t)$; both claims follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the Gamma family: the transforms and the mean]\label{prop:gamma-kernels}
\uses{prop:gamma-family,thm:main-characterization,prop:moments}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.gammaExponent_laplace_kernel, Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.gammaExponent_laplace_increment, Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.gammaExponent_lintegral_id_kernel}\leanok
With $F$ as in \ref{prop:gamma-family}, the kernels and the increment measures have transforms
\[
  e^{-F(xs)} = (1 + x s)^{-\gamma}, \qquad
  e^{-g_{a,b}(s)} = \Bigl(\tfrac{1 + as}{1 + bs}\Bigr)^{\gamma},
\]
and the mean delay is $\Ex\,T_x = \gamma x$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(split from \ref{prop:gamma-family} because the costs differ and a node
reporting the maximum cost of its clauses misreports its cheap ones; split again on 2026-08-15 for
the same reason, the density and the higher moments leaving as \ref{prop:gamma-density} and
\ref{prop:gamma-moments}. \textbf{Proved in Lean} (2026-08-15),
\texttt{Hemigroup/GammaKernels.lean}, on \ledger{A17} alone --- through the constructed family, as
every statement about $T_x$ and $\mu_{a,b}$ is. All three clauses are one computation from the
exponent $\gamma\log(1+s)$; the mean is \ref{prop:moments} applied to
$\int_0^\infty \gamma e^{-t}dt = \gamma$, and that step is Lean core.
\textbf{Note the contrast with \ref{prop:stable-moments}}, which came \emph{off} \ledger{A7} this

\begin{proposition}[non-creation from the signal; the P\'olya frequency members]\label{prop:gamma-regularity}
\uses{prop:gamma-kernels,thm:main-characterization}
Write $S^-(g)$ for the number of sign changes of a function $g$ on $\RR$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For integer $\gamma$ the Gamma kernels are one-sided P\'olya frequency densities, and
convolution with them is variation-diminishing: $S^-(\mu_{0,x} * g) \le S^-(g)$ for every
$x > 0$ and every integrable or bounded $g$, sign changes counted in the essential
(almost-everywhere) sense. In particular, for the field $u(\cdot, x) := \mu_{0,x} * f$,
\[
  S^-\bigl(u(\cdot, x) - c\bigr) \;\le\; S^-\bigl(f - c\bigr)
  \qquad \text{for every level } c \in \RR,
\]
so the field has, at every scale, no more zero-crossings than the signal; and if $f$ is
the primitive of a function $f' \in \Xz$ --- that is, $f \in \core$ --- then, counting
local extrema as essential sign changes of the derivative,
\[
  S^-\bigl(\partial_t u(\cdot, x)\bigr) \;\le\; S^-\bigl(f'\bigr):
\]
no more local extrema either. For non-integer $\gamma$ the kernels are not P\'olya frequency
densities.
\item An admissible family (\ref{thm:main-characterization}) has P\'olya frequency
kernels if and only if
\[
  F(s) \;=\; b_0\,s \;+\; \sum_{i} \log\Bigl(1 + \frac{s}{\tau_i}\Bigr),
  \qquad \tau_i > 0, \quad \sum_i \frac{1}{\tau_i} < \infty
\]
--- drift together with integer-weighted Thorin atoms, the summability condition being
finiteness of the mean delay, $\Ex\,T_1 = b_0 + \sum_i \tau_i^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\statusA\quad\ledger{A19}\quad\ledger{A20}\quad\emph{(total positivity, cited whole;
\sscite{karlin1968total}. Cited, not reproven.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A19} carries the correspondence between variation diminution
and total positivity for translation kernels --- sufficiency for sign-regular kernels
(Ch.~5, Thm~3.1(i), p.~233, stated for bounded Borel arguments; the extension to
integrable ones is the standard truncation and is taken with the citation) and necessity
for density kernels (Ch.~5, Thm~4.2, pp.~242--243). \ledger{A20} carries Schoenberg's
representation: a one-sided density is P\'olya frequency iff the reciprocal of its Laplace
transform is entire of the form $Ce^{\delta s}\prod_i(1+\lambda_i s)$ with $C > 0$,
$\delta \ge 0$, $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty$ (Ch.~7, Thm~3.2(b), p.~345;
the non-integrable one-sided form, Rem.~3.1, p.~346). Everything else is \textbf{[T]} in
prose: reading $(1+xs)^{\gamma}$ against the form, the level reduction, the derivative
route $\partial_t(\mu * f) = \mu * f'$ on $\core$, clause (2)'s matching of linear factors
to atoms, and the admissibility of the matched exponents through
\ref{thm:main-characterization}.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Two classical facts carry the proof. A density supported on $[0,\infty)$ is a P\'olya
frequency density if and only if the reciprocal of its Laplace transform is an entire
function of the form $C e^{\delta s}\prod_i (1 + \lambda_i s)$ with $C > 0$, $\delta \ge 0$,
$\lambda_i \ge 0$ and $\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty$ --- Schoenberg's representation theorem
(\ledger{A20}; \sscite{karlin1968total}, Ch.~7, Thm~3.2(b), p.~345; the one-sided form,
Remark~3.1, p.~346). And convolution with a kernel is variation-diminishing if and only if
the kernel is totally positive (\ledger{A19}; \sscite{karlin1968total}, Ch.~5, Thm~3.1(i),
p.~233, and Thm~4.2, pp.~242--243), which for a translation kernel is the P\'olya frequency
property.

(1) For the Gamma kernel, $1/\widehat{\mu}_{0,x}(s) = (1 + xs)^{\gamma}$: for integer
$\gamma$ this is a polynomial with the single real negative root $-1/x$, of the displayed
form with $\delta = 0$ and $\gamma$ equal factors, so the kernels are P\'olya frequency
densities and convolution with them diminishes essential sign changes of integrable and of
bounded functions. The level form follows applied to $g = f - c$, since
$\mu_{0,x} * (f - c) = u(\cdot,x) - c$ by unit mass, and the zero-crossing clause is the
level $c = 0$. For the extremum clause, $f \in \core$ gives
$f = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)} * f'$, hence
$u(\cdot,x) = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)} * (\mu_{0,x} * f')$ and
$\partial_t u(\cdot,x) = \mu_{0,x} * f'$ a.e.; the variation-diminishing bound applied to
$g = f'$ gives the display. (The count is read from the derivative and not from the
levels: no single level need meet every extremum --- two oscillations can occupy disjoint
value ranges --- so the level bounds alone do not bound the number of extrema.) For
non-integer $\gamma$, $(1 + xs)^{\gamma}$ has
a branch point at $s = -1/x$ and is not entire, so by the same representation theorem the
kernel is not a P\'olya frequency density.

(2) The kernels are supported on $[0,\infty)$ with transform $e^{-F(xs)}$, so the family is
P\'olya frequency exactly when $e^{F(xs)}$ has the displayed form --- for one $x$ or,
equivalently, for all: replacing $s$ by $xs$ rescales $\delta$ and the $\lambda_i$ by $x$
and preserves the form. Matching at $x = 1$: $F(\zp) = 0$ forces $C = 1$; the exponential
factor is the drift, $\delta = b_0$; and the linear factors are atoms, $\lambda_i = 1/\tau_i$,
giving the display, the condition $\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty$ being
$\sum_i \tau_i^{-1} < \infty$, i.e.\ $F'(\zp) = b_0 + \sum_i \tau_i^{-1} < \infty$.
Every such $F$ is admissible: it is of the form (7.1) with the nonincreasing memory kernel
$k(t) = \sum_i e^{-\tau_i t}$ --- a Frullani integral per atom --- and
$F(1) \le b_0 + \sum_i \tau_i^{-1} < \infty$, so \ref{thm:main-characterization}
applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[scale-monotone variation diminution is impossible]\label{prop:vd-impossibility}
\uses{thm:main-characterization,prop:gamma-regularity,prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}
Let $(\Phi_{x,y})$ be an admissible family (\ref{thm:main-characterization}), in
the canonical gauge, and suppose that \emph{every} interior increment operator
$\Phi_{x,y}$, $0 < x < y$, is variation-diminishing. Then $F(s) = b_0 s$: the family is
the pure delay. In particular no nondegenerate admissible family has the scale-monotone
form of non-creation, in which every coarser record has no more sign changes than every
finer one; the endpoint form of \ref{prop:gamma-regularity} is the strongest
variation-diminishing property an admissible family can carry, and the integer-atom
members of its clause~(2) attain it.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A19}\quad\ledger{A20}\quad\emph{(the same two interfaces as
\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}, in the same roles.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A19} converts the hypothesis --- every increment operator
variation-diminishing --- into the P\'olya frequency property of the smoothed increment
densities (the necessity leg, Thm~4.2), and \ledger{A20} converts that into the entire
product form of $e^{g}(1+\varepsilon s)^{2}$. Everything downstream is \textbf{[T]} in
prose and elementary: the L\'evy-tail comparison through
\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}, the integer-atomic representing measures, the
dilation-orbit replication of an atom, and the contradiction with local finiteness. No
property of the smoothing beyond \ref{prop:gamma-regularity}(1)'s P\'olya frequency clause
is used.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the increments' L\'evy tails are integer sums of exponentials.} Fix
$0 < x < y$ and write $g(s) := F(ys) - F(xs)$, the increment exponent, a Bernstein
function with drift $b_0(y - x)$ and L\'evy tail
$h(t) := k(t/y) - k(t/x) \ge 0$, by substitution in (7.1). For $\varepsilon > 0$ let
$G_\varepsilon$ be the Gamma density of shape $2$ at scale $\varepsilon$, with transform
$(1 + \varepsilon s)^{-2}$ --- a one-sided P\'olya frequency density, by the argument of
\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}(1). The measure
$\nu_\varepsilon := \mu_{x,y} * G_\varepsilon$ is absolutely continuous, and its
convolution operator, the composition of two variation-diminishing operators, is
variation-diminishing; so its density is a one-sided P\'olya frequency density
(\ledger{A19}; \sscite{karlin1968total}, Ch.~5, Thm~4.2, pp.~242--243, as in
\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}), and the Schoenberg representation
(\ledger{A20}; \sscite{karlin1968total}, Ch.~7, Thm~3.2(b), p.~345; Rem.~3.1, p.~346) gives
\[
  1/\widehat{\nu_\varepsilon}(s) \;=\; e^{g(s)}\,(1 + \varepsilon s)^{2}
  \;=\; e^{\delta_\varepsilon s} \prod_i \bigl(1 + \lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i s\bigr),
  \qquad \lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i \ge 0, \quad
  \textstyle\sum_i \lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i < \infty,
\]
the constant being $1$ at $s = 0$. Both sides are exponentials of Bernstein functions;
taking logarithms on $s > 0$ and matching the representations --- drifts from the
$s \to \infty$ limit, then L\'evy tails through their transforms and
\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite} --- gives, for a.e.\ $t > 0$,
\[
  h(t) + 2\,e^{-t/\varepsilon} \;=\; \sum_i e^{-t/\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i}
  \;=\; \int_{(0,\infty)} e^{-\tau t}\, M^{(\varepsilon)}(d\tau),
\]
where $M^{(\varepsilon)} := \sum_i \delta_{1/\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i}$ is a positive
integer-atomic measure, locally finite on $(0,\infty)$ because
$\sum_i \lambda^{(\varepsilon)}_i < \infty$. Comparing two values
$\varepsilon \ne \varepsilon'$ through
\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite} once more shows that
$M := M^{(\varepsilon)} - 2\,\delta_{1/\varepsilon}$ does not depend on $\varepsilon$;
evaluating any single point with a third value of $\varepsilon$ whose exceptional atom
lies elsewhere shows $M \ge 0$ and integer-atomic. Hence
\[
  k(t/y) - k(t/x) \;=\; \int_{(0,\infty)} e^{-\tau t}\, M_{x,y}(d\tau)
  \qquad \text{for a.e.\ } t > 0,
\]
with $M_{x,y}$ positive, locally finite, and integer-atomic.

\emph{Step 2: scaling and additivity.} The tails satisfy
$h_{\sigma x,\, \sigma y}(t) = h_{x,y}(t/\sigma)$ and $h_{x,z} = h_{x,y} + h_{y,z}$ for
$x < y < z$, so by \ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite},
\[
  M_{\sigma x,\, \sigma y} \;=\; \text{the pushforward of } M_{x,y}
  \text{ under } \tau \mapsto \tau/\sigma,
  \qquad
  M_{x,z} \;=\; M_{x,y} + M_{y,z}.
\]

\emph{Step 3: an atom replicates into a continuum.} Suppose some $M_{x,y} \ne 0$; by the
scaling relation we may take $M_{1,y_0} \ne 0$, with an atom at $\tau_0$ of mass
$m_0 \ge 1$. For $\sigma \ge 1$, Step 2 gives $M_{\sigma,\, \sigma y_0}$ an atom at
$\tau_0/\sigma$ of mass $m_0$; and for $Y \ge \sigma y_0$, additivity and positivity give
$M_{1,Y} \ge M_{\sigma,\, \sigma y_0}$. Hence $M_{1,Y}$ carries an atom of mass at least
$1$ at \emph{every} point $\tau_0/\sigma$ with $1 \le \sigma \le Y/y_0$ --- uncountably
many atoms of mass $\ge 1$ in the interval $[\tau_0 y_0/Y,\ \tau_0]$ --- while
\[
  e^{-\tau_0 t}\, M_{1,Y}\bigl([\tau_0 y_0/Y,\ \tau_0]\bigr)
  \;\le\; h_{1,Y}(t) \;\le\; k(t/Y) \;<\; \infty
\]
for any fixed $t > 0$: a contradiction. So $M_{x,y} = 0$ for every pair, i.e.\
$k(t/y) = k(t/x)$ for all $0 < x < y$ and a.e.\ $t$; a nonincreasing function that is
a.e.\ dilation-invariant is constant on $(0,\infty)$, and
$\int_1^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt < \infty$ forces the constant to be $0$. Hence
$F(s) = b_0 s$. Conversely the pure delay's increments are the shifts
$\delta_{b_0(y-x)}$, which are variation-diminishing, so the degenerate case does occur
and the statement is sharp.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[ladder monotonicity survives discrete covariance]\label{rem:ladder-vd}
\uses{prop:vd-impossibility,prop:gamma-regularity}
The impossibility is a continuous-covariance phenomenon, and it is sharp in the ratio:
increments of a \emph{single} ratio can all be variation-diminishing. For an integer-atom
family (\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}(2)) and a fixed $q > 1$, the increments
$\Phi_{x,qx}$, $x > 0$, are variation-diminishing precisely when the atom multiset
satisfies $\operatorname{mult}(q\tau) \ge \operatorname{mult}(\tau)$ for every $\tau$:
then each pole of $e^{g_{x,qx}}$ at $-\tau/x$ cancels against a zero, the leftover
product is of Schoenberg form, and the representation theorem runs backwards to
sufficiency (necessity is Step~1 of the proof above, applied at ratio $q$). The
time-causal limit kernel of \sscite{lindeberg2016time} is the fundamental example: its
Thorin measure is the geometric train $\sum_{i \ge 1}\delta_{q^i/c}$, the condition
holds, and every $q$-step increment exponent is
$F(qxs) - F(xs) = \log(1 + cxs)$ --- each rung one exponential (Gamma shape-$1$) kernel,
a P\'olya frequency density. Along any geometric ladder of ratio $q$ the sign-change
count of the record is therefore monotone from rung to rung: scale-monotone
non-creation, impossible under full covariance, is available in the discrete-covariance
stratum, and the published limit-kernel construction has it.
\end{remark}
