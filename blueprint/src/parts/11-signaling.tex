Theorem~4 of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} inverts the scale-Cauchy problem into an
evolution \emph{in time} over the memory line: $\partial_t u = D^{1/\alpha}_{x,-} u$ with
boundary data $u(t, 0) = f(t)$ and $u(0, x) = 0$ --- the signaling problem. The operator there
is translation-invariant in $x$, which is available only in the homogeneous (semigroup) case.
This chapter constructs the general inversion.

The structural observation that replaces homogeneity is this: writing $H := e^{-F}$, the
Laplace-domain profiles of the field,
\[
  \hat u(s, x) = \hat f(s)\, H(sx),
\]
are \emph{dilates of a single profile} $H$, with the eigenvalue $s$ appearing as the dilation
parameter. Operators diagonal on dilation families are the subject of multiplicative harmonic
analysis, so the inversion operator lives in the \textbf{Mellin calculus} --- the
multiplicative-group counterpart of the Wendel step of Chapter~4 on the additive group.

Throughout we work in the canonical gauge, so that $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $x\,T_1$ with
$\Ex[e^{-sT_1}] = H(s)$; we write $\tilde g(z) := \int_0^\infty x^{z-1} g(x)\,dx$ for the
Mellin transform, $\theta := x\,\partial_x$ for the Euler operator, and
$(I^z f)(t) := \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_0^t (t-r)^{z-1} f(r)\,dr$ for the Riemann--Liouville
integral of complex order $z$, $\operatorname{Re} z > 0$ (\sscite{samko1993fractional}).

\begin{definition}[the standing hypothesis (H)]\label{def:standing-hypothesis}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,thm:main-characterization}
An admissible exponent $F$ satisfies the \emph{standing hypothesis} (H) if
$F(\infty) = \infty$ --- equivalently $b_0 > 0$ or $\int_0^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt = \infty$,
i.e.\ no atom of the kernels at zero delay --- and
\[
  z_* \;:=\; \sup\bigl\{\, \zeta > 0 :\ \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-\zeta}\bigr] < \infty \,\bigr\} \;>\; 1 .
\]
\statusT
\end{definition}

The second condition is equivalent to $\int_0^\infty e^{-F(s)}\,ds < \infty$. It holds with
$z_* = \infty$ whenever $b_0 > 0$, for the stable family, and for the Bessel-K family of
\ref{prop:bessel-family}; for the Gamma family $z_* = \gamma$, so (H) requires $\gamma > 1$
there. The construction extends below $z_* = 1$ by meromorphic continuation, which we do not
pursue.

\begin{lemma}[Mellin data of the profile]\label{lem:mellin-data}
% The edge to prop:moment-criterion went with the narrowing: that node (ledger A7) is the
% criterion for POSITIVE integer moments of T_x, and the statement left here is about
% NEGATIVE moments, which z_* governs and def:standing-hypothesis supplies. The Lean proof

\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,thm:main-characterization}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin_profile}\leanok
Under (H), for $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \tilde H(z) \;=\; \Gamma(z)\;\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr],
\]
with $|\tilde H(c + i\tau)| \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\,|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$ for $0 < c < z_*$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin\_profile} and
\texttt{norm\_mellin\_profile\_le}; \texttt{\#print axioms} gives \ledger{A17} and nothing else,
inherited through \texttt{kernel} because $T_1$ is $\mu_{0,1}$. In particular \emph{no} chapter-11
interface enters --- \ledger{A12} grounds \ref{def:inversion-operator}, not this.}

\emph{\textbf{The exchange of integrals is licensed by the strip condition and by nothing else.}
Both clauses come out of one $[0,\infty]$-valued computation,
$\int\!\!\int s^{c-1}e^{-ts}\,ds\,d\mu(t) = \Gamma(c)\,\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$, which is simultaneously the
Fubini side condition, the bound, and the finiteness underneath \ref{lem:mellin-vertical}. Its
left-hand side is the total mass of $|{\cdot}|$ for the product measure, so joint integrability
holds \emph{iff} the right-hand side is finite, which is $c < z_*$. That is the shape of
\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} again: the condition that makes the analysis go through is
characteristic rather than merely sufficient, and seeing that is what keeps a hypothesis from
being invented.}

\emph{\textbf{The first clause of (H) is load-bearing.} $F(\infty) = \infty$ is used exactly once,
to give $T_1 > 0$ almost surely --- the mass at the origin is below $\Ex[e^{-sT_1}] = e^{-F(s)}$ for
every $s$. Without it the computation above is \emph{false}, not merely unprovable: at an atom
$t = 0$ the inner integral $\int_0^\infty s^{c-1}ds$ diverges, while $\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$, an integral
over $(0,\infty)$, does not see the atom at all.}

\emph{Three design decisions were taken in writing the statement down. $T_1$ is the kernel
$\mu_{0,1}$ the construction already produces, so no new object enters and the density $\phi_1$
is \emph{not} needed --- which matters, because $\phi_1$ would drag in \ledger{A9} for a node
that does not otherwise need it. The profile is the Laplace transform rather than the density, so
Mathlib's \texttt{mellin} applies to it directly. And the negative moments are carried in
$[0,\infty]$: divergence is the whole point of $z_*$, and Chapter~9 is the cautionary tale about
departing from that convention.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
By Tonelli,
\[
  \tilde H(z) = \int_0^\infty s^{z-1}\,\Ex[e^{-sT_1}]\,ds
  = \Ex\Bigl[\int_0^\infty s^{z-1}e^{-sT_1}ds\Bigr]
  = \Ex[T_1^{-z}]\,\Gamma(z),
\]
the inner integral being the Gamma integral at rate $T_1$. The exchange is legitimate because the
same computation run on absolute values gives $\Gamma(c)\,\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$ with
$c = \operatorname{Re} z$, finite exactly when $c < z_*$. Since
$|T_1^{-z}| = T_1^{-\operatorname{Re} z}$, the identity also gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[the inversion operator]\label{def:inversion-operator}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol,def:cascade-family}
\lean{Skeleton.inversionOperator}\notready
Fix $c \in (0, z_* - 1)$. For $g$ such that $\tilde g$ is defined on the line
$\operatorname{Re} z = c$ and $B(-z)\tilde g(z)$ is absolutely integrable there, set
\[
  \bigl(A g\bigr)(x) \;:=\; \frac{1}{x}\cdot \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)} x^{-z}\, B(-z)\,\tilde g(z)\,dz
  \;=\; \Bigl(\frac{1}{x}\,B(\theta)\,g\Bigr)(x),
\]

symbol $-z$. Equivalently, at the transform level,
$\widetilde{A g}(z) = B(1 - z)\,\tilde g(z - 1)$. Note the covariant scaling: $A$ commutes with
dilations up to the weight $x^{-1}$, i.e.\ it scales like $\partial_t$ under
$(t, x) \mapsto (\sigma t, \sigma x)$, as (A8) requires of any inversion.

\begin{lemma}[uniqueness of the symbol]\label{lem:symbol-uniqueness}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol,lem:symbol-rigidity,def:inversion-operator}
If two operators of the form $x^{-1}B_1(\theta)$ and $x^{-1}B_2(\theta)$ both satisfy
$A[H(s\,\cdot)] = s\,H(s\,\cdot)$ for all $s > 0$, then $B_1 = B_2$ on the strip. Hence $A$ is
the unique inversion within the covariant Mellin class.
\statusT\quad\emph{(the rigidity half is \textbf{machine-checked} as
\ref{lem:symbol-rigidity}; what is left here is the reduction of the operator relation to the
transform relation, which is \ref{def:inversion-operator}'s transform-level identity and so
carries \ledger{A12}. That is the honest place for this node to be waiting, and the split is what
makes it visible: the mathematical content --- that the eigenfunction relation \emph{pins} the
symbol rather than merely constraining it --- is the rigidity step, and it is done.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:symbol-rigidity}
Evaluating both operators on the profiles and taking Mellin transforms gives
$B_1(-z)\,s^{-z}\tilde H(z) = B_2(-z)\,s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$ on the line; cancelling the nonzero
factor $s^{-z}$ leaves the hypothesis of \ref{lem:symbol-rigidity}, which concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the memory line stores fractional integrals]\label{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,thm:main-characterization,lem:delay-core}
Under (H), let $f \in \Xz$ be causal and $u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f$. Then for every $t > 0$
and $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\, u(t, x)\,dx
  \;=\; \tilde H(z)\,\bigl(I^{z} f\bigr)(t),
\]
absolutely convergent. If moreover $f \in \core$, the same holds for $\partial_t u$ with
$I^z f$ replaced by $I^z f' = I^{z-1} f$.
\statusT
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the canonical gauge $u(t, x) = \Ex\bigl[f(t - x T_1)\bigr]$. Tonelli applies, since for
$c = \operatorname{Re} z > 1$,
$\Ex[T_1^{-c}]\int_0^t y^{c-1}|f(t-y)|\,dy \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\, t^{c-1}\|f\|_1 < \infty$, and
gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\,\Ex\bigl[f(t - xT_1)\bigr]\,dx
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\int_0^t y^{z-1} f(t - y)\,dy
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\,\Gamma(z)\,(I^z f)(t),
\]
which is the claim by \ref{lem:mellin-data}. For $f \in \core$ we have
$\partial_t u(t, x) = \Ex[f'(t - xT_1)]$, and $I^z f' = I^{z-1} I^1 f' = I^{z-1} f$ using
$f(0) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4$'$: the signaling form]\label{thm:signaling-form}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,lem:mellin-vertical,lem:inversion-symbol,def:inversion-operator,lem:symbol-uniqueness,lem:memory-fractional-integrals,def:cascade-family,thm:main-characterization}
Assume (H) and work in the canonical gauge. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Eigenfunctions.)} For every $s > 0$ the profile $H(s\,\cdot)$ lies in the domain
of \ref{def:inversion-operator} for every $c \in (0, z_*-1)$, and
\[
  A\bigl[H(s\,\cdot)\bigr](x) \;=\; s\, H(sx), \qquad x > 0 .
\]
\item \emph{(The field solves the signaling problem.)} Let $f \in \core$ and
$u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f$. Then $u$ is causal in $t$ with $u(\cdot, x) \to f$ in $\Xz$ as
$x \downarrow 0$, and
\begin{itemize}
\item Laplace form: for every $s > 0$, $A\bigl[\hat u(s, \cdot)\bigr] = s\,\hat u(s, \cdot)$,
with $\hat u(s, \zp) = \hat f(s)$;
\item time-domain (Mellin) form: for every $t > 0$ and $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \widetilde{\partial_t u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; B(1 - z)\;\widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z - 1),
\]
that is, $\partial_t u = A\,u$ read through the Mellin transform in $x$.
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{(Uniqueness.)} $A$ is the unique operator in the covariant Mellin class with
property (1), hence with property (2).
\end{enumerate}
\statusT\quad\emph{(the hemigroup analogue of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~4, whose
translation-invariant operator is recovered in \ref{ex:stable-signaling} as an accident of
homogeneity. Proved here, not cited.)}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) $\widetilde{H(s\,\cdot)}(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$, so
$B(-z)\widetilde{H(s\cdot)}(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z+1)$, which is absolutely integrable on the
line $(c)$ by \ref{lem:mellin-vertical}; no pole of $B$ intervenes, the product containing no
division. Then
\[
  A\bigl[H(s\cdot)\bigr](x)
  = \frac{1}{x}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)} (xs)^{-z}\,\tilde H(z+1)\,dz
  = \frac{1}{x}\,(xs)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c+1)} (xs)^{-w}\,\tilde H(w)\,dw
  = s\,H(sx),
\]
by the substitution $w = z + 1$ and Mellin inversion on the line
$c + 1 \in (1, z_*)$, valid since $\tilde H$ is integrable there and $H$ is continuous.

(2) Causality and the boundary attainment are (A3) and (A7). The Laplace form is (1) applied
to $\hat u(s, \cdot) = \hat f(s) H(s\,\cdot)$. The Mellin form combines
\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals} with \ref{lem:mellin-data}:
\[
  B(1-z)\,\widetilde{u(t,\cdot)}(z-1)
  = \frac{\tilde H(z)}{\tilde H(z-1)}\cdot\tilde H(z-1)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \tilde H(z)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \widetilde{\partial_t u(t,\cdot)}(z).
\]

(3) is \ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[reading of Lemma 11.5; embodiment]\label{rem:embodiment}
\uses{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}
\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals} gives the motivation of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S5, an exact form: at each instant $t$, the Mellin analysis
of the memory line holds precisely the analytic family of Riemann--Liouville integrals
$\{(I^z f)(t)\}$ of the \emph{past} signal, weighted by the negative delay-moments
$\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$. The observer's embodiment of its past is fractional integration, with the
admissible weightings classified by \ref{thm:main-characterization}.
\end{remark}

% draft: Remark 11.8
\begin{remark}[classical interpretation; poles]\label{rem:poles}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol}
The time-domain equation in \ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) is stated at the level of Mellin
transforms, and that is the honest general formulation: $B$ may have poles, at zeros of
$\tilde H$, and polynomial growth along vertical lines, so $A u(t, \cdot)$ need not be given by
an absolutely convergent integral for rough $f$. On the field the identity is nevertheless
regular, the factor $\tilde H(z-1)$ cancelling every denominator.

When the symbol $B$ is a \emph{polynomial}, $A$ is a local differential operator and the
equation is a classical PDE. That is precisely the situation of Theorem~5$'$ in Chapter~12,
and of \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic} below.
\end{remark}

% draft: Example 11.9
\begin{example}[extremal stable; recovering Theorem 4 of 2005]\label{ex:stable-signaling}
\uses{prop:stable-family,lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol,thm:signaling-form}
For $F(s) = s^\alpha$ we have $\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z/\alpha)/\alpha$ and
\[
  B(-z) \;=\; \frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{z+1}{\alpha}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{z}{\alpha}\bigr)}
  \;\sim\; \Bigl(\tfrac{z}{\alpha}\Bigr)^{1/\alpha} \quad (z \to \infty),
\]
a symbol of order $1/\alpha$. On the profiles $e^{-(sx)^\alpha}$ the right-sided Liouville
derivative acts by $D^{1/\alpha}_{x,-} e^{-cx} = c^{1/\alpha} e^{-cx}$ with $c = s^\alpha$,
i.e.\ it also produces the eigenvalue $s$; so the Mellin representative and the operator of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~4, agree on the span of the field profiles. The
translation-invariant representative is a coincidence of homogeneity; the Mellin
representative is the one that survives in general.
\end{example}

% draft: Example 11.10
\begin{example}[the Gamma family; scale-recursive form]\label{ex:gamma-signaling}
\uses{prop:gamma-family,ex:gamma-recursive,def:inversion-operator}
For $F(s) = \gamma \log(1+s)$ with $\gamma > 1$ we have
$\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z)\Gamma(\gamma - z)/\Gamma(\gamma)$ and $B(-z) = z/(\gamma - 1 - z)$,
i.e.
\[
  B(\theta) = -\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
  \qquad A = -\,\frac{1}{x}\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
\]
a \emph{rational} Mellin symbol --- the memory-line mirror of the time-side resolvent
$\gamma\,\partial_t(1 + x\partial_t)^{-1}$ of \ref{ex:gamma-recursive}. Explicitly,
$w := (\theta + \gamma - 1)^{-1} u$ solves $x\,w_x + (\gamma-1)w = u$, with the regular-at-$0$
solution
\[
  w(t, x) = x^{-(\gamma-1)}\int_0^x y^{\gamma - 2}\, u(t, y)\,dy,
\]
so the time evolution at scale $x$ is computed from the field at \emph{finer} scales by a
single weighted average --- scale-recursive, one-sided from the boundary, matching the causal
architecture of the time side.
\end{example}

% draft: Example 11.11
\begin{example}[parabolic gauge; the Bessel family is the quadratic case]\label{ex:bessel-quadratic}
\uses{prop:bessel-family,rem:gauge-freedom,def:inversion-operator,rem:poles}
In the $\alpha$-gauge (similarity variable $x^{1/\alpha} s$; \ref{rem:gauge-freedom}) the
profiles are $H(x^{1/\alpha}s)$, the Euler operator acts on the profile through
$\alpha^{-1}\theta$, and the inversion becomes $A = x^{-1/\alpha}\,B(\alpha\,\theta)$. Take the
parabolic gauge $\alpha = \tfrac12$ and the Bessel-K family of \ref{prop:bessel-family},
$T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$: then $\Ex[T_1^{-z}] = 2^z\,\Gamma(a + z)/\Gamma(a)$, so
\[
  B(-z) = 2z(z + a), \qquad
  A = x^{-2}\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta^2}{2} - a\theta\Bigr)
  = \tfrac12\,\partial_x^2 + \frac{\beta}{x}\,\partial_x, \quad \beta = \tfrac12 - a .
\]
The symbol is a \emph{quadratic polynomial}, so $A$ is local: the signaling form of the
Bessel-K scale space is exactly the Bessel diffusion-with-drift medium, and
\ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) becomes the classical signaling problem
$\partial_t u = \tfrac12 u_{xx} + \tfrac{\beta}{x}u_x$ with $u(t,0) = f(t)$ and $u(0,x) = 0$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Markov media]\label{rem:markov-media}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,ex:stable-signaling,ex:gamma-signaling}
Within the class of \ref{thm:signaling-form}, the operators $A$ that additionally satisfy the
positive maximum principle --- i.e.\ generate a sub-Markov process on the memory half-line, so
that the medium is a physical transport medium --- are, after the logarithmic substitution
$x = e^\xi$, exactly those whose symbol $B$ is the Laplace exponent of a Lévy process. By the
Lamperti correspondence (\sscite{lamperti1972semi}) these media are positive self-similar
Markov processes absorbed at the boundary, and the kernels are the boundary-arrival times
$T_x \stackrel{d}{=}$ absorption time from depth $x$.

The spectral orientation of the jumps is \emph{not} constrained. For spectrally positive media
the process creeps downward through every level and the hemigroup (A6) holds \emph{pathwise},
the kernels being genuine level-passage times; but media with downward jumps are also
admissible, the factorization then holding in law though not pathwise, being inherited from
the exponent $F$ rather than from the paths.

The Gamma family is the standard example: unfolding \ref{ex:gamma-signaling}, its medium is
the multiplicative jump process that waits at scale $x$ an $\mathrm{Exp}(\text{mean } x)$ time
and jumps to $\sigma x$ with $\sigma \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\gamma-1, 1)$, reaching the boundary by
accumulation of jumps; a first-jump computation confirms
$\Ex_x[e^{-s\tau_0}] = (1+sx)^{-\gamma}$.

A necessary condition in all cases is quadratic symbol growth, $B(\theta) = O(\theta^2)$; by
\ref{ex:stable-signaling} the homogeneous members with $\alpha < \tfrac12$, of symbol order
$1/\alpha > 2$, admit \emph{no} Markov medium, although \ref{thm:signaling-form} always
supplies the analytic signaling form --- the operator exists, the process does not. The
classification of which admissible $F$ are Markov-realizable is the memory-line inverse
problem, and is taken up with Theorem~5$'$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[the profile's transform is integrable on vertical lines]\label{lem:mellin-vertical}
\uses{lem:mellin-data}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.verticalIntegrable_mellin_profile}\leanok
Under (H), for every $c \in (0, z_*)$ the function $\tau \mapsto \tilde H(c + i\tau)$ is
absolutely integrable on $\RR$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.verticalIntegrable\_mellin\_profile};
\texttt{\#print axioms} gives \ledger{A17} and nothing else. The Lean statement is
\texttt{Complex.VerticalIntegrable}, which is \emph{verbatim} the hypothesis Mathlib's
\texttt{mellinInv\_mellin\_eq} asks for, so this node is the bridge between the chapter and
the inversion theorem it cites.}

\emph{\textbf{It was recorded twice as blocked on Mathlib, and was not.} The bound of
\ref{lem:mellin-data} reduces this to the decay of $|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$, and Mathlib has no such
estimate --- its Stirling file is Stirling's formula for $n!$ alone. That is true about Mathlib
and false about the obligation. The classical asymptotic
$|\Gamma(c+i\tau)| \sim \sqrt{2\pi}\,|\tau|^{c-1/2}e^{-\pi|\tau|/2}$ does need Stirling in the
complex plane; \emph{integrability needs only quadratic decay}, and quadratic decay is the
functional equation twice. That is the proof below, and it is elementary.}

\emph{\textbf{So chapter 11 is no longer blocked upstream.} What \ref{def:inversion-operator}
still needs from Mathlib's inversion theorem is the \emph{operator} formulation --- exhibiting
the function whose Mellin transform is $B(-z)\tilde g(z)$ --- and a continuity hypothesis. Both
are questions about this article, which is where \ledger{A12}'s correction said the cost would
sit once the estimate was in hand. \texttt{blueprint/AXIOMS.md} now records four assessments of
that entry and what each of the first three got wrong.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$|\tilde H(c+i\tau)| \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\,|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$ by \ref{lem:mellin-data}, the first
factor being finite because $c < z_*$. For the second, two elementary bounds. Euler's integral
gives $|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)| \le \Gamma(\sigma)$ for $\sigma > 0$, the imaginary part only rotating
the integrand; and $\Gamma(z+2) = (z+1)z\Gamma(z)$ with $|z|, |z+1| \ge |\tau|$ --- both have
imaginary part $\tau$ --- gives $|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|\,\tau^2 \le \Gamma(c+2)$. Adding them,
\[
  |\Gamma(c+i\tau)|\,(1+\tau^2) \;\le\; \Gamma(c) + \Gamma(c+2),
\]
and $(1+\tau^2)^{-1}$ is integrable on the line.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the inversion symbol]\label{lem:inversion-symbol}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,def:standing-hypothesis}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.meromorphicOn_inversionSymbol}\leanok
Under (H), $\tilde H$ is analytic on the strip $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$ and does not
vanish identically there. Define the \emph{inversion symbol}
\[
  B(-z) \;:=\; \frac{\tilde H(z+1)}{\tilde H(z)}
  \;=\; z\,\frac{\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z-1}\bigr]}{\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]},
  \qquad 0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_* - 1 .
\]
Then $B$ is meromorphic on that strip, with poles only at the isolated zeros of $\tilde H$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.meromorphicOn\_inversionSymbol} together with
\texttt{analyticAt\_mellin\_profile} (analyticity),
\texttt{mellin\_profile\_ofReal\_ne\_zero} ($\tilde H \not\equiv 0$),
\texttt{eventually\_mellin\_profile\_ne\_zero} (the zeros are isolated),
\texttt{inversionSymbol\_eq} (the closed form) and
\texttt{analyticAt\_inversionSymbol} (poles only at those zeros);
\texttt{\#print axioms} gives \ledger{A17} and nothing else. This is the clause every later
node in the chapter consumes: \ref{def:inversion-operator} needs $B$ and its strip,
\ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness} needs the isolated zeros, \ref{rem:poles} needs the poles.}

\emph{\textbf{The analyticity came from the identity, not from the Mellin integral.} The direct
route is Mathlib's \texttt{mellin\_differentiableAt\_of\_isBigO\_rpow}, which wants
$H(s) = O(s^{-a})$ for every $a < z_*$ --- true, since $H$ is antitone and
$H(s)s^a/a \le \int_0^s u^{a-1}H(u)du$, but work, and it re-derives from the Mellin integral what
\ref{lem:mellin-data} already says. Reading $m(z) = \Ex[T_1^{-z}] = \Ex[e^{-z\log T_1}]$ as the
complex moment-generating function of $-\log T_1$ instead makes it Mathlib's
\texttt{analyticAt\_complexMGF}, whose hypothesis is finiteness of $\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$ on an open
interval of exponents --- which is $(0, z_*)$, and is \ref{lem:mellin-data}'s own strip condition
verbatim. So the strip of analyticity and the strip of the identity are the \emph{same} strip for
the \emph{same} reason, rather than two conditions that coincide.}

\emph{\textbf{The closed form needs no non-vanishing hypothesis.} $\Gamma(z+1) = z\Gamma(z)$ is the
whole of it, and that cancellation is also why $B$ alone grows along vertical lines where
$\tilde H$ decays --- the point \ledger{A12} turns on. Lean's $x/0 = 0$ makes both sides of the
closed form vanish together at a zero of $\tilde H$, so the statement is unconditional and no
reader is misled into thinking a side condition was checked.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:mellin-data}
$\tilde H$ is analytic on the strip by differentiation under the integral sign, the majorant
$s^{c-1}H(s)$ being locally uniformly integrable there. It is not identically zero: by
\ref{lem:mellin-data}, $\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z)\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$, and both factors are strictly
positive at real $z \in (0, z_*)$. Hence its zeros are isolated, and the quotient
$\tilde H(z+1)/\tilde H(z)$ --- which is $z\,\Ex[T_1^{-z-1}]/\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$ by
$\Gamma(z+1) = z\Gamma(z)$ --- is meromorphic with poles only there.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[rigidity of the symbol]\label{lem:symbol-rigidity}
\uses{lem:inversion-symbol}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.SameSymbolAction.eventuallyEq}\leanok
Assume (H), and let $B_1, B_2$ satisfy
\[
  B_1(-z)\,\tilde H(z) \;=\; B_2(-z)\,\tilde H(z),
  \qquad 0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_* - 1 .
\]
Then $B_1 = B_2$ as meromorphic functions on that strip: they agree on a punctured neighbourhood
of every point of it. Where in addition both are continuous --- in particular at any point that
is a pole of neither --- they agree there too.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.SameSymbolAction.eventuallyEq}, with
\texttt{eqOn\_of\_ne\_zero} (agreement off the zeros of $\tilde H$), \texttt{eq\_of\_continuousAt}
and \texttt{eqOn} (the pointwise readings) alongside it; \texttt{\#print axioms} gives
\ledger{A17} and nothing else. Split from \ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness}, which keeps the reduction
of the operator relation to the one above --- that step is \ref{def:inversion-operator}'s
transform-level identity and carries \ledger{A12}. This is the half that does the mathematical
work: the cancellation is what makes the eigenfunction relation \emph{pin} the symbol rather than
merely constrain it.}

\emph{\textbf{``$B_1 = B_2$ on the strip'' has to say which equality it means, and writing it in
Lean is what forced the question.} The symbols are meromorphic, with poles at the zeros of
$\tilde H$; at a pole a Lean function still has a value, a junk one, so pointwise equality of
functions is the wrong reading and would be false as stated. The unconditional statement is
agreement on a punctured neighbourhood of every point --- which is exactly what equality of
meromorphic functions means, and exactly what the draft's ``everywhere on the strip by
meromorphy'' is asserting. Pointwise equality is then recovered under a continuity hypothesis
that says, in so many words, that the symbols have no poles. Both forms are proved, and a
consumer has to choose; that choice is information the prose was eliding.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:inversion-symbol}
Where $\tilde H(z) \ne 0$, cancel it. By \ref{lem:inversion-symbol} the zeros of $\tilde H$ are
isolated, so that cancellation is available on a punctured neighbourhood of \emph{every} point of
the strip, the zeros included; hence $B_1$ and $B_2$ agree near every point, the point itself
excepted. If both are continuous at such a point, the punctured neighbourhood determines the
value there and the agreement extends to it.
\end{proof}
