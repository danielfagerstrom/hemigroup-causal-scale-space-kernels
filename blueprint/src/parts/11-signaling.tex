Theorem~4 of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} inverts the scale-Cauchy problem into an
evolution \emph{in time} over the memory line: $\partial_t u = D^{1/\alpha}_{x,-} u$ with
boundary data $u(t, 0) = f(t)$ and $u(0, x) = 0$ --- the signaling problem. The operator there
is translation-invariant in $x$, which is available only in the homogeneous (semigroup) case.
This chapter constructs the general inversion.

The structural observation that replaces homogeneity is this: writing $H := e^{-F}$, the
Laplace-domain profiles of the field,
\[
  \hat u(s, x) = \hat f(s)\, H(sx),
\]
are \emph{dilates of a single profile} $H$, with the eigenvalue $s$ appearing as the dilation
parameter. Operators diagonal on dilation families are the subject of multiplicative harmonic
analysis, so the inversion operator lives in the \textbf{Mellin calculus} --- the
multiplicative-group counterpart of the Wendel step of Chapter~4 on the additive group.

Throughout we work in the canonical gauge, so that $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $x\,T_1$ with
$\Ex[e^{-sT_1}] = H(s)$; we write $\tilde g(z) := \int_0^\infty x^{z-1} g(x)\,dx$ for the
Mellin transform, $\theta := x\,\partial_x$ for the Euler operator, and
$(I^z f)(t) := \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_0^t (t-r)^{z-1} f(r)\,dr$ for the Riemann--Liouville
integral of complex order $z$, $\operatorname{Re} z > 0$ (\sscite{samko1993fractional}).

\begin{lemma}[Mellin data of the profile]\label{lem:mellin-data}
% The edge to prop:moment-criterion went with the narrowing: that node (ledger A7) is the
% criterion for POSITIVE integer moments of T_x, and the statement left here is about
% NEGATIVE moments, which z_* governs and def:standing-hypothesis supplies. The Lean proof
% is the check: #print axioms on mellin_profile gives A17 and nothing else, so no path
% through A7 is used.
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,thm:main-characterization}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin_profile}\leanok
Under (H), for $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \tilde H(z) \;=\; \Gamma(z)\;\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr],
\]
with $|\tilde H(c + i\tau)| \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\,|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$ for $0 < c < z_*$.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin\_profile} and
\texttt{norm\_mellin\_profile\_le}; \texttt{\#print axioms} gives \ledger{A17} and nothing else,
inherited through \texttt{kernel} because $T_1$ is $\mu_{0,1}$. In particular \emph{no} chapter-11
interface enters --- \ledger{A12} grounds \ref{def:inversion-operator}, not this.}

\emph{\textbf{The exchange of integrals is licensed by the strip condition and by nothing else.}
Both clauses come out of one $[0,\infty]$-valued computation,
$\int\!\!\int s^{c-1}e^{-ts}\,ds\,d\mu(t) = \Gamma(c)\,\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$, which is simultaneously the
Fubini side condition, the bound, and the finiteness underneath \ref{lem:mellin-vertical}. Its
left-hand side is the total mass of $|{\cdot}|$ for the product measure, so joint integrability
holds \emph{iff} the right-hand side is finite, which is $c < z_*$. That is the shape of
\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} again: the condition that makes the analysis go through is
characteristic rather than merely sufficient, and seeing that is what keeps a hypothesis from
being invented.}

\emph{\textbf{The first clause of (H) is load-bearing.} $F(\infty) = \infty$ is used exactly once,
to give $T_1 > 0$ almost surely --- the mass at the origin is below $\Ex[e^{-sT_1}] = e^{-F(s)}$ for
every $s$. Without it the computation above is \emph{false}, not merely unprovable: at an atom
$t = 0$ the inner integral $\int_0^\infty s^{c-1}ds$ diverges, while $\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$, an integral
over $(0,\infty)$, does not see the atom at all.}

\emph{Three design decisions were taken in writing the statement down. $T_1$ is the kernel
$\mu_{0,1}$ the construction already produces, so no new object enters and the density $\phi_1$
is \emph{not} needed --- which matters, because $\phi_1$ would drag in \ledger{A9} for a node
that does not otherwise need it. The profile is the Laplace transform rather than the density, so
Mathlib's \texttt{mellin} applies to it directly. And the negative moments are carried in
$[0,\infty]$: divergence is the whole point of $z_*$, and Chapter~9 is the cautionary tale about
departing from that convention.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
By Tonelli,
\[
  \tilde H(z) = \int_0^\infty s^{z-1}\,\Ex[e^{-sT_1}]\,ds
  = \Ex\Bigl[\int_0^\infty s^{z-1}e^{-sT_1}ds\Bigr]
  = \Ex[T_1^{-z}]\,\Gamma(z),
\]
the inner integral being the Gamma integral at rate $T_1$. The exchange is legitimate because the
same computation run on absolute values gives $\Gamma(c)\,\Ex[T_1^{-c}]$ with
$c = \operatorname{Re} z$, finite exactly when $c < z_*$. Since
$|T_1^{-z}| = T_1^{-\operatorname{Re} z}$, the identity also gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[the inversion operator]\label{def:inversion-operator}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol,def:cascade-family}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.inversionOperator}\leanok

\[

\]
the second expression being the functional-calculus reading, the Euler operator having Mellin
symbol $-z$.

The second equality is an assertion and not a rewriting, and it is worth being explicit about
what it asserts: $B(\theta)$ is a functional calculus for a symbol with poles, so what the
display says is that there \emph{exists} a function $h$ on $(0,\infty)$ whose Mellin transform is
$B(-z)\tilde g(z)$ on the line, and that the contour integral computes $h(x)/x$. Absolute
integrability on the line is exactly the hypothesis under which the inversion integral recovers
such an $h$. Given $h$, the rest is elementary and is \ref{lem:inversion-operator-action}; in
particular the transform-level form is $\widetilde{A g}(z) = B(1 - z)\,\tilde g(z - 1)$, wherever
the identity defining $h$ holds pointwise. Every application below \emph{exhibits} its $h$ rather
than inferring it --- for the profiles $g = H(s\,\cdot)$ it is $h(x) = s\,x\,H(sx)$,
\ref{lem:profile-eigenfunction} --- so the cited inversion theorem is available but never spent.

\begin{definition}[the covariant Mellin class]\label{def:covariant-mellin-class}
\uses{def:inversion-operator,lem:inversion-symbol}

\begin{lemma}[uniqueness of the symbol]\label{lem:symbol-uniqueness}

\emph{\textbf{Two things the proof does not need.} It needs no injectivity of the inverse Mellin
transform --- the obvious tool, and a second citation (\sscite{widder1941laplace}, Thm.~6a, listed

\emph{same} realising function there, since $x^{-1}h_1 = x^{-1}h_2$ on $(0,\infty)$ is $h_1 = h_2$;
so their transforms agree at every point and the symbols are read off directly. And it needs only

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:symbol-rigidity,lem:profile-eigenfunction}
Fix a height $c \in (0, z_* - 1)$. Both operators send $H(s\,\cdot)$ to $s\,H(s\,\cdot)$, so on
$(0,\infty)$ their realising functions at height $c$ agree; call the common value $h$. Then
$B_1(-z)\,\tilde g(z) = \tilde h(z) = B_2(-z)\,\tilde g(z)$ at every point of the line
$\operatorname{Re} z = c$ where the identity holds, and $\tilde g(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$, so
cancelling the nonvanishing factor $s^{-z}$ gives $B_1(-z)\tilde H(z) = B_2(-z)\tilde H(z)$ at
every point of the line where $\tilde H(z) \ne 0$, and trivially at its zeros, where both sides
vanish. As $c$ ranges over $(0, z_* - 1)$ the lines fill the strip, which is the hypothesis of
\ref{lem:symbol-rigidity}, and that lemma concludes.
\end{proof}

and $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\, u(t, x)\,dx
  \;=\; \tilde H(z)\,\bigl(I^{z} f\bigr)(t),
\]
absolutely convergent. If moreover $f \in \core$, the same holds for $\partial_t u$ --- the
$\Xz$-derivative of \ref{lem:delay-core}, i.e.\ $u(\cdot, x)$ is the primitive of
$\Ex[f'(\cdot - xT_1)]$ --- with
$I^z f$ replaced by $I^z f' = I^{z-1} f$; and in that case the identity extends to the wider strip
$0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$.
\statusT
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
In the canonical gauge $u(t, x) = \Ex\bigl[f(t - x T_1)\bigr]$. Tonelli applies, since for
$c = \operatorname{Re} z > 1$,
$\Ex[T_1^{-c}]\int_0^t y^{c-1}|f(t-y)|\,dy \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\, t^{c-1}\|f\|_1 < \infty$, and
gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\,\Ex\bigl[f(t - xT_1)\bigr]\,dx
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\int_0^t y^{z-1} f(t - y)\,dy
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\,\Gamma(z)\,(I^z f)(t),
\]
which is the claim by \ref{lem:mellin-data}. (For bounded $f$ the inner integral converges for
every $c > 0$, which is the wider strip.) For $f \in \core$, $f$ is bounded --- $f(0) = 0$ and
$f' \in \Xz$ give $\|f\|_\infty \le \|f'\|_1$ --- so the weight $x^{z-1}$ is then integrable at
the origin on its own, which is where the extension to $c > 0$ is used. Also for $f \in \core$:
$u(\cdot,x) = \mu_{0,x} * f$ and
$f = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)} * f'$, so $u(\cdot,x) = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)} * (\mu_{0,x} * f')$ ---
that is, $u(\cdot,x)$ is the primitive of $\Ex[f'(\cdot - xT_1)]$, which is what
$\partial_t u = \Ex[f'(\cdot - xT_1)]$ asserts in $\Xz$. Finally $I^zf' = I^{z-1}f$: substituting
$f(r) = \int_0^r f'$ and exchanging the order of integration over $0 < \rho \le r \le t$ leaves
$\int_\rho^t (t-r)^{z-2}dr = (t-\rho)^{z-1}/(z-1)$, and $\Gamma(z) = (z-1)\Gamma(z-1)$; the
semigroup identity $I^{z-1}I^1 = I^z$ is not needed (\ref{lem:fractional-integral-derivative}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the core supplies]\label{rem:core-accounting}
\uses{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}
Of the three conditions defining $\core$ the proofs use exactly three consequences and no
others: boundedness, which is what widens the strip; that $f$ is the primitive of $f'$, which is
what the derivative clause asserts; and $f \in \Xz$ itself, needed for the Laplace form of
\ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) and \emph{not} implied by the other two, a primitive of an $L^1$
function lying in $L^1$ only when $\int_0^\infty f' = 0$. Density and invariance under $\shift_r$
and $\Phi_{x,y}$ --- the substance of \ref{lem:delay-core} --- are not used here.
\end{remark}

\emph{\textbf{The first clause is machine-checked}, as \ref{lem:delayed-average-mellin}, which
also carries the identification of $\Ex[f(t-xT_1)]$ with $\Phi_{0,x}f$ and so the canonical gauge
at the level of measures. See that node's remark for why a representative of the field has to be
named at all --- the quantifiers ``for each $x$, a.e.\ $t$'' do not commute, so this is not avoided
by weakening the reading.}

\emph{\textbf{The derivative clause is machine-checked}, as

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4$'$: the signaling form]\label{thm:signaling-form}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,lem:mellin-vertical,lem:inversion-symbol,def:inversion-operator,def:covariant-mellin-class,lem:profile-eigenfunction,lem:symbol-uniqueness,lem:memory-fractional-integrals,def:cascade-family,thm:main-characterization}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.signaling_form}\leanok
Assume (H) and work in the canonical gauge. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Eigenfunctions.)} For every $s > 0$ the profile $H(s\,\cdot)$ lies in the domain
of \ref{def:inversion-operator} for every $c \in (0, z_*-1)$, and
\[
  A\bigl[H(s\,\cdot)\bigr](x) \;=\; s\, H(sx), \qquad x > 0 .
\]
\item \emph{(The field solves the signaling problem.)} Let $f \in \core$ and
$u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f$. Then $u$ is causal in $t$ with $u(\cdot, x) \to f$ in $\Xz$ as
$x \downarrow 0$, and
\begin{itemize}
\item Laplace form: for every $s > 0$, $A\bigl[\hat u(s, \cdot)\bigr] = s\,\hat u(s, \cdot)$,
with $\hat u(s, \zp) = \hat f(s)$;
\item time-domain (Mellin) form: for every $t > 0$ and every $z$ with
$1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$ and $\tilde H(z-1) \ne 0$ --- all of the strip off an isolated
set, \ref{rem:poles} ---
\[
  \widetilde{\partial_t u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; B(1 - z)\;\widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z - 1),
\]
both transforms absolutely convergent; that is, $\partial_t u = A\,u$ read through the Mellin
transform in $x$.
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{(Uniqueness.)} $A$ is the unique operator in the covariant Mellin class
(\ref{def:covariant-mellin-class}) with property (1), at every height $c$; in particular, it is
the operator of the signaling problem (2).
\end{enumerate}
\statusT\quad\emph{(the hemigroup analogue of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~4, whose
translation-invariant operator is recovered in \ref{ex:stable-signaling} as an accident of
homogeneity. Proved here, not cited.}

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(1) The realising function is exhibited: put $h(x) := s\,x\,H(sx)$, so that
$\tilde h(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z+1)$, the weight $x$ shifting the Mellin argument by one and the two
powers of $s$ combining as $s\cdot s^{-(z+1)}$. On the other side
$\tilde g(z) := \widetilde{H(s\,\cdot)}(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$, so
\[
  B(-z)\,\tilde g(z)
  = \frac{\tilde H(z+1)}{\tilde H(z)}\cdot s^{-z}\tilde H(z)
  = s^{-z}\tilde H(z+1)
  = \tilde h(z)
\]
at every point of the line where $\tilde H(z) \ne 0$, which is all but an isolated --- hence null
--- set of it. The cancellation is the whole content, being \ref{lem:inversion-symbol}'s recursion
with the denominator cleared; it should \emph{not} be described as a product containing no
division, since the division is there and clearing it is what costs the exceptional set. Both
convergence hypotheses of \ref{def:inversion-operator} hold for $h$ at height $c$, being
\ref{lem:mellin-data} and \ref{lem:mellin-vertical} at height $c + 1 \in (1, z_*)$, and $h$ is
continuous because $H$ is. So $h$ realises $B(\theta)H(s\,\cdot)$ and
\ref{lem:inversion-operator-action} gives $A[H(s\cdot)](x) = h(x)/x = s\,H(sx)$: the weight
$x^{-1}$ of \ref{def:inversion-operator} is exactly what cancels the weight $x$ that shifted the
transform, so the exhibition of $h$ and the eigenfunction relation are one statement rather than
two. This clause is \ref{lem:profile-eigenfunction}.

(2) Causality and the boundary attainment are (A3) and (A7), and $u(\cdot,x) = \Phi_{0,x}f$ is
the convolution representation of \ref{thm:main-characterization} read in the canonical gauge,
$\mu_{0,x}$ being the law of $xT_1$. The Laplace form is (1) applied to
$\hat u(s, \cdot) = \hat f(s) H(s\,\cdot)$, and the boundary value $\hat u(s,\zp) = \hat f(s)$ is
$H(sx) \to H(0) = 1$, the transform of a probability measure being continuous on $[0,\infty)$.
The $\Xz$-reading of $\partial_t u$ is the derivative clause of
\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}: $u(\cdot,x)$ is the primitive of $\Ex[f'(\cdot - xT_1)]$.
The Mellin form combines \ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals} with \ref{lem:mellin-data}, both
transforms converging absolutely on the stated strip by that lemma:
\[
  B(1-z)\,\widetilde{u(t,\cdot)}(z-1)
  = \frac{\tilde H(z)}{\tilde H(z-1)}\cdot\tilde H(z-1)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \tilde H(z)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \widetilde{\partial_t u(t,\cdot)}(z).
\]

(3) is \ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[classical interpretation; poles]\label{rem:poles}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,lem:mellin-data,lem:inversion-symbol}
The time-domain equation in \ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) is stated at the level of Mellin
transforms, and that is the honest general formulation: $B$ may have poles, at zeros of
$\tilde H$, and polynomial growth along vertical lines, so $A u(t, \cdot)$ need not be given by
an absolutely convergent integral for rough $f$. On the field the identity is nevertheless
regular, the factor $\tilde H(z-1)$ cancelling every denominator. That last statement is about the
product as a \emph{meromorphic function}; read pointwise it needs $\tilde H(z-1) \ne 0$, which
holds off an isolated set by \ref{lem:inversion-symbol}, and \ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) is stated
accordingly. The distinction recurs through this chapter --- it is the same one that makes
\ref{lem:symbol-rigidity}'s conclusion an equality of meromorphic functions rather than of
functions --- and is worth keeping in view: a cancellation performed on symbols has to be
justified at each point where the identity is used.

When the symbol $B$ is a \emph{polynomial}, $A$ is a local differential operator and the
equation is a classical PDE. That is precisely the situation of Theorem~5$'$ in Chapter~12,
and of \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic} below.
\end{remark}

representative is the one that survives in general.

\begin{example}[the Gamma family; scale-recursive form]\label{ex:gamma-signaling}
\uses{prop:gamma-family,ex:gamma-recursive,def:inversion-operator}
For $F(s) = \gamma \log(1+s)$ with $\gamma > 1$ we have
$\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z)\Gamma(\gamma - z)/\Gamma(\gamma)$ and $B(-z) = z/(\gamma - 1 - z)$,
i.e.
\[
  B(\theta) = -\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
  \qquad A = -\,\frac{1}{x}\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
\]
a \emph{rational} Mellin symbol --- the memory-line mirror of the time-side resolvent
$\gamma\,\partial_t(1 + x\partial_t)^{-1}$ of \ref{ex:gamma-recursive}. Explicitly,
$w := (\theta + \gamma - 1)^{-1} u$ solves $x\,w_x + (\gamma-1)w = u$, with the regular-at-$0$
solution
\[
  w(t, x) = x^{-(\gamma-1)}\int_0^x y^{\gamma - 2}\, u(t, y)\,dy,
  \qquad
  \partial_t u \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{x}\,\theta\, w \;=\; -\,\partial_x w
  \quad\text{(one scale-Volterra step)},
\]
so the time evolution at scale $x$ is computed from the field at \emph{finer} scales by a
single weighted average --- scale-recursive, one-sided from the boundary, matching the causal
architecture of the time side.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[parabolic gauge; the Bessel family is the quadratic case]\label{ex:bessel-quadratic}

\begin{remark}[Markov media]\label{rem:markov-media}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,ex:stable-signaling,ex:gamma-signaling}
Within the class of \ref{thm:signaling-form}, the operators $A$ that additionally satisfy the
positive maximum principle (\ref{def:locality-pmp}) --- i.e.\ generate a sub-Markov process on the
memory half-line, so that the medium is a physical transport medium --- are --- we record this
without proof here --- after the logarithmic substitution $x = e^\xi$, exactly those whose symbol
$B$ is the Laplace exponent of a Lévy process. By the
Lamperti correspondence (\sscite{lamperti1972semi}) these media are positive self-similar
Markov processes absorbed at the boundary, and the kernels are the boundary-arrival times
$T_x \stackrel{d}{=}$ absorption time from depth $x$.

The spectral orientation of the jumps is \emph{not} constrained. For spectrally positive media
the process creeps downward through every level and the hemigroup (A6) holds \emph{pathwise},
the kernels being genuine level-passage times; but media with downward jumps are also
admissible, the factorization then holding in law though not pathwise, being inherited from
the exponent $F$ rather than from the paths.

The Gamma family is the standard example: unfolding \ref{ex:gamma-signaling}, its medium is
the multiplicative jump process that waits at scale $x$ an $\mathrm{Exp}(\text{mean } x)$ time
and jumps to $\sigma x$ with $\sigma \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\gamma-1, 1)$, reaching the boundary by
accumulation of jumps; a first-jump computation confirms
$\Ex_x[e^{-s\tau_0}] = (1+sx)^{-\gamma}$.

A necessary condition in all cases --- again recorded without proof --- is quadratic symbol
growth, $B(\theta) = O(\theta^2)$; by \ref{ex:stable-signaling} the homogeneous members with
$\alpha < \tfrac12$, of symbol order $1/\alpha > 2$, admit \emph{no} Markov medium, although
\ref{thm:signaling-form} always supplies the analytic signaling form --- the operator exists, the
process does not. The locality theorem settles the local sub-case --- a polynomial symbol of
degree at most two --- but the general classification of which admissible $F$ are
Markov-realizable is the memory-line inverse problem, and it is left open; it is listed with the
open problems in the concluding chapter.
\end{remark}

gives $|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)| \le \Gamma(\sigma)$ for $\sigma > 0$, the imaginary part only rotating
the integrand; and $\Gamma(z+2) = (z+1)z\Gamma(z)$ with $|z|, |z+1| \ge |\tau|$ --- both have
imaginary part $\tau$ --- gives $|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|\,\tau^2 \le \Gamma(c+2)$. Adding them,
\[
  |\Gamma(c+i\tau)|\,(1+\tau^2) \;\le\; \Gamma(c) + \Gamma(c+2),
\]
and $(1+\tau^2)^{-1}$ is integrable on the line.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the inversion symbol]\label{lem:inversion-symbol}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,def:standing-hypothesis}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.meromorphicOn_inversionSymbol}\leanok
Under (H), $\tilde H$ is analytic on the strip $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$ and does not
vanish identically there. Define the \emph{inversion symbol}

  B(-z) \;:=\; \frac{\tilde H(z+1)}{\tilde H(z)}

  \qquad 0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_* - 1 .
\]
Then $B$ is meromorphic on that strip, with poles only at the isolated zeros of $\tilde H$.

\emph{\textbf{The analyticity came from the identity, not from the Mellin integral.} The direct
route is Mathlib's \texttt{mellin\_differentiableAt\_of\_isBigO\_rpow}, which wants
$H(s) = O(s^{-a})$ for every $a < z_*$ --- true, since $H$ is antitone and
$H(s)s^a/a \le \int_0^s u^{a-1}H(u)du$, but work, and it re-derives from the Mellin integral what

verbatim. So the strip of analyticity and the strip of the identity are the \emph{same} strip for
the \emph{same} reason, rather than two conditions that coincide.}

whole of it, and that cancellation is also why $B$ alone grows along vertical lines where
$\tilde H$ decays --- the point \ledger{A12} turns on. Lean's $x/0 = 0$ makes both sides of the

reader is misled into thinking a side condition was checked.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:mellin-data}
$\tilde H$ is analytic on the strip by differentiation under the integral sign, the majorant
$s^{c-1}H(s)$ being locally uniformly integrable there. It is not identically zero: by
\ref{lem:mellin-data}, $\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z)\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$, and both factors are strictly
positive at real $z \in (0, z_*)$. Hence its zeros are isolated, and the quotient
$\tilde H(z+1)/\tilde H(z)$ --- which is $z\,\Ex[T_1^{-z-1}]/\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$ by
$\Gamma(z+1) = z\Gamma(z)$ --- is meromorphic with poles only there.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the operator computes the realising function]\label{lem:inversion-operator-action}

\qquad
  \widetilde{A g}(z) = \tilde h(z-1) \quad\text{for every } z .
\]

\begin{proof}

second is then the Mellin transform of $x^{-1}h(x)$, i.e.\ $\tilde h$ with its argument shifted by

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the delayed average is the field, and its transform]\label{lem:delayed-average-mellin}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,prop:canonical-gauge,def:cascade-family}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin_delayedField}\leanok
Assume (H) and work in the canonical gauge; let $f \in L^1$ be causal and $t > 0$. Then for every
$x > 0$,
\[
  \bigl(\Phi_{0,x}f\bigr)(\cdot) \;=\; \Ex\bigl[f(\,\cdot - xT_1)\bigr] \quad\text{a.e.},
\]
and for $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$ the right-hand side, read as a function of $x$, has
\[
  \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\,\Ex\bigl[f(t - xT_1)\bigr]\,dx \;=\; \tilde H(z)\,\bigl(I^z f\bigr)(t),
\]
both sides absolutely convergent.
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.mellin\_delayedField}, with

\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}; what is left there is its derivative clause.}

\emph{\textbf{The quantifiers do not commute, and that is the honest reason a representative has to
be chosen.} $\Phi_{0,x}$ acts on $L^1$ classes, so the first display holds for each $x$ only up to
a null set of times --- and the null set depends on $x$. ``For each $x$, for a.e.\ $t$'' does not
give ``for a.e.\ $t$, for every $x$'', so the second display, which is an integral over $x$ at a
fixed $t$, cannot be taken of $(\Phi_{0,x}f)(t)$ as it stands. Some representative must be named

\emph{\textbf{$I^z$ is defined in the development, not cited.} Mathlib carries no fractional
integral of any order, so the Riemann--Liouville integral is given its definition here. That adds
no interface: \sscite{samko1993fractional} is cited in this chapter for the notation and the
theory of $I^z$, and nothing here uses more of that theory than the definition itself.}

\emph{\textbf{The exchange is licensed by the two ends of the strip, one apiece.} Inside, the
dilate of the past integrand is integrable because $\operatorname{Re} z > 1$: on $(0,t]$ the
weight $y^{c-1}$ is bounded by $t^{c-1}$, so $f$ needs nothing beyond $L^1$, and beyond $t$
causality kills the integrand outright. Outside, the dilation $x \mapsto \tau x$ leaves exactly
the factor $\tau^{-z}$, whose expectation is finite because $\operatorname{Re} z < z_*$. That is
the same shape as \ref{lem:mellin-data}'s hinge with the lower end moved from $0$ to $1$ --- and
the move is what \emph{makes} (H) ask for $z_* > 1$ rather than $z_* > 0$. Below $1$ this lemma

why \ref{prop:extreme-rays}'s observation that every extreme ray has $z_* = 1$ bites here.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:mellin-data}

the two integrals; the inner one is then a dilate, contributing $\tau^{-z}$ times the past
integral, and the outer integral of $\tau^{-z}$ is $\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$. The past integral is

already confined the integral to $(0,t]$; and $\Ex[T_1^{-z}]\Gamma(z) = \tilde H(z)$ by
\ref{lem:mellin-data}. The two $\Gamma$'s cancel, which is why the statement has none.
\end{proof}

poses them under} --- they are unconditional facts about $z_*$ and \texttt{negMoment}, so what is
proved is strictly more than clause~2 asks for, not less.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(1) $s \mapsto H(s) := \Ex[e^{-sT_1}]$ decreases to $\Pr[T_1 = 0]$ as $s \to \infty$: the
integrand $e^{-sT_1}$ is dominated by the constant $1$ and converges pointwise to the indicator
of $\{T_1 = 0\}$, so dominated convergence gives $H(s) \to \Pr[T_1=0]$. Since $H(s) = e^{-F(s)}$,
$F(\infty) = \infty$ iff $H(s) \to 0$ iff $\Pr[T_1 = 0] = 0$. For general $x > 0$, $\mu_{0,x}$ is
the law of $xT_1$ (the canonical gauge), and multiplication by $x \ne 0$ carries $\{0\}$ to
itself and nowhere else, so $\mu_{0,x}(\{0\}) = \Pr[xT_1 = 0] = \Pr[T_1 = 0]$ for every $x > 0$;
the case $x=1$ already gives the stated equivalence, and every other $x$ agrees with it.

(2) By Tonelli, $\int_0^\infty e^{-F(s)}\,ds = \int_0^\infty \Ex[e^{-sT_1}]\,ds =
\Ex\bigl[\int_0^\infty e^{-sT_1}\,ds\bigr] = \Ex[T_1^{-1}]$ in $[0,\infty]$, the inner integral
$\int_0^\infty e^{-sT_1}ds = T_1^{-1}$ being licensed pointwise because $T_1 > 0$ almost surely
by~(1). For the two consequences, the finiteness set
$\{\zeta > 0 : \Ex[T_1^{-\zeta}] < \infty\}$ is downward closed --- $t^{-\zeta'} \le
t^{-\zeta} + 1$ for $t > 0$ and $\zeta' \le \zeta$, so a finite $\zeta$-th moment bounds every
smaller one up to an additive unit of mass --- so $z_* > 1$ puts a real $\zeta > 1$ in the set,
and hence $1$ in it too, giving $\Ex[T_1^{-1}] < \infty$; conversely if $\Ex[T_1^{-1}] < \infty$
then $1$ is itself a member of the set whose supremum is $z_*$, so $z_* \ge 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lévy-data reading; the first clause is automatic]\label{lem:standing-levy-reading}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}
\lean{Skeleton.tendsto_toRealExponent_atTop_iff_levy,Skeleton.tendsto_toRealExponent_atTop_of_ne_zero}\notready

\begin{proof}
(1) ($\Leftarrow$) If $b_0 > 0$ then $F(s) \ge b_0 s \to \infty$. If $\int_0^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt =
\infty$, monotone convergence in (7.1) --- $1 - e^{-st}$ increases pointwise to $1$ on
$(0,\infty)$ as $s \to \infty$ --- gives $F(s) \to b_0\cdot\infty + \int_0^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt =
\infty$. ($\Rightarrow$) Contrapositive: if $b_0 = 0$ and $\int_0^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt =: M <
\infty$, the same monotone limit gives $F(s)$ increasing to $M < \infty$, so
$F(\infty) \ne \infty$.

(2) If $k \not\equiv 0$, nonincreasing $k$ that is positive somewhere is bounded below by that
value on the interval below it: pick $t_0$ with $k(t_0) > 0$, then $k(t) \ge k(t_0)$ for
$t \in (0,t_0]$, so $\int_0^{t_0} k(t)t^{-1}dt \ge k(t_0)\int_0^{t_0}t^{-1}dt = \infty$, and (1)
gives $F(\infty) = \infty$. If $k \equiv 0$ and $F \not\equiv 0$, (7.1) reduces to $F(s) = b_0 s$
with $b_0 > 0$, and (1) again gives $F(\infty) = \infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the log-growth reading of $z_*$]\label{lem:zstar-log-growth}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:memory-kernel,lem:standing-levy-reading,lem:standing-kernel-readings,thm:main-characterization,lem:admissible-cone}
\lean{Skeleton.tendsto_toRealExponent_div_log_atTop_zStar,Skeleton.tendsto_toRealExponent_div_log_atTop_of_b₀_pos,Skeleton.tendsto_toRealExponent_div_log_atTop_of_b₀_zero,Skeleton.zStar_smul}\notready
Let $F \not\equiv 0$ be admissible, with $(b_0, k)$ as in (7.1). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The limit $\lim_{s\to\infty} F(s)/\log s$ exists in $[0,\infty]$ and equals $z_*$.
\item The limit is $\infty$ if $b_0 > 0$, and equals $k(0^+) := \sup_{t>0} k(t)$ if $b_0 = 0$
(finite or infinite).
\item Consequently, (H) holds if and only if $b_0 > 0$ or $k(0^+) > 1$.
\item $z_*$ is homogeneous of degree one on the admissible cone: $z_*(cF) = c\,z_*(F)$ for every
$c > 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $F \not\equiv 0$ is admissible, \ref{lem:standing-levy-reading}(2) gives
$F(\infty) = \infty$, and \ref{lem:standing-kernel-readings}(1) turns that into $T_1 > 0$ almost
surely, so $\Ex[T_1^{-\zeta}]$ is an honest integral over $(0,\infty)$ for every $\zeta > 0$.

\textbf{The exponent's slope.} By \ref{lem:memory-kernel}, $F'(s) = b_0 + \int_0^\infty
e^{-st}k(t)\,dt$, so $B(s) := sF'(s) = b_0 s + \int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,k(u/s)\,du$ after the
substitution $u = st$. For $0 < s_1 \le s_2$: $b_0 s_1 \le b_0 s_2$, and $k$ nonincreasing gives
$k(u/s_1) \le k(u/s_2)$ pointwise, since $u/s_1 \ge u/s_2$; so $B$ is nondecreasing --- no appeal
to \ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} or to $B$ being a Bernstein function as a class, only to
the explicit formula and the definition of nonincreasing. And since $k(u/s)$ increases pointwise
to $k(0^+)$ as $s \to \infty$, monotone convergence gives
$B(\infty) = b_0\cdot\infty + k(0^+)\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,du$: $\infty$ if $b_0 > 0$, and $k(0^+)$
if $b_0 = 0$. That is clause~(2).

(1) $G(u) := F(e^u)$ has $G'(u) = e^u F'(e^u) = B(e^u)$ nondecreasing by the above, so $G$ is
convex in $u = \log s$. A convex function's difference quotient $G(u)/u$ increases, past any zero
of $u$, to its terminal slope, so
$L := \lim_{s\to\infty} F(s)/\log s = \lim_{u\to\infty} G(u)/u = \lim_{s\to\infty} B(s)$ exists in
$[0,\infty]$, with value given by clause~(2). To identify $L$ with $z_*$: by Tonelli,
$\Gamma(\zeta)\,\Ex[T_1^{-\zeta}] = \int_0^\infty s^{\zeta-1}e^{-F(s)}\,ds$ for every $\zeta > 0$,
the exchange licensed exactly as in \ref{lem:standing-kernel-readings}(2), now at general $\zeta$
rather than at $\zeta = 1$. For $0 < \zeta < L$, eventually $F(s) \ge (\zeta+\varepsilon)\log s$
for some $\varepsilon > 0$, so $e^{-F(s)} \le s^{-\zeta-\varepsilon}$ eventually and the integral
converges; for $\zeta > L$, eventually $F(s) \le (\zeta - \varepsilon)\log s$, so the integrand is
eventually $\ge s^{\varepsilon - 1}$ and the integral diverges. Hence
$\{\zeta > 0 : \Ex[T_1^{-\zeta}] < \infty\}$ contains every $\zeta < L$ and excludes every
$\zeta > L$, so its supremum, $z_*$, is $L$.

(3) is~(1)--(2) read against (H)'s two clauses. The first is automatic here by
\ref{lem:standing-levy-reading}(2), so (H) reduces to $z_* > 1$, which by~(2) is exactly
$b_0 > 0$ or $k(0^+) > 1$ --- an equality throughout, so with no boundary subtlety.

(4) $cF$ is admissible by \ref{lem:admissible-cone}, with data $(cb_0, ck)$, so
$k_{cF}(0^+) = c\,k(0^+)$ and $(cF)$'s drift is $cb_0$; applying~(1)--(2) to $cF$ in place of $F$
gives $z_*(cF) = c\,z_*(F)$, whether $b_0 > 0$ or $b_0 = 0$.
\end{proof}
