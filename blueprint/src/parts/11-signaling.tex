Theorem~4 of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} inverts the scale-Cauchy problem into an
evolution \emph{in time} over the memory line: $\partial_t u = D^{1/\alpha}_{x,-} u$ with
boundary data $u(t, 0) = f(t)$ and $u(0, x) = 0$ --- the signaling problem. The operator there
is translation-invariant in $x$, which is available only in the homogeneous (semigroup) case.
This chapter constructs the general inversion.

The structural observation that replaces homogeneity is this: writing $H := e^{-F}$, the
Laplace-domain profiles of the field,
\[
  \hat u(s, x) = \hat f(s)\, H(sx),
\]
are \emph{dilates of a single profile} $H$, with the eigenvalue $s$ appearing as the dilation
parameter. Operators diagonal on dilation families are the subject of multiplicative harmonic
analysis, so the inversion operator lives in the \textbf{Mellin calculus} --- the
multiplicative-group counterpart of the Wendel step of Chapter~4 on the additive group.

Throughout we work in the canonical gauge, so that $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $x\,T_1$ with
$\Ex[e^{-sT_1}] = H(s)$; we write $\tilde g(z) := \int_0^\infty x^{z-1} g(x)\,dx$ for the
Mellin transform, $\theta := x\,\partial_x$ for the Euler operator, and
$(I^z f)(t) := \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_0^t (t-r)^{z-1} f(r)\,dr$ for the Riemann--Liouville
integral of complex order $z$, $\operatorname{Re} z > 0$ (\sscite{samko1993fractional}).

% draft: Definition 11.1
\begin{definition}[the standing hypothesis (H)]\label{def:standing-hypothesis}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,thm:main-characterization}
An admissible exponent $F$ satisfies the \emph{standing hypothesis} (H) if
$F(\infty) = \infty$ --- equivalently $b_0 > 0$ or $\int_0^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt = \infty$,
i.e.\ no atom of the kernels at zero delay --- and
\[
  z_* \;:=\; \sup\bigl\{\, \zeta > 0 :\ \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-\zeta}\bigr] < \infty \,\bigr\} \;>\; 1 .
\]
\statusT
\end{definition}

The second condition is equivalent to $\int_0^\infty e^{-F(s)}\,ds < \infty$. It holds with
$z_* = \infty$ whenever $b_0 > 0$, for the stable family, and for the Bessel-K family of
\ref{prop:bessel-family}; for the Gamma family $z_* = \gamma$, so (H) requires $\gamma > 1$
there. The construction extends below $z_* = 1$ by meromorphic continuation, which we do not
pursue.

% draft: Lemma 11.2
\begin{lemma}[Mellin data of the profile]\label{lem:mellin-data}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,thm:main-characterization,prop:moment-criterion}
Under (H), for $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \tilde H(z) \;=\; \Gamma(z)\;\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr],
\]
analytic on the strip, with
$|\tilde H(c + i\tau)| \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\,|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$, hence absolutely integrable on
every vertical line of the strip. Define the \emph{inversion symbol}
\[
  B(-z) \;:=\; \frac{\tilde H(z+1)}{\tilde H(z)}
  \;=\; z\,\frac{\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z-1}\bigr]}{\Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]},
  \qquad 0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_* - 1,
\]
meromorphic on the strip, with poles only at the isolated zeros of $\tilde H$.
\statusT
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Tonelli,
\[
  \tilde H(z) = \int_0^\infty s^{z-1}\,\Ex[e^{-sT_1}]\,ds
  = \Ex\Bigl[\int_0^\infty s^{z-1}e^{-sT_1}ds\Bigr]
  = \Ex[T_1^{-z}]\,\Gamma(z).
\]
Finiteness on the strip is the definition of $z_*$ together with
$|T_1^{-z}| = T_1^{-\operatorname{Re}z}$, which also gives the stated bound; and
$|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$ decays super-polynomially in $\tau$, so the bound is integrable on vertical
lines. Analyticity is standard. Finally $\tilde H \not\equiv 0$, so its zeros are isolated and
$B$ is meromorphic with poles only there.
\end{proof}

% draft: Definition 11.3
\begin{definition}[the inversion operator]\label{def:inversion-operator}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,def:cascade-family}
Fix $c \in (0, z_* - 1)$. For $g$ such that $\tilde g$ is defined on the line
$\operatorname{Re} z = c$ and $B(-z)\tilde g(z)$ is absolutely integrable there, set
\[
  \bigl(A g\bigr)(x) \;:=\; \frac{1}{x}\cdot \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)} x^{-z}\, B(-z)\,\tilde g(z)\,dz
  \;=\; \Bigl(\frac{1}{x}\,B(\theta)\,g\Bigr)(x),
\]
the second expression being the functional-calculus reading, the Euler operator having Mellin
symbol $-z$. Equivalently, at the transform level,
$\widetilde{A g}(z) = B(1 - z)\,\tilde g(z - 1)$. Note the covariant scaling: $A$ commutes with
dilations up to the weight $x^{-1}$, i.e.\ it scales like $\partial_t$ under
$(t, x) \mapsto (\sigma t, \sigma x)$, as (A8) requires of any inversion.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A12}\quad\emph{(Mellin inversion on a line of absolute convergence;
\sscite{widder1941laplace}, Ch.~VI, \S9, Thm.~9a. Cited, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A12} carries exactly the step that makes this a definition of an
operator rather than of an integral: that absolute integrability on the line
$\operatorname{Re} z = c$ is sufficient for the contour integral to recover $B(\theta)g$.
Everything else is \textbf{[T]} --- the symbol $B$ and its strip come from
\ref{lem:mellin-data}, the transform-level identity is a change of variable, and the covariance
is a computation. The ledger records a correction to the draft here: it cited
\sscite{samko1993fractional} for this, which states Mellin inversion only as an unhypothesized
display, equation~(1.113); Widder states it with the absolute-convergence hypothesis that is
exactly the one imposed above.)}
\end{definition}

% draft: Lemma 11.4
\begin{lemma}[uniqueness of the symbol]\label{lem:symbol-uniqueness}
\uses{lem:mellin-data,def:inversion-operator}
If two operators of the form $x^{-1}B_1(\theta)$ and $x^{-1}B_2(\theta)$ both satisfy
$A[H(s\,\cdot)] = s\,H(s\,\cdot)$ for all $s > 0$, then $B_1 = B_2$ on the strip. Hence $A$ is
the unique inversion within the covariant Mellin class.
\statusT
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluating both operators on the profiles and taking Mellin transforms gives
$B_1(-z)\,s^{-z}\tilde H(z) = B_2(-z)\,s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$ on the line. By
\ref{lem:mellin-data}, $\tilde H \ne 0$ off an isolated set, so $B_1 = B_2$ almost everywhere
on the line, hence everywhere on the strip by meromorphy.
\end{proof}

% draft: Lemma 11.5
\begin{lemma}[the memory line stores fractional integrals]\label{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,thm:main-characterization,lem:delay-core}
Under (H), let $f \in \Xz$ be causal and $u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f$. Then for every $t > 0$
and $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\, u(t, x)\,dx
  \;=\; \tilde H(z)\,\bigl(I^{z} f\bigr)(t),
\]
absolutely convergent. If moreover $f \in \core$, the same holds for $\partial_t u$ with
$I^z f$ replaced by $I^z f' = I^{z-1} f$.
\statusT
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the canonical gauge $u(t, x) = \Ex\bigl[f(t - x T_1)\bigr]$. Tonelli applies, since for
$c = \operatorname{Re} z > 1$,
$\Ex[T_1^{-c}]\int_0^t y^{c-1}|f(t-y)|\,dy \le \Ex[T_1^{-c}]\, t^{c-1}\|f\|_1 < \infty$, and
gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty x^{z-1}\,\Ex\bigl[f(t - xT_1)\bigr]\,dx
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\int_0^t y^{z-1} f(t - y)\,dy
  \;=\; \Ex\bigl[T_1^{-z}\bigr]\,\Gamma(z)\,(I^z f)(t),
\]
which is the claim by \ref{lem:mellin-data}. For $f \in \core$ we have
$\partial_t u(t, x) = \Ex[f'(t - xT_1)]$, and $I^z f' = I^{z-1} I^1 f' = I^{z-1} f$ using
$f(0) = 0$.
\end{proof}

% draft: Theorem 11.6
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4$'$: the signaling form]\label{thm:signaling-form}
\uses{def:standing-hypothesis,lem:mellin-data,def:inversion-operator,lem:symbol-uniqueness,lem:memory-fractional-integrals,def:cascade-family,thm:main-characterization}
Assume (H) and work in the canonical gauge. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Eigenfunctions.)} For every $s > 0$ the profile $H(s\,\cdot)$ lies in the domain
of \ref{def:inversion-operator} for every $c \in (0, z_*-1)$, and
\[
  A\bigl[H(s\,\cdot)\bigr](x) \;=\; s\, H(sx), \qquad x > 0 .
\]
\item \emph{(The field solves the signaling problem.)} Let $f \in \core$ and
$u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f$. Then $u$ is causal in $t$ with $u(\cdot, x) \to f$ in $\Xz$ as
$x \downarrow 0$, and
\begin{itemize}
\item Laplace form: for every $s > 0$, $A\bigl[\hat u(s, \cdot)\bigr] = s\,\hat u(s, \cdot)$,
with $\hat u(s, \zp) = \hat f(s)$;
\item time-domain (Mellin) form: for every $t > 0$ and $1 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$,
\[
  \widetilde{\partial_t u(t, \cdot)}(z) \;=\; B(1 - z)\;\widetilde{u(t, \cdot)}(z - 1),
\]
that is, $\partial_t u = A\,u$ read through the Mellin transform in $x$.
\end{itemize}
\item \emph{(Uniqueness.)} $A$ is the unique operator in the covariant Mellin class with
property (1), hence with property (2).
\end{enumerate}
\statusT\quad\emph{(the hemigroup analogue of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~4, whose
translation-invariant operator is recovered in \ref{ex:stable-signaling} as an accident of
homogeneity. Proved here, not cited.)}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) $\widetilde{H(s\,\cdot)}(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$, so
$B(-z)\widetilde{H(s\cdot)}(z) = s^{-z}\tilde H(z+1)$, which is absolutely integrable on the
line $(c)$ by \ref{lem:mellin-data}; no pole of $B$ intervenes, the product containing no
division. Then
\[
  A\bigl[H(s\cdot)\bigr](x)
  = \frac{1}{x}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)} (xs)^{-z}\,\tilde H(z+1)\,dz
  = \frac{1}{x}\,(xs)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c+1)} (xs)^{-w}\,\tilde H(w)\,dw
  = s\,H(sx),
\]
by the substitution $w = z + 1$ and Mellin inversion on the line
$c + 1 \in (1, z_*)$, valid since $\tilde H$ is integrable there and $H$ is continuous.

(2) Causality and the boundary attainment are (A3) and (A7). The Laplace form is (1) applied
to $\hat u(s, \cdot) = \hat f(s) H(s\,\cdot)$. The Mellin form combines
\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals} with \ref{lem:mellin-data}:
\[
  B(1-z)\,\widetilde{u(t,\cdot)}(z-1)
  = \frac{\tilde H(z)}{\tilde H(z-1)}\cdot\tilde H(z-1)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \tilde H(z)\,(I^{z-1}f)(t)
  = \widetilde{\partial_t u(t,\cdot)}(z).
\]

(3) is \ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness}.
\end{proof}

% draft: Remark 11.7
\begin{remark}[reading of Lemma 11.5; embodiment]\label{rem:embodiment}
\uses{lem:memory-fractional-integrals}
\ref{lem:memory-fractional-integrals} gives the motivation of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S5, an exact form: at each instant $t$, the Mellin analysis
of the memory line holds precisely the analytic family of Riemann--Liouville integrals
$\{(I^z f)(t)\}$ of the \emph{past} signal, weighted by the negative delay-moments
$\Ex[T_1^{-z}]$. The observer's embodiment of its past is fractional integration, with the
admissible weightings classified by \ref{thm:main-characterization}.
\end{remark}

% draft: Remark 11.8
\begin{remark}[classical interpretation; poles]\label{rem:poles}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,lem:mellin-data}
The time-domain equation in \ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) is stated at the level of Mellin
transforms, and that is the honest general formulation: $B$ may have poles, at zeros of
$\tilde H$, and polynomial growth along vertical lines, so $A u(t, \cdot)$ need not be given by
an absolutely convergent integral for rough $f$. On the field the identity is nevertheless
regular, the factor $\tilde H(z-1)$ cancelling every denominator.

When the symbol $B$ is a \emph{polynomial}, $A$ is a local differential operator and the
equation is a classical PDE. That is precisely the situation of Theorem~5$'$ in Chapter~12,
and of \ref{ex:bessel-quadratic} below.
\end{remark}

% draft: Example 11.9
\begin{example}[extremal stable; recovering Theorem 4 of 2005]\label{ex:stable-signaling}
\uses{prop:stable-family,lem:mellin-data,thm:signaling-form}
For $F(s) = s^\alpha$ we have $\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z/\alpha)/\alpha$ and
\[
  B(-z) \;=\; \frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{z+1}{\alpha}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{z}{\alpha}\bigr)}
  \;\sim\; \Bigl(\tfrac{z}{\alpha}\Bigr)^{1/\alpha} \quad (z \to \infty),
\]
a symbol of order $1/\alpha$. On the profiles $e^{-(sx)^\alpha}$ the right-sided Liouville
derivative acts by $D^{1/\alpha}_{x,-} e^{-cx} = c^{1/\alpha} e^{-cx}$ with $c = s^\alpha$,
i.e.\ it also produces the eigenvalue $s$; so the Mellin representative and the operator of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~4, agree on the span of the field profiles. The
translation-invariant representative is a coincidence of homogeneity; the Mellin
representative is the one that survives in general.
\end{example}

% draft: Example 11.10
\begin{example}[the Gamma family; scale-recursive form]\label{ex:gamma-signaling}
\uses{prop:gamma-family,ex:gamma-recursive,def:inversion-operator}
For $F(s) = \gamma \log(1+s)$ with $\gamma > 1$ we have
$\tilde H(z) = \Gamma(z)\Gamma(\gamma - z)/\Gamma(\gamma)$ and $B(-z) = z/(\gamma - 1 - z)$,
i.e.
\[
  B(\theta) = -\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
  \qquad A = -\,\frac{1}{x}\,\theta\,\bigl(\theta + \gamma - 1\bigr)^{-1},
\]
a \emph{rational} Mellin symbol --- the memory-line mirror of the time-side resolvent
$\gamma\,\partial_t(1 + x\partial_t)^{-1}$ of \ref{ex:gamma-recursive}. Explicitly,
$w := (\theta + \gamma - 1)^{-1} u$ solves $x\,w_x + (\gamma-1)w = u$, with the regular-at-$0$
solution
\[
  w(t, x) = x^{-(\gamma-1)}\int_0^x y^{\gamma - 2}\, u(t, y)\,dy,
\]
so the time evolution at scale $x$ is computed from the field at \emph{finer} scales by a
single weighted average --- scale-recursive, one-sided from the boundary, matching the causal
architecture of the time side.
\end{example}

% draft: Example 11.11
\begin{example}[parabolic gauge; the Bessel family is the quadratic case]\label{ex:bessel-quadratic}
\uses{prop:bessel-family,rem:gauge-freedom,def:inversion-operator,rem:poles}
In the $\alpha$-gauge (similarity variable $x^{1/\alpha} s$; \ref{rem:gauge-freedom}) the
profiles are $H(x^{1/\alpha}s)$, the Euler operator acts on the profile through
$\alpha^{-1}\theta$, and the inversion becomes $A = x^{-1/\alpha}\,B(\alpha\,\theta)$. Take the
parabolic gauge $\alpha = \tfrac12$ and the Bessel-K family of \ref{prop:bessel-family},
$T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$: then $\Ex[T_1^{-z}] = 2^z\,\Gamma(a + z)/\Gamma(a)$, so
\[
  B(-z) = 2z(z + a), \qquad
  A = x^{-2}\Bigl(\tfrac{\theta^2}{2} - a\theta\Bigr)
  = \tfrac12\,\partial_x^2 + \frac{\beta}{x}\,\partial_x, \quad \beta = \tfrac12 - a .
\]
The symbol is a \emph{quadratic polynomial}, so $A$ is local: the signaling form of the
Bessel-K scale space is exactly the Bessel diffusion-with-drift medium, and
\ref{thm:signaling-form}(2) becomes the classical signaling problem
$\partial_t u = \tfrac12 u_{xx} + \tfrac{\beta}{x}u_x$ with $u(t,0) = f(t)$ and $u(0,x) = 0$.
\end{example}

% draft: Remark 11.12
\begin{remark}[Markov media]\label{rem:markov-media}
\uses{thm:signaling-form,ex:stable-signaling,ex:gamma-signaling}
Within the class of \ref{thm:signaling-form}, the operators $A$ that additionally satisfy the
positive maximum principle --- i.e.\ generate a sub-Markov process on the memory half-line, so
that the medium is a physical transport medium --- are, after the logarithmic substitution
$x = e^\xi$, exactly those whose symbol $B$ is the Laplace exponent of a Lévy process. By the
Lamperti correspondence (\sscite{lamperti1972semi}) these media are positive self-similar
Markov processes absorbed at the boundary, and the kernels are the boundary-arrival times
$T_x \stackrel{d}{=}$ absorption time from depth $x$.

The spectral orientation of the jumps is \emph{not} constrained. For spectrally positive media
the process creeps downward through every level and the hemigroup (A6) holds \emph{pathwise},
the kernels being genuine level-passage times; but media with downward jumps are also
admissible, the factorization then holding in law though not pathwise, being inherited from
the exponent $F$ rather than from the paths.

The Gamma family is the standard example: unfolding \ref{ex:gamma-signaling}, its medium is
the multiplicative jump process that waits at scale $x$ an $\mathrm{Exp}(\text{mean } x)$ time
and jumps to $\sigma x$ with $\sigma \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\gamma-1, 1)$, reaching the boundary by
accumulation of jumps; a first-jump computation confirms
$\Ex_x[e^{-s\tau_0}] = (1+sx)^{-\gamma}$.

A necessary condition in all cases is quadratic symbol growth, $B(\theta) = O(\theta^2)$; by
\ref{ex:stable-signaling} the homogeneous members with $\alpha < \tfrac12$, of symbol order
$1/\alpha > 2$, admit \emph{no} Markov medium, although \ref{thm:signaling-form} always
supplies the analytic signaling form --- the operator exists, the process does not. The
classification of which admissible $F$ are Markov-realizable is the memory-line inverse
problem, and is taken up with Theorem~5$'$.
\end{remark}
