The representation (7.1) attaches to every admissible family a nonincreasing function $k$.
This appendix shows that $k$ is the \emph{memory kernel} of the evolution across scale; that it
forms a Sonine pair with the associated potential kernel, through the conservation identity
$\kappa * \ell \equiv 1$; and that the kernels of \ref{thm:main-characterization} satisfy a
linear Volterra integral equation from which they can be computed, without Laplace inversion.
This half of the appendix is self-contained: the scale-evolution result it needs is stated and proved as
\ref{prop:scale-evolution}.

Throughout we work in the canonical gauge, so that $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $x\,T_1$.
Throughout, $F$ is an admissible exponent as in (7.1), with drift $b_0$ and kernel $k$, and
$F \not\equiv 0$ (equivalently (ND); \ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}). A function $f \in X$ is
\emph{causal} if it vanishes a.e.\ on $(-\infty, 0)$, in which case
$\hat f(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st} f(t)\,dt$ is defined for $s > 0$ and satisfies
$|\hat f(s)| \le \|f\|_1$.

\begin{lemma}[the kernel behind $F'$]\label{lem:memory-kernel}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,lem:criterion-converse}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.hasDerivAt_toRealExponent}\leanok
Let $F$ be as in (7.1). Then
\[
  F'(s) \;=\; b_0 \;+\; \int_0^\infty e^{-st}\, k(t)\,dt, \qquad s > 0.
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.hasDerivAt\_toRealExponent}; \texttt{\#print axioms}
reduces to Lean core. Differentiation under the integral sign, which Mathlib has; all the work is
in the side conditions, and \emph{none of them is a hypothesis}. The dominating function
$e^{-(s/2)t}k(t)$ is integrable because \ref{lem:criterion-converse} extracts both integrability
facts from the class membership itself, so this lemma applies to every admissible $F$ with no
regularity assumed of $k$ beyond the definition. That is worth recording because the proof below
reads as though $\int_0^1 k < \infty$ and boundedness at infinity were extra conditions to be
checked per family; they are not, and 8.8 is why.}

\emph{Two small design points the formalisation fixed. The neighbourhood on which the domination
holds is $(s/2,\infty)$ rather than a ball, which is what makes the bound uniform without a case
split. And the exponent must first be read as a real-valued Bochner integral --- the development
carries it in $[0,\infty]$ so that no integrability hypothesis travels with the definition ---
which is \texttt{toRealExponent\_eq}, valid for $s \ge 0$ by the structure's \texttt{ne\_top}

in.)}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Differentiating the integrand of (7.1) in $s$ gives $e^{-st}k(t)$, which for $s \ge s_0 > 0$ is
dominated by $e^{-s_0 t} k(t)$. That dominating function is integrable near $0$ because
$\int_0^1 k < \infty$, and at infinity because $k$ is bounded there, being nonincreasing; so
differentiation under the integral sign is justified.
\end{proof}

\bigskip

Thus the memory kernel of the scale flow is the self-decomposability function $k$ itself, in
$L^1$-normalized dilation to the current scale. The monotonicity of $k$, which entered
abstractly as condition (3) of \ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, is precisely the
admissibility of $\kappa^{(x)}$ as a Lévy tail. In the extremal stable case
$k(t) = \alpha t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)$ and the operator $\partial_t(\kappa^{(x)} * \cdot)$
below is the Riemann--Liouville derivative $D^{\alpha}_{t,+}$ of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}; in the Gamma family $k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$, a bounded kernel.

\begin{proposition}[scale evolution in memory-kernel form]\label{prop:scale-evolution}
\uses{thm:main-characterization,lem:memory-kernel,lem:memory-kernel-transform,lem:transform-ode,prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}
Let $(\Phi_{x,y})$ be as in \ref{thm:main-characterization}, in the canonical gauge, let
$f \in X$ be causal, and set $u(\cdot, x) := \Phi_{0,x} f = \mu_{0,x} * f$. Then, with the
transform equation $\partial_x \hat u = -sF'(xs)\hat u$ of \ref{lem:transform-ode} in hand:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] For each $x > 0$ the causal function $w(\cdot, x) := \kappa^{(x)} * u(\cdot, x)$ lies in
$L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and for all $0 \le a \le b$,
\[
  u(\cdot, b) - u(\cdot, a)
  \;=\; -\,\partial_t \int_a^b \bigl(\kappa^{(x)} * u(\cdot, x)\bigr)\,dx
  \qquad \text{in } \Distr(\RR).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Differentiating in $x$, $u$ satisfies, in the distributional sense on
$\RR_t \times (0,\infty)_x$ and with boundary value $u(\cdot, 0) = f$,
\[
  \partial_x u \;=\; -\,\partial_t\bigl(\kappa^{(x)} * u\bigr)
  \;=\; -\,b_0\,\partial_t u
  \;-\; \partial_t \int_0^t \tfrac{1}{x}\,k\bigl(\tfrac{t-r}{x}\bigr)\,u(r, x)\,dr .
\]
Since $u(\cdot, x)$ is causal, the outer $\partial_t$ may equivalently be moved onto $u$ inside
the convolution: the Riemann--Liouville and Caputo forms of the operator coincide on this
family.
\statusT\quad\emph{(the scalar transform equation this rests on was split off as
\ref{lem:transform-ode}. Both halves are \textbf{[T]} and no ledger entry is involved either
way, so the split is not about cost --- it is about \emph{reach}: the transform equation is a
chain rule and is formalisable today, while the identity here is distributional, and Mathlib has
no distribution theory to state it in. Keeping them in one node would have made a provable
result unreachable behind an unprovable one.}

\emph{\textbf{Corrected 2026-08-11.} That last clause was true when written and is not now:
\texttt{Analysis/Distribution/} carries test functions, the space $\Distr(\Omega,F)$, the Dirac
distribution, and a distributional directional derivative as a continuous linear map. It is
still not enough for this node, whose statement needs a locally integrable function read as a

own docstring says it ``contains very few mathematical statements''. The correction is recorded
because this is a moving target: the right habit is to re-check when Mathlib is bumped, not to
treat the node as permanently out of reach.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Local integrability.} For $T > 0$,
\[
  \int_0^T |w(t,x)|\,dt \le \kappa^{(x)}([0,T])\,\|u(\cdot,x)\|_1
  \le \Bigl(b_0 + \int_0^{T/x} k\Bigr)\|f\|_1 < \infty,
\]
locally uniformly in $x > 0$; joint measurability follows from (A7). By Tonelli, for $s > 0$,
$\hat w(s, x) = \widehat{\kappa^{(x)}}(s)\,\hat u(s, x) = F'(xs)\,\hat u(s, x)$.

\emph{Integration in $x$.} Integrate \ref{lem:transform-ode} over $x \in [a, b]$. The exchange of $\int_a^b dx$
with the Laplace integral is justified by Tonelli, since
$\int_a^b s\,F'(xs)\,\|u(\cdot,x)\|_1\,dx \le \|f\|_1 \int_a^b s F'(xs)\,dx
= \|f\|_1\bigl(F(bs) - F(as)\bigr) < \infty$, finite also for $a = 0$. This gives
\[
  \hat u(s, b) - \hat u(s, a) \;=\; -\,s \int_a^b \hat w(s, x)\,dx \;=\; -\,s\,\widehat{W}(s),
  \qquad W := \int_a^b w(\cdot, x)\,dx,
\]
with $W \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and causal.

\emph{Inversion.} The causal, locally integrable function
$V(t) := \int_0^t \bigl(u(r, b) - u(r, a)\bigr)\,dr + W(t)$ has Laplace transform
$\bigl(\hat u(s,b) - \hat u(s,a)\bigr)/s + \widehat W(s) = 0$ for every $s > 0$, so $V = 0$
a.e.\ by \ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}. That is the displayed identity. The final statement
holds because $u(\cdot, x)$ vanishes on $(-\infty, 0)$, so no boundary term arises when moving
$\partial_t$ through the convolution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[potential kernel]\label{lem:potential-kernel}
\uses{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents,prop:bernstein-toolbox,lem:vanishing,prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.existsUnique_potentialKernel}\leanok
For $x > 0$ set $\varphi_x(s) := s F'(xs)$. Then $\varphi_x$ is a nonzero Bernstein function,
positive on $(0,\infty)$, and there is a unique positive, locally finite measure $\ell^{(x)}$
on $[0,\infty)$ --- the \emph{potential kernel} --- with
\[
  \widehat{\ell^{(x)}}(s) \;=\; \frac{1}{\varphi_x(s)} \;=\; \frac{1}{s\,F'(xs)}, \qquad s > 0.
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.existsUnique\_potentialKernel}; \texttt{\#print axioms}
gives \ledger{A17} and nothing else. Route B --- construct the measure as the
subordinator's potential $U = \int_0^\infty \mu_t\,dt$ rather than represent it via
Bernstein--Widder --- is what is proved: causality, the transform by Tonelli, local finiteness
from convergence at one point, and uniqueness from Laplace injectivity for measures that are not
finite; the two sub-lemmas that were once open --- that $\varphi_x$ is a L\'evy exponent with its
triple exhibited (\texttt{exists\_levyTriple\_symbol}: drift $b_0$, L\'evy measure the Stieltjes
measure $-d[k(\cdot/x)/x]$), and that the laws $\mu_t$ form a \emph{measurable} family
(\texttt{exists\_subordinatorFamily}) --- are proved. The statement's first sentence, that
$\varphi_x$ is a nonzero Bernstein function positive on $(0,\infty)$, is carried by those two
declarations and by \texttt{symbol\_pos} rather than by the $\exists!$ theorem's conjuncts
(fidelity review R21).}

\emph{\textbf{That second clause is the finding, and the work order had omitted it.} Forming
$\int_0^\infty \mu_t\,dt$ reads as bookkeeping and is not: \ledger{A17} supplies $\mu_t$ for each $t$
\emph{by choice, independently}, so nothing connects the choices across $t$ and the integral is
not a measure at all. The route to it is where the \emph{subordinator} structure finally does
work rather than being merely named: $\mu_{t+t'} = \mu_t * \mu_{t'}$ and causality make
$t \mapsto \mu_t(Iic\,r)$ antitone, hence measurable; the intervals form a $\pi$-system generating the
Borel sets and the $\mu_t$ are finite, so Dynkin extends measurability to every Borel set. The
increasing paths, which the prose treats as intuition, are exactly what make the potential
measure \emph{exist}. Same shape as the \texttt{IsCausal} clause that writing
\ref{thm:sonine-conservation} found: a hypothesis that reads as a formality and carries the
reachability of the statement.)}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
With $B(s) := sF'(s) \in \BF \setminus \{0\}$ by \ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}(2), we
have $\varphi_x(s) = B(xs)/x \in \BF \setminus \{0\}$, and $\varphi_x > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$ by
\ref{lem:vanishing}.

For existence, \emph{construct} the measure rather than representing it. Since $\varphi_x$ is a
Bernstein function without killing term it is the exponent of a subordinator: let
$(\mu^{(x)}_\tau)_{\tau \ge 0}$ be its laws, $\widehat{\mu^{(x)}_\tau}(s) =
e^{-\tau \varphi_x(s)}$, and set
\[
  \ell^{(x)} \;:=\; \int_0^\infty \mu^{(x)}_\tau \, d\tau,
\]
the subordinator's \emph{potential measure}. By Tonelli its transform is
$\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau\varphi_x(s)}\,d\tau = 1/\varphi_x(s)$ for every $s > 0$, which is finite,
so $\ell^{(x)}$ is locally finite; and uniqueness is uniqueness of the Laplace transform for
locally finite measures whose transform converges somewhere (\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}).

Two remarks on this route. It uses no property of $\CM$: neither
$\CM \circ \BF \subseteq \CM$ (\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(2), \ledger{A2}) nor Bernstein--Widder
in its general form (\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(1), \ledger{A1}), both of which the older
argument spent. What it does use is the existence of the subordinator, \ledger{A17}, which the
main theorem has already spent --- so the exchange is two interfaces for none. And the integral
$\int_0^\infty \mu^{(x)}_\tau\,d\tau$ is a measure only because $\tau \mapsto \mu^{(x)}_\tau$ is
measurable, which is not automatic and is where the subordinator's increasing paths are actually
used: $\mu^{(x)}_{\tau+\tau'} = \mu^{(x)}_\tau * \mu^{(x)}_{\tau'}$ with causality makes
$\tau \mapsto \mu^{(x)}_\tau\bigl((-\infty, r]\bigr)$ nonincreasing, hence measurable, and a
Dynkin argument on the generating $\pi$-system $\{(-\infty,r]\}$ extends this to every Borel set.

The scaling law follows by comparing transforms:
$\widehat{\ell^{(x)}}(s) = x/B(xs) = x\,\widehat{\ell^{(1)}}(xs)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sonine conservation]\label{thm:sonine-conservation}
\uses{lem:memory-kernel,lem:memory-kernel-transform,lem:potential-kernel,prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite}
\lean{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.sonine_conservation}\leanok
For every $x > 0$,
\[
  \kappa^{(x)} * \ell^{(x)} \;=\; \mathrm{Leb}_{[0,\infty)},
  \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad
  \int_{[0,t]} \ell^{(x)}\bigl([0,\, t - r]\bigr)\,\kappa^{(x)}(dr) \;=\; t
  \quad \text{for all } t \ge 0.
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(\textbf{Machine-checked}, as
\texttt{Hemigroup.SelfDecomposableExponent.sonine\_conservation}; \texttt{\#print axioms} reduces
to Lean core, so this half's headline crosses no interface at all. The tagged declaration states
the identity restricted to $[0,\infty)$; \texttt{sonine\_conservation'} is the unrestricted
equality of measures on $\mathbb{R}$ displayed above, the two agreeing because both factors ---
$\kappa^{(x)} * \ell^{(x)}$ and $\mathrm{Leb}_{[0,\infty)}$ --- are causal, i.e.\ vanish on
$(-\infty, 0)$ (fidelity review R23).}

\emph{\textbf{It does not wait on \ref{lem:potential-kernel}.} The Lean statement quantifies over
any causal $\ell$ meeting that lemma's specification rather than naming the $\ell^{(x)}$ it
produces, so the identity is independent of how existence is discharged --- which matters here,
because existence is the one open question of this half and the one place a third
trust-boundary entry could enter. Only \ref{prop:sonine-pair-exists} needs it. Decoupling the two
is also what exposed three hypotheses this statement had been borrowing silently: that $\ell$ is
causal, that its transform is prescribed in $[0,\infty]$ rather than as a Bochner integral
(which reads $0$, not $\infty$, when the integrand fails to be integrable), and axiom~(ND) ---
without which $\varphi_x$ vanishes, $\kappa^{(x)} = 0$, and the identity is \emph{false}. All
three are inherited in the paper by taking $(\Phi_{x,y})$ from
\ref{thm:main-characterization}; a statement that stands on its own has to say them.}

\emph{Everything the proof needs was built for other reasons, which is the one respect in which
this node was cheap: transforms multiply over convolution (chapter~4), the first factor is
\ref{lem:memory-kernel-transform}, the second is the hypothesis, and the conclusion is
\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite} --- the extension of \ledger{A6} to measures that are
not finite, which was proved in advance precisely because $\kappa^{(x)}$, $\ell^{(x)}$ and
Lebesgue measure are none of them finite and this identity compares them.)}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Both sides are locally finite measures on $[0,\infty)$ with finite Laplace transforms, and
those transforms agree:
\[
  \widehat{\kappa^{(x)}}(s)\,\widehat{\ell^{(x)}}(s)
  = \frac{\varphi_x(s)}{s}\cdot\frac{1}{\varphi_x(s)} = \frac{1}{s}
  = \widehat{\mathrm{Leb}}(s),
\]
using \ref{lem:memory-kernel} for the first factor and \ref{lem:potential-kernel} for the
second. \ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness-sigma-finite} concludes.
\end{proof}

\bigskip

Note the scaling structure: $\kappa^{(x)}$ dilates with $L^1$ normalization and $\ell^{(x)}$
with sup normalization (\ref{lem:potential-kernel}), and it is exactly this mismatch that
renders the identity scale-invariant --- the pair $(\kappa^{(x)}, \ell^{(x)})$ is itself
scale-covariant.

\begin{corollary}[exact invertibility; conservation of information]\label{cor:exact-inversion}
\uses{thm:sonine-conservation}
Define, on causal $v \in X$, the generalized derivative and integral
\[
  D^{(x)} v := \partial_t\bigl(\kappa^{(x)} * v\bigr), \qquad I^{(x)} v := \ell^{(x)} * v .
\]
Then $D^{(x)} I^{(x)} v = v$ in $\Distr$, hence a.e.
\statusT
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \ref{thm:sonine-conservation} and associativity of convolution --- Tonelli on causal
supports --- $\kappa^{(x)} * \ell^{(x)} * v = \mathrm{Leb} * v = \int_0^{\,\cdot} v(r)\,dr$,
and $\partial_t \int_0^t v = v$.
\end{proof}

\bigskip

\ref{cor:exact-inversion} is the conservation content of the axioms in Volterra form: the
infinitesimal smoothing applied at each scale is exactly invertible, so no single step of the
cascade destroys information --- it only redistributes it, consistently with (A5), which fixes
the mass. Probabilistically, $\ell^{(x)}$ is the potential (renewal) measure of the
subordinator with exponent $\varphi_x$, $\kappa^{(x)}$ is its drift-plus-Lévy-tail, and
\ref{thm:sonine-conservation} is the renewal identity balancing expected occupation against
tail decay.

\begin{proposition}[regularity of the pair]\label{prop:pair-regularity}
\uses{lem:memory-kernel,lem:memory-kernel-transform,lem:potential-kernel,thm:sonine-conservation,def:completely-monotone}
\lean{Skeleton.hasCMDensity_iff}\notready
Write $\kappa^{(x)} = b_0\,\delta_0 + \kappa_c^{(x)}$, with
$\kappa_c^{(x)}(dt) := \tfrac1x\,k\bigl(\tfrac tx\bigr)\,dt$ the absolutely continuous part of
\ref{lem:memory-kernel-transform} --- an unconditional decomposition, $k$ being nonincreasing.
The two clauses below record when $\ell^{(x)}$ admits an analogous decomposition
$\ell^{(x)} = c\,\delta_0 + \ell_c^{(x)}$, for a constant $c \ge 0$ not in general equal to
$b_0$, and describe the regularity of $\kappa_c^{(x)}$ and $\ell_c^{(x)}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ell^{(x)}$ admits such a decomposition with $\ell_c^{(x)}$ a nonincreasing density if
and only if $1/F'$ is a Bernstein function --- equivalently, $\varphi_x$ is a \emph{special}
Bernstein function; by dilation invariance the condition does not depend on $x$.
\item $\kappa_c^{(x)}$ is completely monotone iff $k$ is completely monotone, which holds iff
$F'$ belongs to the Stieltjes class
$\mathcal{S} := \bigl\{\,h(s) = \tfrac{a}{s} + b + \int_{(0,\infty)} \tfrac{\sigma(d\tau)}{s + \tau}\,\bigr\}$.
In that case $\ell^{(x)}$ also admits the decomposition above with $\ell_c^{(x)}$ completely
monotone, i.e.\ the absolutely continuous parts $(\kappa_c^{(x)}, \ell_c^{(x)})$ form a
classical Sonine pair of CM kernels; moreover $k$ completely monotone implies $F$ is a complete
Bernstein function.
\end{enumerate}
\statusA\quad\ledger{A9}\quad\emph{(special Bernstein functions, the Stieltjes class and
complete Bernstein functions; \sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Thm.~11.3, Thm.~7.3 and
Thm.~6.2, with $\mathcal{S}$ defined in Ch.~2. Cited, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} Clause~(1) is carried by \ledger{A9} \emph{entire}: the characterization of
the potential measures of special subordinators is SSV~Thm.~11.3 and is not proved here. Within
clause~(2) the ledger carries the two structural inputs --- that $1/h \in \CBF$ for
$h \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}$ (Thm.~7.3), and that a complete Bernstein function has a
completely monotone Lévy density (Thm.~6.2) --- while the computations joining them are
\textbf{[T]} and given below. The existence clause, which needs no ledger entry at all, was
split off as \ref{prop:sonine-pair-exists}; before the split this node reported A9's cost for a
restatement of three proved results. Note the edition hazard recorded in the ledger: SSV's
Thm.~11.3 is Thm.~10.3 in the first edition, a chapter having been inserted at position~10 in
the second.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the cited characterization of potential measures of special subordinators.

(2) Dilation preserves complete monotonicity, so the first equivalence reduces to a statement
about $k$. If $k$ is completely monotone then $k(t) = \int e^{-\tau t}\sigma(d\tau)$ for a
positive measure $\sigma$, and \ref{lem:memory-kernel} gives
$F'(s) = b_0 + \int \sigma(d\tau)/(s+\tau) \in \mathcal{S}$. Conversely, an
$\mathcal{S}$-representation of $F'$ must have $a = 0$ --- an $a/s$ term corresponds to an
additive constant in $k$, contradicting $\int_1^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$ --- and then
$k(t) = \int e^{-\tau t}\sigma(d\tau)$ is completely monotone.

For the second assertion, $1/h \in \CBF$ for $h \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}$, so
$g := 1/F'(x\,\cdot) \in \CBF$ and $\widehat{\ell^{(x)}}(s) = g(s)/s$. Writing the
Lévy--Khintchine triple of $g$ as $(a_g, b_g, \nu_g)$, with completely monotone Lévy density
because $g \in \CBF$, gives
$g(s)/s = \tfrac{a_g}{s} + b_g + \widehat{\bar\nu_g}(s)$, that is
$\ell^{(x)} = b_g\,\delta_0 + \ell_c^{(x)}$ with
$\ell_c^{(x)}(t) := a_g + \bar\nu_g(t)$; and
$a_g + \bar\nu_g(t) = a_g + \int_t^\infty(\text{completely monotone})$ is completely monotone.

Finally, $k$ completely monotone implies the Lévy density $k(t)/t$ of $F$ is completely
monotone, being a product of completely monotone functions, hence $F \in \CBF$.
\end{proof}

Part (2) delineates exactly where the classical Sonine calculus with completely monotone
kernel pairs --- the standing hypothesis of most of the general-fractional-calculus literature
--- applies: on the complete-Bernstein subfamily, which Chapter~10 identifies with the
locality corner of the memory-line realization. For general admissible $F$ the pair, and
\ref{thm:sonine-conservation}, live at the level of measures.

  \qquad \theta_x(dt) := b_0\,x\,\delta_0(dt) + k\bigl(\tfrac{t}{x}\bigr)\,dt. \tag{V.1}
\]

\bigskip

Equation (V.2) is the constructive counterpart of the inverse Laplace transform in
\ref{thm:main-characterization}: the kernels are \emph{derived} from the memory kernel $k$ by
Volterra integration. We record here, without a formal statement, why this determines $\phi_x$
rather than proving it: on $(b_0x, \infty)$, where $\phi_x$ is supported, (V.2) is linear and
homogeneous, and a \emph{regular} equation of that form --- continuous coefficient, bounded
away from zero --- has the trivial solution only; it is the vanishing of the coefficient
$t - b_0x$ exactly at the left endpoint of that interval that admits a nontrivial family
instead, from which the unit-area axiom (A5) selects $\phi_x$. Positivity of that choice is
the property Picard iteration of (V.2) preserves term by term, starting from a nonnegative
seed, on the interior where the equation is regular.

\begin{example}[verification: the Gamma family]\label{ex:gamma-volterra}
\uses{prop:gamma-family,prop:volterra}
For $F(s) = \gamma\log(1+s)$ we have $b_0 = 0$, $k(u) = \gamma e^{-u}$, and
$\phi_x(t) = t^{\gamma-1}e^{-t/x}/(\Gamma(\gamma)x^\gamma)$ by \ref{prop:gamma-family}.
Indeed, since $e^{-u/x}e^{-(t-u)/x} = e^{-t/x}$,
\[
  \int_0^t \gamma e^{-u/x}\,\frac{(t-u)^{\gamma-1} e^{-(t-u)/x}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\,x^{\gamma}}\,du
  \;=\; \frac{\gamma\, e^{-t/x}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\,x^{\gamma}} \cdot \frac{t^{\gamma}}{\gamma}
  \;=\; t\,\phi_x(t),
\]
which is (V.2) for this family, as required.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[computation; relation to the 2005 series]\label{rem:volterra-computation}
\uses{prop:volterra,prop:stable-family}
For $F(s) = s^\alpha$, i.e.\ $k(u) = \alpha u^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)$, Picard iteration of
(V.2) regenerates term by term the series expansion following Theorem~2 of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}; the Volterra route thus contains the original series as its
homogeneous special case. Numerically, (V.2) is a weakly singular Volterra equation of the
second kind, amenable to standard product-integration quadrature, and it provides an
inversion-free forward scheme for the kernels for arbitrary admissible $k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[literature]\label{rem:sonine-literature}
\uses{prop:pair-regularity,thm:sonine-conservation}
Kernel pairs satisfying $\kappa * \ell \equiv 1$ go back to \sscite{sonine1884sur}; the
operator calculus built on them is the general fractional calculus of
\sscite{kochubei2011general} and \sscite{luchko2021general}, usually developed under
completely monotone or Sonine-class kernel hypotheses, which by
\ref{prop:pair-regularity}(2) delineate the complete-Bernstein subfamily. In
Volterra-equation theory the kernels $\kappa^{(x)}$ are the \emph{completely positive} kernels
of \sscite{clement1981asymptotic}; the resolvent theory of \sscite{pruss1993evolutionary} then
yields well-posedness and positivity of the evolution in \ref{prop:scale-evolution} by purely
deterministic arguments. The integral equation (V.1) is the classical characterization of
infinitely divisible laws on the half-line of \sscite{steutel2004infinite}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[the Volterra equation for the density]\label{prop:volterra-density}
\uses{prop:volterra,rem:sato-reading,rem:drift-boundary}
Let $F$ be as in (7.1), let $x > 0$, and set $\mu_x := \mu_{0,x}$. If $k \not\equiv 0$ then
$\mu_x$ is absolutely continuous, and its density $\phi_x$ solves the linear Volterra equation
\[
  \bigl(t - b_0 x\bigr)\,\phi_x(t)
  \;=\; \int_0^t k\bigl(\tfrac{u}{x}\bigr)\,\phi_x(t - u)\,du
  \qquad \text{for a.e.\ } t > 0. \tag{V.2}
\]
Away from its left endpoint $t = b_0 x$ --- where the \emph{coefficient} $t - b_0 x$
vanishes; $\phi_x$ vanishes below it, the drift shifting every kernel by $b_0 x$
(\ref{rem:drift-boundary}), but need not vanish at it --- this is a Volterra equation of the
second kind.
\statusA\quad\ledger{A10}\quad\emph{(absolute continuity of nondegenerate self-decomposable
laws; \sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~27.13. Cited, not reproven here.
\textbf{Assignment.} \ledger{A10} carries absolute continuity and nothing else. Given it, the
descent from the measure identity (V.1) of \ref{prop:volterra} to the density equation (V.2) is
restriction to densities and is \textbf{[T]}. (V.2) is the point of the pair of propositions,
being the inversion-free forward scheme; the hypothesis $k \not\equiv 0$ is nondegeneracy, and
excluding the degenerate case --- the pure drift $F(s) = b_0 s$, whose kernel is the atom
$\delta_{b_0 x}$ --- is ours, not the citation's.)}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $k \not\equiv 0$, $\mu_x$ is a nondegenerate self-decomposable law by
\ref{rem:sato-reading}, and \ledger{A10} makes it absolutely continuous. Restricting the
identity (V.1) of \ref{prop:volterra} to densities yields (V.2).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the transform equation]\label{lem:transform-ode}
\uses{thm:main-characterization,prop:bernstein-toolbox}

  \partial_x \hat u(s, x) \;=\; -\,s\,F'(xs)\,\hat u(s, x).
\]
\statusT\quad\emph{(the chain rule, $F$ being smooth on $(0,\infty)$ by
\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(3). Numbered 9.14, a number the draft does not use. It is stated on

\end{proof}
