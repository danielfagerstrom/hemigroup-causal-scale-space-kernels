\section{Examples and moments}\label{sec:examples}

Throughout this section we work in the canonical gauge, so that the kernel at scale $x$
is $\mu_{0,x} = \Lap^{-1}[e^{-F(x s)}]$, with density $\phi_x$ where it has one, and $T_x$
denotes a random variable with law $\mu_{0,x}$ --- the delay at scale $x$, so that
$T_x \stackrel{d}{=} xT_1$. The section does three things. It states once the one criterion by
which every family below is admissible, and its converse, which makes the two integrability
conditions of (7.1) characteristic of the class rather than an artifact of an estimate. It
gives the mean delay and the moment criterion, the two quantities by which the families are
then compared. And it works the three families the rest of the paper uses, each the worked
case of one of the slices in Figure~\ref{fig:admissible-cone}: the stable family, cut by
dilation symmetry, which is the semigroup theory; the Gamma family, the atoms of the Thorin
representation, which is what \S\ref{sec:implementation} implements; and the Bessel family,
cut by locality, which is the subject of \S\S\ref{sec:signaling}--\ref{sec:locality}.

% shared with blueprint prop:admissibility-criterion
\begin{proposition}[admissibility criterion for a memory kernel]\label{prop:admissibility-criterion}
Let $b_0 \ge 0$ and let $k : (0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ be nonincreasing with
\[
  \int_0^1 k(t)\,dt < \infty
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \int_1^\infty \frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt < \infty .
\]
Then $F(s) = b_0 s + \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,k(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}$ is finite for every
$s \ge 0$.

Consequently $F$ is of the form (7.1), and if $F \not\equiv 0$ Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}
applies to it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split the integral at $t = 1$. On $(0,1]$ use $1 - e^{-st} \le st$, which is $1 + x \le e^x$ at
$x = -st$; the integrand is then at most $s\,k(t)$, integrable by the first hypothesis. On
$(1,\infty)$ use $1 - e^{-st} \le 1$; the integrand is at most $k(t)/t$, integrable by the
second. The drift term is finite for every real $s$.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint lem:criterion-converse
\begin{lemma}[the criterion is characteristic]\label{lem:criterion-converse}
Let $b_0 \ge 0$ and let $k : (0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ be nonincreasing. If the $[0,\infty]$-valued
exponent (7.1) at $s = 1$,
\[
  b_0 + \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-t})\,k(t)\,\frac{dt}{t} ,
\]
is finite, then $\int_0^1 k(t)\,dt < \infty$ and $\int_1^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $s = 1$ in (7.1), so that $\int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-t})k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$. For $t > 0$,
$1 - e^{-t} \ge t/(1+t)$, so the integrand is at least $k(t)/(1+t)$. On $(0,1]$ that is at least
$k(t)/2$, and on $(1,\infty)$ it is at least $k(t)/(2t)$; both claims follow.
\end{proof}

In particular every admissible $F$ carries both integrability facts already, so nothing below
assumes regularity of $k$ beyond the definition.

The first quantity to compare families by is the mean delay.

% shared with blueprint prop:moments
\begin{proposition}[the mean delay and the influence curve]\label{prop:moments}
$\Ex\,T_x = x\,F'(\zp) = x\bigl(b_0 + \int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt\bigr)$, finite if and only if $k$
is integrable at infinity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the transform at the origin,
$\Ex\,T_x = -\partial_s e^{-F(xs)}\big|_{s=\zp} = x F'(\zp)$, and monotone convergence in
(7.1) gives $F'(\zp) = b_0 + \int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt$, finite exactly when $k$ is integrable at
infinity.
\end{proof}

The \emph{influence curve} of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S8, p.~106, is the
distance $t_0 - t_m$ between the time $t_0$ at which a measurement is made and the time
$t_m$ with the largest influence on it, as a function of scale. There $t_m$ had to be the
mode of the kernel --- whose scaling is Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-mode} --- the mean of the stable kernels being infinite (\emph{ibid.}, p.~102).
Whenever the mean is finite it can be taken as $t_0 - t_m$ instead, and the proposition
says that in the canonical gauge the influence curve is then exactly linear,
$t_0 - t_m = \Ex\,T_x = F'(\zp)\,x$. The
condition "$k$ integrable at infinity" is the case $n = 1$ of the general criterion, which
runs through one cited equivalence.

% shared with blueprint prop:moment-criterion
\begin{proposition}[the moment criterion]\label{prop:moment-criterion}
$\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} < \infty$ if and only if $\int_1^\infty t^{\,n-1} k(t)\,dt < \infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Lévy measure of $T_x$ is $\nu(dt) = k(t)t^{-1}dt$ up to the scaling by $x$, so
$\int_{t>1} t^n\,\nu(dt) = \int_1^\infty t^{\,n-1}k(t)\,dt$; the moment criterion for
infinitely divisible laws (\sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~25.3) equates finiteness of that
integral with finiteness of $\Ex\,T_x^n$.
\end{proof}

\emph{The stable family} is the semigroup theory seen from inside the cone: $F(s) = s^\alpha$
is the only exponent, up to scale, that is homogeneous, and its $k$ is a pure power.

% shared with blueprint prop:stable-family
\begin{proposition}[the extremal stable family: the exponent]\label{prop:stable-family}
For $0 < \alpha < 1$ the exponent $F(s) = s^\alpha$ is of the form (7.1), with Lévy density
factor
\[
  k(t) = \frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\, t^{-\alpha},
\]
which is nonincreasing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$s^\alpha = \frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,t^{-\alpha-1}\,dt$ is
the standard integral representation, which is (7.1) with $b_0 = 0$ and
$k(t)/t = \alpha t^{-\alpha-1}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)$; $k$ is a negative power, hence
nonincreasing, and both integrability conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion}
hold ($t^{-\alpha}$ integrable at $0$, $t^{-\alpha-1}$ integrable at infinity), so $F$ is finite
and of the form (7.1).
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:stable-moments
\begin{proposition}[the extremal stable family: every moment is infinite]\label{prop:stable-moments}
With $F$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-family}, $\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} = \infty$ for every $n \ge 1$
and every $x > 0$, so the delay of the 2005 stable kernels cannot be measured by any moment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} the mean delay is $\Ex\,T_x = x\bigl(b_0 + \int_0^\infty
k\bigr)$, finite exactly when $k$ is integrable at infinity; here $b_0 = 0$ and
$k(t) = \alpha t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1 - \alpha)$, which is not integrable at infinity for
$\alpha < 1$. So $\Ex\,T_x = \infty$. Every higher moment follows without a further criterion:
$t \le 1 + t^{\,n}$ for $t \ge 0$ and $n \ge 1$, so on the probability measure $\mu_{0,x}$ we get
$\Ex\,T_x \le 1 + \Ex\,T_x^{\,n}$, and a divergent mean forces $\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} = \infty$.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:stable-mode
\begin{proposition}[the extremal stable family: the mode]\label{prop:stable-mode}
With $F$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-family}, $T_x$ is unimodal, and its mode is $x$ times
the mode of $T_1$ in the canonical gauge; in the parametrization of the one-parameter family of
Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case}, where $\chi(x) = x^{1/\alpha}$, it scales as $x^{1/\alpha}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:extreme-rays}, $T_x$ is unimodal, since $k \not\equiv 0$ for the stable family.
In the canonical gauge $\mu_{0,x}$ is the law of $xT_1$, so its mode is $x$ times the mode of
$T_1$. The self-similarity is the case $\chi(x) = x^{1/\alpha}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case},
and the mode of $x^{1/\alpha}T_1$ is $x^{1/\alpha}$ times the mode of $T_1$.
\end{proof}

\emph{The Gamma family} is the one the introduction started from. Its $k$ is an exponential
--- bounded, with every moment finite --- and its kernels are the Gamma densities, which is
what makes it implementable by a cascade of first-order filters.

% shared with blueprint prop:gamma-family
\begin{proposition}[the Gamma family: the exponent]\label{prop:gamma-family}
For $\gamma > 0$ the exponent $F(s) = \gamma\,\log(1 + s)$ is of the form (7.1) with
$k(t) = \gamma\, e^{-t}$, which is nonincreasing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Frullani integral gives
$\log(1+s) = \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-st})\,e^{-t}\,t^{-1}\,dt$, so $F$ has the form (7.1) with
$b_0 = 0$ and $k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$, nonincreasing, and both integrability conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} hold ($e^{-t}$ bounded near $0$, integrable at
infinity), so $F$ is finite and of the form (7.1); hence $F$ is admissible by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:gamma-kernels
\begin{proposition}[the Gamma family: the transforms and the mean]\label{prop:gamma-kernels}
With $F$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family}, the kernels and the increment measures have
transforms
\[
  e^{-F(xs)} = (1 + x s)^{-\gamma}, \qquad
  e^{-g_{a,b}(s)} = \Bigl(\tfrac{1 + as}{1 + bs}\Bigr)^{\gamma},
\]
and the mean delay is $\Ex\,T_x = \gamma x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F(s) = \gamma\log(1+s)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family}, so $e^{-F(xs)} =
(1+xs)^{-\gamma}$; and $g_{a,b} = F(b\cdot) - F(a\cdot)$, so the increment transform is the ratio
$e^{-F(bs)}/e^{-F(as)}$, which is the stated one. For the mean, Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}
gives $\Ex\,T_x = x\bigl(b_0 + \int_0^\infty k\bigr)$ with $b_0 = 0$ and $k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$,
whose integral is $\gamma$.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:gamma-density
\begin{proposition}[the Gamma family: the kernel density]\label{prop:gamma-density}
With $F$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family} and $\gamma > 0$,
\[
  \phi_x(t) = \frac{t^{\gamma - 1} e^{-t/x}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\, x^{\gamma}},
\]
the Gamma density of fixed shape $\gamma$ and scale $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(1+xs)^{-\gamma}$ is the Laplace transform of the stated Gamma density --- the Gamma integral
$\int_0^\infty t^{\gamma-1}e^{-(1/x + s)t}\,dt = \Gamma(\gamma)/(1/x + s)^{\gamma}$ --- so
Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness} identifies $\phi_x$.
\end{proof}

% Verbatim from the blueprint's rem:gamma-sato; not shared, since remarks are not shareable.
\begin{remark}[the Gamma family as a Sato subordinator]\label{rem:gamma-sato}
The increment measures $\mu_{a,b}$ of this family are the increments of the Gamma-type Sato
subordinator. This is Remark~\ref{rem:sato-reading} read at the Gamma exponent.
\end{remark}

% shared with blueprint prop:gamma-moments
\begin{proposition}[the Gamma family: the higher moments]\label{prop:gamma-moments}
With $F$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family}, every moment of $T_x$ is finite, and
$\operatorname{Var} T_x = \gamma x^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-density} the law of $T_x$ is the Gamma law of shape $\gamma$ and
rate $1/x$, so $\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} = \Gamma(\gamma+n)x^{n}/\Gamma(\gamma)$, finite for every $n$. In
particular $\Ex\,T_x = \gamma x$ and $\Ex\,T_x^{2} = \gamma(\gamma+1)x^2$, so
$\operatorname{Var} T_x = \gamma(\gamma+1)x^2 - \gamma^2x^2 = \gamma x^2$.
\end{proof}

Two facts about the Gamma family belong here although they are proved later. It is the
family \S\ref{sec:implementation} implements, exactly, by first-order recursive sections, one
per unit of shape. And the memory-line development of \S\ref{sec:signaling} needs more of it
than admissibility: the standing hypothesis stated there requires a negative moment
$\Ex\,T_1^{-\zeta} < \infty$ of some order $\zeta > 1$, which for a Gamma law of shape $\gamma$
holds exactly when $\gamma > 1$. The propositions above hold for every $\gamma > 0$; the
memory line uses $\gamma > 1$, and $\gamma \le 1$ is listed among the open problems in
\S\ref{sec:conclusions}.

\emph{The Bessel family} is cut by the third principle, locality of the memory-line
evolution, and it is the family on which the second half of the paper is worked. It is
introduced here by its law and transform; what it is the kernel \emph{of} is the subject of
\S\S\ref{sec:signaling}--\ref{sec:locality}.

\begin{proposition}[the Bessel-K family; preview of the memory line]\label{prop:bessel-family}
For $a > 0$ let $T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(2\gamma_a)$ be the scaled inverse-gamma law, where
$\gamma_a$ has the Gamma density of shape $a$, and let $F$ be its Laplace exponent. Then
\[
  e^{-F(s)} = \frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma(a)}\,\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)^{a} K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr),
\]
and $T_1$ is self-decomposable, so $F$ is of the form (7.1) and the family is admissible. The
moments satisfy $\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ if and only if $a > n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\gamma_a$ for a Gamma$(a,1)$ variable and $T_1 = 1/(2\gamma_a)$. Substituting
$u = 1/(2t)$ in the Gamma density gives the density of $T_1$,
\[
  \phi_1(t) = \frac{t^{-a-1}\,e^{-1/(2t)}}{2^{a}\,\Gamma(a)}, \qquad t > 0 .
\]
The classical integral representation
$\int_0^\infty t^{\nu-1}\exp(-A/t - Bt)\,dt = 2\,(A/B)^{\nu/2}K_\nu\bigl(2\sqrt{AB}\bigr)$,
taken at $\nu = -a$, $A = \tfrac12$, $B = s$, together with $K_{-a} = K_a$, gives
\[
  \Ex\,e^{-sT_1}
  = \frac{1}{2^{a}\Gamma(a)}\int_0^\infty t^{-a-1}e^{-st - 1/(2t)}\,dt
  = \frac{2\,(2s)^{a/2}\,K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)}{2^{a}\Gamma(a)}
  = \frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma(a)}\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr)^{a}K_a\bigl(\sqrt{2s}\bigr).
\]
Self-decomposability of $T_1$ is a theorem of Halgreen (\sscite{halgreen1979self}, for the
generalized inverse Gaussian laws, of which this is a limiting case); by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} its exponent is of the form (7.1), and both
integrability conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion} hold for the resulting
$(b_0,k)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion-converse}, since $F(1) = -\log\Ex\,e^{-T_1} < \infty$; so
$F$ is finite and Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} makes the family admissible. Finally,
the Lévy density factor of this $F$ satisfies $k(t) \sim a$ as $t \to 0$ and decays like $t^{-a}$
at infinity, so $\int_1^\infty t^{n-1}k(t)\,dt < \infty$ exactly when $n < a$;
Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-criterion} converts that into $\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ iff $a > n$.
\end{proof}

In the parabolic gauge $\xi$ of Remark~\ref{rem:gauge-freedom}, $\tilde x = \xi^2$, these
kernels are the laws of the first hitting time of the boundary $\xi = 0$ by the Bessel-type
diffusion with generator $\tfrac12\partial_\xi^2 + \tfrac{\beta}{\xi}\partial_\xi$,
$a = \tfrac12 - \beta$, started at $\xi$; that identification is proved in
Example~\ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}, where it appears as the statement that the family's
signaling form is local, and \S\ref{sec:locality} shows it is the only admissible family with
that property. Figure~\ref{fig:families} shows the family across $a$: the tails are power laws
of exponent $-(a+1)$, so the shape parameter is a dial on the moments, and $a = \tfrac12$ is
the stable member.

\begin{remark}[heavy tails are the semigroup's doing]\label{rem:heavy-tails}
The heavy tails of the 2005 kernels are an artifact of the semigroup axiom, not of causality
together with covariance. Within Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} the semigroup sub-case
(Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case}) is the subset with $k$ a pure power $c\,t^{-\alpha}$,
$0 < \alpha < 1$, or the pure delay, $k \equiv 0$ with $b_0 > 0$ --- the only data compatible
with homogeneity --- and a pure power is never integrable at infinity, so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} no stable member has a mean (Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-moments}). Any other nonincreasing $k$ is
admitted, and its moments are governed by Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-criterion} alone:
$\Ex\,T_x^n < \infty$ exactly when $\int_1^\infty t^{n-1}k(t)\,dt < \infty$. For
$k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$ every moment is finite (Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-moments}); for
the Bessel family with shape $a$ exactly the moments of order below $a$ are finite
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}). The comparison at matched delay is drawn in
Figure~\ref{fig:kernels}.
\end{remark}

% both as recalled; the asymptotic reading -log P/(r log r) -> 1/c is the squeeze of
% (i) against (ii), a corollary rather than a verbatim line of the book. The floor

% unbounded support at every a > 0); on the drift ray Remark 26.3's Gaussian tail is
% lighter, so the qualifier is necessary.
\begin{remark}[the tail dichotomy: how light can an aperture be?]\label{rem:tail-dichotomy}
The moment criterion (Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-criterion}) reads the
\emph{polynomial} weight of a kernel's tail off the tail of $k$. The
super-exponential end is governed by a theorem that needs only infinite divisibility
--- axioms (A1)--(A7), no covariance --- and so binds every stratum at once
(\sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~26.1, p.~168): off the drift ray, a kernel's tail
satisfies $\mu_{0,x}\bigl((r,\infty)\bigr) \ge e^{-c\,r\log r}$ for some $c$ and all
large $r$. The mechanism is the cascade itself: the delay is assembled from
independent increments, and independent increments can always conspire --- $n$
repetitions of one available delay cost only the Poisson probability
$\lambda^n/n! \approx e^{-n\log n}$ --- so no admissible tail can be suppressed
faster than $e^{-c\,r\log r}$. Consequently no kernel of compact support and none
with Gaussian-or-lighter tail belongs to any causal cascade family: apart from the
pure delay, every admissible smoother has infinite impulse response, and truncation
is a violation of the cascade, not an implementation detail.

The theorem is two-sided, and the dichotomy ranks the families. If the Lévy support
is \emph{bounded} --- $k$ vanishing beyond a cutoff $\tau$ --- the floor is
attained: $-\log \mu_{0,x}\bigl((r,\infty)\bigr) \sim (r/\tau x)\log r$. If it is
\emph{unbounded}, the tail is strictly heavier than every bounded-support rate. A
completely monotone $k$ is strictly positive on all of $(0,\infty)$, so the entire
Thorin subclass sits on the heavy side of the dichotomy, the Gamma family's plain
exponential tail being typical; the bounded-support members --- the Dickman rays of
Remark~\ref{rem:extreme-rays} and their superpositions --- attain the lightest
tails the axioms permit and, their $k$ being compactly supported, have all moments
finite as well: the extreme rays of the cone are its best-localized members. The
trade-off is legible in the kernel $k$ itself: a hard cutoff of long delays buys
super-exponential tails and a full set of moments at the price of the echo
signatures of Remark~\ref{rem:extreme-rays}, unattainable within the Thorin
subclass --- the delay-equation dynamics behind them are developed in a companion
note; a completely monotone $k$ buys echo-free kernels realizable by
first-order sections (\S\ref{sec:implementation}) at the price of a tail no lighter
than exponential. Both dials --- the polynomial one of
Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-criterion} and the super-exponential one here --- are
turned by the same object, the tail of $k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the classical catalogue]\label{rem:classical-catalogue}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} is a machine: any single self-decomposable
law on $[0,\infty)$, with Laplace exponent $F$, generates a complete time-causal
scale space $\phi_x = \Lap^{-1}[e^{-F(xs)}]$. The three families above are
therefore worked representatives, not an enumeration; the classical catalogue of
class~$L$ supplies more, each with its own physiognomy as a family of apertures.
The \emph{generalized inverse Gaussian} laws are self-decomposable in their
entirety (\sscite{halgreen1979self} --- the citation
Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family} already rests on, the Bessel family being the
inverse-Gamma member); the inverse
Gaussian member has the closed-form exponent $F(s) = \sqrt{1 + 2s} - 1$, the
first-passage time of a drifting Brownian motion, so its kernels are hitting-time
delays with all moments finite. The \emph{lognormal} law is admissible: Thorin
proved its infinite divisibility by exhibiting it as a generalized Gamma
convolution (\sscite{thorin1977lognormal}) --- the theorem for which the Thorin
class was created --- giving kernels whose delay is a product of many independent
small factors, multiplicative rather than additive accumulation of latency. The
\emph{Pareto} laws are admissible by the companion result
(\sscite{thorin1977pareto}); a Pareto kernel of index $a$ has exactly the moments
of order below $a$, the Bessel family's position on the moment dial with an exact
power tail. The \emph{Mittag-Leffler} laws are admissible: the law with transform
$(1 + s^{\alpha})^{-\gamma}$, $0 < \alpha < 1$ --- the delay $Y^{1/\alpha}X_\alpha$
of a Gamma variable's power times an independent stable factor --- is a generalized
Gamma convolution with explicit Thorin density
(\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}, Ex.~3.2.4, p.~38, where the object appears
unnamed), interpolating the Gamma and stable corners on one dial. The class is
closed under a multiplicative calculus of delays: the pdf of
a ratio of independent Gamma variables is hyperbolically completely monotone
(\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}, Ex.~4.3.1, p.~59), as is any power $X^{q}$,
$|q| \ge 1$, of a Gamma variable (Ex.~4.3.4, p.~60), and $\HCM$ laws are $\GGC$
(Thm.~4.3.1, p.~57), with closure under independent products and ratios
(Thm.~5.1.1, p.~69; Thm.~6.2.1, p.~92) --- so compound latencies assembled
multiplicatively from Gamma factors stay admissible. And the phenomenon behind the
Bessel family is general: the first-passage time of \emph{every} one-dimensional
diffusion is a generalized convolution of mixtures of exponentials
(\sscite{kent1982spectral}, p.~207, the completely monotone L\'evy density being
Definition~2.1 there, p.~208) --- every diffusion hitting time lies in the Thorin
subclass, and the memory-line reading of \S\ref{sec:signaling} applies to each. Two boundaries frame the catalogue. Outward: nothing with tail lighter
than $e^{-c\,t\log t}$ is admissible at all (Remark~\ref{rem:tail-dichotomy}), so
no windowed or truncated Gaussian appears however the data $(b_0,k)$ are chosen.
Inward: every law just named lies in the Thorin subclass --- the classical
catalogue is classical largely because completely monotone structure is where the
classical proofs live --- while the admissible class is strictly larger. Its
non-Thorin territory, with plateaus, kinks and cutoffs in $k$, is new ground as a
source of scale spaces; the Dickman family of Remark~\ref{rem:extreme-rays} is its
paradigm, and a companion note develops its theory --- the delay-equation
dynamics, the echo structure, and the delay-line implementation that replaces the
first-order cascades of \S\ref{sec:implementation} outside the Thorin subclass.
\end{remark}
