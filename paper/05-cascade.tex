\section{The cascade: additivity and infinite divisibility}\label{sec:cascade}

With the representation in hand, the cascade axiom (A6) becomes a statement about
kernels: composition of operators is convolution of measures, so the exponent of
\S\ref{sec:representation} is \emph{additive} over adjacent intervals of the scale axis. Set
\[
  G(x, s) := g_{0,x}(s),
\]
the exponent accumulated from scale $0$ to scale $x$. The section shows three things about
it: that the exponent of any increment is a difference of its values,
$g_{x,y} = G(y,\cdot) - G(x,\cdot)$, so that $G(\cdot, s)$ is nondecreasing; that every such
increment is a Bernstein function, which is the hemigroup form of infinite divisibility; and
that, under (ND), $G(\cdot, s)$ is strictly increasing --- so that
it is a coordinate on the scale axis, which is what \S\ref{sec:covariance} will use to
straighten that axis. No covariance is used anywhere in this section: everything below holds
for every family satisfying (A1)--(A7), and the stratification of \S\ref{sec:conclusions}
depends on exactly that.

% shared with blueprint lem:additivity
\begin{lemma}[additivity]\label{lem:additivity}
Under (A1)--(A7), $\mu_{x,z} = \mu_{y,z} * \mu_{x,y}$ for $x \le y \le z$, hence
\[
  g_{x,z}(s) = g_{x,y}(s) + g_{y,z}(s), \qquad g_{x,x} = 0.
\]
Consequently, with $G(x, s) := g_{0,x}(s)$,
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) = G(y, s) - G(x, s),
\]
where $G(0, \cdot) = 0$, $G(\cdot, s)$ is nondecreasing and continuous for each $s$, and
$G(x, 0) = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from (A6), Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation} (under which composition of
operators corresponds to convolution of measures), and Lemma~\ref{lem:transform-continuity}. In
detail: (A6) gives $\Phi_{y,z}\Phi_{x,y} = \Phi_{x,z}$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation} reads $\mu_{y,z} * (\mu_{x,y} * f) = \mu_{x,z} * f$
for all $f \in X$, so $\mu_{x,z} = \mu_{y,z} * \mu_{x,y}$ by the uniqueness clause there. Taking
Laplace transforms turns convolution into multiplication and hence $-\log$ into addition ---
legitimate because $\hat\mu(s) > 0$, which is the first clause of
Lemma~\ref{lem:transform-continuity} --- giving the additivity of $g$;
$\Phi_{x,x} = \Id$ gives $\mu_{x,x} = \delta_0$ and so
$g_{x,x} = 0$. Setting $x = 0$ defines $G$, and additivity gives
$g_{x,y} = G(y,\cdot) - G(x,\cdot)$. Monotonicity in the first argument is the nonnegativity
of $g_{x,y}$, continuity is Lemma~\ref{lem:transform-continuity}, and $G(x,0) = 0$ is the
corresponding clause there.
\end{proof}

The next step upgrades the increments from ``exponents of probability measures'' to Bernstein
functions. In the semigroup case this is trivial, since there $\mu_\tau = \mu_{\tau/n}^{*n}$
exhibits every kernel as an $n$-fold convolution power and infinite divisibility is immediate.
In the hemigroup case there is no such power: the increments over a partition need not be
equal, or even comparable, and what replaces the argument is a null-array limit. On the half
line it can be made elementary, and the proof below is the whole of it.

% shared with blueprint thm:increments-bernstein
\begin{theorem}[increments are Bernstein]\label{thm:increments-bernstein}
Under (A1)--(A7), $g_{x,y} \in \BFz$ for every $x \le y$. Equivalently, and this is the form the
proof produces: $g_{x,y} \in \LE$, i.e.\ there are a drift $b_0(x,y) \ge 0$ and a Lévy measure
$\nu_{x,y}$ on $(0,\infty)$ with
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) = b_0(x,y)\,s + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\nu_{x,y}(dt).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x < y$ and the uniform partition $t_i = x + \tfrac{i}{n}(y - x)$, $i = 0, \dots, n$.
Write $\mu_i := \mu_{t_i, t_{i+1}}$ and $g_i := g_{t_i, t_{i+1}}$, so that by
Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity}
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} g_i(s) \qquad \text{for every } n.
\]

Fix $s > 0$. Since $G(\cdot, s)$ is continuous on the compact interval $[x, y]$ it is
uniformly continuous there, so $m_n := \max_i g_i(s) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Using
$0 \le u - (1 - e^{-u}) \le u^2/2$ for $u \ge 0$,
\[
  \Bigl|\, g_{x,y}(s) - \sum_{i}\bigl(1 - e^{-g_i(s)}\bigr) \Bigr|
  \;\le\; \tfrac12 \sum_i g_i(s)^2
  \;\le\; \tfrac{m_n}{2}\, g_{x,y}(s)
  \;\longrightarrow\; 0 .
\]

On the other hand, with $\Pi_n := \sum_i \mu_i \in \Mplus([0,\infty))$, a finite measure,
\[
  \sum_i \bigl(1 - e^{-g_i(s)}\bigr) = \sum_i \bigl(1 - \hat\mu_i(s)\bigr)
  = \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\Pi_n(dt),
\]
which is a Bernstein function of $s$ vanishing at $\zp$ --- it is in Lévy--Khintchine form
with finite Lévy measure $\Pi_n$ and no drift or killing term.

It remains to pass to the limit, and the point is to extract the \emph{representation} rather
than to invoke closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits. The total masses $\Pi_n([0,\infty)) = n$
diverge, because mass piles up at the origin --- and that pile \emph{is} the drift. So weight the
measures by $1 - e^{-t}$, which vanishes at the origin at exactly the rate that compensates, and
substitute $v = 1 - e^{-t}$, carrying $[0,\infty]$ onto the compact $[0,1]$ with
$\infty \mapsto 1$. Weighting $\mu_i$ by $1 - e^{-t}$ and integrating gives $1 - \hat\mu_i(1)$, so
the weighted measures have total mass
\[
  \sum_i \bigl(1 - \hat\mu_i(1)\bigr) \;=\; \sum_i \bigl(1 - e^{-g_i(1)}\bigr)
  \;\le\; \sum_i g_i(1) \;=\; g_{x,y}(1),
\]
using $1 - e^{-u} \le u$ for $u \ge 0$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity} at $s = 1$; this bound is
uniform in $n$. So the weighted measures live on the fixed compact $[0,1]$ with uniformly bounded
mass, and a subsequence converges weakly, with no further tightness argument needed. Integrating
against
\[
  k_s(v) \;=\; \frac{1 - (1-v)^s}{v},
\]
which has a removable singularity at $v = 0$ with value $s$, gives
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) \;=\; b_0(x,y)\,s + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\nu_{x,y}(dt),
\]
where the limit measure $\rho$ on $[0,1]$ is read in three pieces. The atom at $v = 0$ gives the
drift $b_0(x,y) := \rho(\{0\})$, because $k_s(0) = s$. On the interior $(0,1)$, the substitution
$v \mapsto t = -\log(1-v)$ carries $\rho$ back to a measure $\nu_{x,y}$ on $(0,\infty)$: the
weight $1/v$ in $k_s$ undoes the weighting by $1 - e^{-t}$, and $1 - (1-v)^s = 1 - e^{-st}$, so
$k_s(v)\,d\rho(v)$ on $(0,1)$ reads as $(1 - e^{-st})\,d\nu_{x,y}(t)$ on $(0,\infty)$; finiteness
of $\int_0^\infty (1 \wedge t)\,\nu_{x,y}(dt)$ follows from the finiteness of $\rho$, since
$1 - e^{-t} \ge (1 - e^{-1})(1 \wedge t)$. The atom at $v = 1$ would be a killing term, and is
excluded by $g_{x,y}(0) = 0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:transform-continuity}). So $g_{x,y} \in \BFz$ by the
representation itself.
The case $x = y$ is $g_{x,x} = 0 \in \BFz$.
\end{proof}

In particular every kernel is infinitely divisible: for each $n$, $e^{-g_{x,y}/n}$ is
completely monotone with value $1$ at $\zp$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(2)
and~(1)), hence the Laplace transform of a probability measure on $[0,\infty)$, whose $n$-fold
convolution is $\mu_{x,y}$.

% shared with blueprint cor:strict-monotonicity
\begin{corollary}[strict monotonicity]\label{cor:strict-monotonicity}
Assume in addition (ND). Then for every $s > 0$ and $x < y$,
\[
  G(y, s) - G(x, s) = g_{x,y}(s) > 0,
\]
i.e.\ $G(\cdot, s)$ is strictly increasing for every $s > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By (ND), $\Phi_{x,y} \ne \Id$, so $\mu_{x,y} \ne \delta_0$ by the uniqueness clause of
Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation}, and therefore $g_{x,y} \not\equiv 0$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}, $g_{x,y} \in \BFz$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:vanishing} a
member of $\BFz$ vanishing at a single point of $(0,\infty)$ vanishes identically, so
$g_{x,y}(s) > 0$ for all $s > 0$. The displayed identity is Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity}.
\end{proof}

The corollary is the formal content of ``the scale axis carries no idle stretches''
(\S\ref{sec:measurement-continuity}): for each $s > 0$ the accumulated exponent $G(\cdot, s)$
is injective. That injectivity is what makes the relabelling $\Sact_\sigma$ of (A8) rigid and
the canonical gauge exist, in \S\ref{sec:covariance}.
