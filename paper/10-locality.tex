\section{The locality theorem}\label{sec:locality}

Theorem~5 of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} singles out $\alpha = \tfrac12$ --- the heat
signaling equation --- as the unique member of the stable family whose memory-line evolution is
a local, positivity-respecting differential equation. This section proves the hemigroup
analogue.

The structure is genuinely two-layered: locality \emph{alone} opens a ladder of admissible
local media of every order, each rung an $(n-1)$-parameter family, and it is the positive
maximum principle --- the requirement that the medium be a physical transport medium --- that
closes the ladder at order two, where it leaves the Bessel family together with the degenerate
transport member of case (1) (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}). Throughout we assume (H), work in the
canonical gauge, write $m(z) := \Ex[T_1^{-z}]$, and take $A$ and $B$ as in
\S\ref{sec:signaling}.

% shared with blueprint def:locality-pmp
\begin{definition}[locality; positive maximum principle]\label{def:locality-pmp}
The inversion operator $A$ is \emph{local of order $n$} if it agrees with a differential
expression $\sum_{j=0}^{n} c_j(x)\,\partial_x^j$, with $c_j \in C((0,\infty))$ and
$c_n \not\equiv 0$, on $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ \emph{and on the profiles $H(s\,\cdot)$,
$s > 0$}. It satisfies the \emph{positive maximum principle} (PMP) if $(Ag)(x_0) \le 0$
whenever $g \in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ attains a nonnegative maximum at $x_0$.

The profiles are part of the test class because the ($\Rightarrow$) direction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol} must reach the symbol, and $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ is
disjoint from the only class on which $B$'s behaviour is controlled; the maximum principle
stays on $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$, where a profile, having no interior maximum, would say
nothing.
\end{definition}

% shared with blueprint lem:local-polynomial-symbol
\begin{lemma}[covariant local = polynomial symbol]\label{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}
$A$ is local of order $n$ if and only if $B$ is a polynomial of degree $n$; in that case the
covariance built into the Mellin class (Definition~\ref{def:covariant-mellin-class}) forces the
coefficients
\[
  c_j(x) \;=\; \gamma_j\, x^{\,j-1}, \qquad
  A \;=\; \frac{1}{x}\sum_{j=0}^n \gamma_j\,\theta(\theta-1)\cdots(\theta-j+1)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{x}\,B(\theta).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) $x^{j}\partial_x^{j} = \theta(\theta-1)\cdots(\theta - j + 1)$, so a polynomial
$B$, expanded in falling factorials, gives $x^{-1}B(\theta) = \sum \gamma_j x^{j-1}\partial_x^j$,
a differential expression of order $\deg B$. This has to be checked on both classes of
Definition~\ref{def:locality-pmp}. On $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ it is the Euler-factor identity
and the linearity of the inverse Mellin transform along the line. On a profile $H(s\,\cdot)$ the
same argument applies to $h(x) = \sum_j\gamma_j x^j\partial_x^j H(sx)$, whose transform is
$P(z)s^{-z}\tilde H(z)$ by the identity used in ($\Rightarrow$) below; that transform is
absolutely integrable on the line because $|\Gamma(c+i\tau)|$ decays faster than every power of
$\tau$, so inverting recovers $h$ and $x^{-1}h$ is $A[H(s\,\cdot)]$.

($\Rightarrow$) Every operator of the covariant Mellin class
(Definition~\ref{def:covariant-mellin-class}) satisfies the covariance
$A\Delta_\sigma = \sigma^{-1}\Delta_\sigma A$, where
$(\Delta_\sigma g)(x) = g(x/\sigma)$: dilation multiplies the Mellin transform by $\sigma^{z}$,
and multiplying the symbol integrand by $\sigma^{z}$ translates the inverse transform's
argument, both reducing to $x^{-(c+i\tau)}\sigma^{c+i\tau} = (x/\sigma)^{-(c+i\tau)}$.
Imposing this on $\sum c_j(x)\partial_x^j$ and comparing coefficients of $g^{(j)}(x/\sigma)$
gives $c_j(x)\sigma^{-j} = \sigma^{-1}c_j(x/\sigma)$, i.e.\ $c_j(x) = c_j(1)x^{j-1}$.
Rewriting via falling factorials, $A$ agrees with $x^{-1}P(\theta)$ for the polynomial
$P(w) = \sum_j \gamma_j E_j(w)$, where $E_j(w) = \prod_{i<j}(-w-i)$.

It remains to identify $P$ with $B$, and the comparison is made on the profiles rather than on
$C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$ --- the class Definition~\ref{def:locality-pmp} was widened to
include, and the only one on which $B$'s behaviour is known. Fix $s > 0$ and put
$g = H(s\,\cdot)$. Locality gives $(Ag)(x) = \sum_j \gamma_j x^{j-1}\partial_x^j H(sx)$, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}(1)'s eigenfunction relation gives $(Ag)(x) = sH(sx)$; equating
them and clearing the weight,
\[
  s\,x\,H(sx) \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{n} \gamma_j\, x^{j}\,\partial_x^{j} H(sx),
  \qquad x > 0 .
\]
The left side is the realising function $h$ of Definition~\ref{def:inversion-operator} for
$g$, with $\tilde h(w) = s^{-w}\tilde H(w+1)$. On the right, $\partial_x^j H(sx) = (-s)^j\int t^j
e^{-sxt}\mu(dt)$, so $x \mapsto x^j\partial_x^j H(sx)$ has, by the Mellin shift $x^j$, the
dilation factor, and Lemma~\ref{lem:mellin-data} applied to the measure $t^j\mu(dt)$, the
transform $E_j(w)\tilde g(w)$ throughout $0 < \operatorname{Re} w < z_*$. Each term converges
there, so the sum may be transformed term by term, giving $\tilde h(w) = P(w)\tilde g(w)$ ---
which is exactly the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol-uniqueness} with $P$ in the place of a
candidate symbol. Hence $B$ and $P$ agree as meromorphic functions on
$0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*-1$; equivalently, since $\tilde g(w) = s^{-w}\tilde H(w)$ never
loses its nonvanishing factor,
\[
  \tilde H(z+1) \;=\; P(z)\,\tilde H(z)
\]
at every point of the strip, and $B(z) = P(z)$ at every point where $\tilde H(z) \ne 0$. No
injectivity of the inverse Mellin transform is used, and none is available.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the moment recursion]\label{lem:moment-recursion}
Suppose $A$ is local of order $n$, i.e.\ $B$ is a polynomial of degree $n$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B(0) = 0$; write $B(-z) = z\,Q(z)$ with $Q$ a polynomial of degree $n - 1$;
\item $z_* = \infty$: all negative moments of $T_1$ are finite;
\item $m(z+1) = Q(z)\,m(z)$ for all $z > 0$, and $Q > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) By Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion-symbol}, $B(-z) = z\,m(z+1)/m(z)$ on the strip. As
$z \downarrow 0$, $m(z) \to 1$ by dominated convergence --- the integrand $t^{-z}$ tends to
$1$ and is dominated by $1 + t^{-1}$, integrable because (H) places $1$ strictly below $z_*$
--- and $m(z+1)$ stays bounded, splitting the integral at $t = 1$ against a fixed
$\zeta \in (1, z_*)$. So $B(-z) \to 0$, and $B$ being a polynomial, $B(0) = 0$;
$Q(z) := B(-z)/z$ is then a polynomial of degree $n-1$.

(2) On $0 < z < z_* - 1$ we have the identity $\tilde H(z+1) = B(-z)\tilde H(z)$ between
functions analytic on the strip $0 < \operatorname{Re} z < z_*$. If $z_* < \infty$, the right
side is analytic at $z = z_* - 1 + \varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$, which extends
$\tilde H$ analytically through the real point $z_*$. But $\tilde H(z) = \int_0^\infty
x^{z-1}H(x)\,dx$ has nonnegative integrand --- $H(x) = \Ex\,e^{-xT_1} \ge 0$ --- which is
exactly the hypothesis of the cited theorem: for a Laplace transform with nonnegative
integrand, the real endpoint of the convergence strip is a singularity when finite
(\sscite{widder1941laplace}, Ch.~II, \S5, Thm.~5b, pp.~58--59 --- usually called the
Pringsheim--Landau theorem, though Widder himself credits Hamburger). Hence $z_* = \infty$.

(3) The recursion is now Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion-symbol} on all of $(0,\infty)$, and
positivity of $Q$ follows from $m > 0$.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint lem:log-convexity
\begin{lemma}[log-convexity]\label{lem:log-convexity}
$z \mapsto m(z)$ is log-convex on $(0, \infty)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
H\"older at the exponent pair $\bigl(\tfrac1\theta, \tfrac1{1-\theta}\bigr)$, applied to
$T_1^{-z_1}$ and $T_1^{-z_2}$, gives
\[
  m\bigl(\theta z_1 + (1-\theta) z_2\bigr)
    \;=\; \Ex\bigl[(T_1^{-z_1})^{\theta}\,(T_1^{-z_2})^{1-\theta}\bigr]
    \;\le\; m(z_1)^{\theta}\,m(z_2)^{1-\theta}
\]
at every $\theta \in [0,1]$ at once --- Cauchy--Schwarz being the case $\theta = \tfrac12$.
Since that inequality is the definition of log-convexity, nothing remains to upgrade.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness
\begin{lemma}[the shifted Gamma recursion; Krull--Webster at a linear $Q$]
\label{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}
Let $c, a > 0$ and let $m : (0,\infty) \to (0,\infty)$ be log-convex and satisfy
\[
  m(z+1) \;=\; c\,(z + a)\,m(z) \quad (z > 0),
  \qquad m(z) \to 1 \ \ (z \downarrow 0).
\]
Then
\[
  m(z) \;=\; c^{\,z}\,\frac{\Gamma(a + z)}{\Gamma(a)} \qquad (z > 0).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an integer $n \ge a$ and put $d := n - a \ge 0$. Define, for $y > 0$,
\[
  f(y) \;:=\; \frac{m(y + d)\; c^{-(y+d)}}{P(y)},
  \qquad P(y) := \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(y + j),
\]
which uses $m$ only on $(d, \infty) \subseteq (0,\infty)$. Two shifts cancel: the recursion
gives $m(y + 1 + d) = c\,(y + n)\,m(y+d)$, since $d + a = n$, while
$P(y+1)\,y = P(y)\,(y+n)$; together with $c^{-(y+1+d)} = c^{-(y+d)}c^{-1}$ this leaves
\[
  f(y+1) \;=\; y\,f(y) \qquad (y > 0).
\]
$f$ is positive, and $\log f(y) = \log m(y+d) - (y+d)\log c - \log P(y)$ is convex on
$(0,\infty)$: the first term is Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity} precomposed with a translation,
the second is affine, and the third is convex because $-\log(y+j)$ is, $\log$ being concave.
Bohr--Mollerup applied to $f/f(1)$ therefore gives $f = f(1)\,\Gamma$ on $(0,\infty)$.

Write $C := f(1)$ and $G(z) := c^{\,z}\Gamma(z+a)$. Unwinding the definition of $f$ at
$y = z - d$ and using $P(y)\,\Gamma(y) = \Gamma(y+n)$ with $y + n = z + a$ gives
$m(z) = C\,G(z)$ for every $z > d$. Both $m$ and $G$ satisfy the same recursion on
$(0,\infty)$, so $m(z + k)\,G(z) = m(z)\,G(z+k)$ for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$; choosing $k$
with $z + k > d$ and cancelling $G(z+k) > 0$ extends $m = C\,G$ to all of $(0,\infty)$.
Finally $z \downarrow 0$ gives $C\,\Gamma(a) = 1$, since $c^{\,z} \to 1$ and $\Gamma$ is
continuous at $a > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 5$'$: the locality theorem]\label{thm:locality}
Assume (H). The inversion operator $A$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form} is local
\emph{and} satisfies the positive maximum principle \textbf{iff} one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Order 1; pure transport, degenerate.)} $B(\theta) = -c'\theta$ with $c' > 0$:
then $A = -c'\,\partial_x$, the kernels are the deterministic delays
$\mu_{0,x} = \delta_{x/c'}$, and the signaling problem is the transport equation. This member
has no smoothing and is excluded by requiring absolutely continuous kernels
(Remark~\ref{rem:drift-boundary}).
\item \emph{(Order 2; the Bessel family.)} After normalizing the unit of time so that
$q_1 = 2$: $B(\theta) = 2\,\theta(\theta - a)$ for some $a > 0$; the kernels are the
inverse-gamma family $T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(2\gamma_a)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}; and in the parabolic gauge $\xi$
(Remark~\ref{rem:gauge-freedom}) the signaling problem is the classical degenerate-parabolic
PDE
\[
  \partial_t u \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial_\xi^2 u + \frac{\beta}{\xi}\,\partial_\xi u,
  \qquad \beta = \tfrac12 - a, \qquad u(t,0) = f(t),\quad u(0,\xi) = 0 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
Within case (2), the depth-homogeneous (semigroup) members are exactly $a = \tfrac12$, i.e.\
$\beta = 0$ --- the heat signaling equation --- recovering
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~5, as the zero-drift point of the family. The kernel
moments in case (2) satisfy $\Ex\,T_x^{\,n} < \infty$ iff $n < a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) Definition~\ref{def:locality-pmp}'s pair of hypotheses --- locality
(agreement with a differential expression on $C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$) together with the
positive maximum principle there --- is exactly Courrège's \emph{local} positive maximum
principle, whose Corollaire~2 (\sscite{courrege1966sur}, p.~2-10) bounds the order of such an
operator by $2$: it is a pure second-order diffusion operator with no jump part. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}, $\deg B \le 2$, and $B(0) = 0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1) --- equivalently, there is no killing, which is (A5).

\emph{Order 1.} Here $Q \equiv c'$ is constant, so $m(z+1) = c'm(z)$ with $m(\zp)=1$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity}, $\varphi := \log m$ is convex on $(0,\infty)$ and satisfies
$\varphi(z+1) - \varphi(z) = \log c'$ for every $z > 0$. The difference quotients of a convex
function are nondecreasing, and on each unit interval they average to $\log c'$; a
nondecreasing function whose average over every unit interval is the same constant equals that
constant identically, so $\varphi$ is affine. Hence $m(z) = c'^{\,z}$, the constant fixed by
$m(\zp) = 1$. Thus $T_1 = 1/c'$ a.s., $B(-z) = c'z$, i.e.\ $B(\theta) = -c'\theta$ and
$A = -c'x^{-1}\theta = -c'\partial_x$; positivity of $Q$ gives $c' > 0$.

\emph{Order 2.} Here $Q(z) = q_1 z + q_0$ with $q_1 > 0$, by positivity of $Q$ as
$z \to \infty$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(3), and $q_0 = Q(0) = m(1) > 0$; write
$Q(z) = q_1(z + a)$ with $a := q_0/q_1 > 0$. The recursion $m(z+1) = q_1(z+a)\,m(z)$ with
$m(\zp) = 1$ --- the normalisation established in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1) --- has a \emph{unique} log-convex solution by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}, namely
\[
  m(z) \;=\; q_1^{\,z}\,\frac{\Gamma(a + z)}{\Gamma(a)}
  \;=\; \Ex\Bigl[\bigl(q_1\,\gamma_a\bigr)^{z}\Bigr],
\]
and $m$ is log-convex by Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity}, hence equals it. So
$m(z) = \Ex[(q_1\gamma_a)^z]$ for every real $z > 0$. Both sides are, as functions of $z$, the
moment functions of $\log(1/T_1)$ and of $\log(q_1\gamma_a)$: they are analytic for
$\operatorname{Re} z > 0$ ($m = \tilde H/\Gamma$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion-symbol} and
$\Gamma$'s own analyticity, the right side being entire), and, since $|T_1^{-z}|$ and
$|(q_1\gamma_a)^z|$ are bounded by $1$ on the line $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$, each is
continuous up to it as well. The identity therefore extends, first by the identity theorem
across the open half-plane, then by continuity to the line $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$, where it
says that $\log(1/T_1)$ and $\log(q_1\gamma_a)$ have the same Fourier transform; Fourier
uniqueness then identifies the two laws, giving $T_1 \stackrel{d}{=} 1/(q_1\gamma_a)$. (This is
the one place in this proof that spends transform uniqueness on the scale variable itself,
rather than on moment data alone.) The time normalization $q_1 = 2$ gives
Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}, the profile is the Bessel-K function, and the operator
is computed in Example~\ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}: $B(\theta) = 2\theta(\theta - a)$, with the
parabolic-gauge form as displayed.

($\Leftarrow$) \emph{Case (1).} Admissibility is the pure-drift member $F(s) = s/c'$ of
(7.1), and a first-order operator satisfies the PMP trivially, since $g'(x_0) = 0$ at an
interior maximum. \emph{Case (2).} Admissibility of the inverse-gamma family is
Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}; locality is Example~\ref{ex:bessel-quadratic}; and, in
the parabolic gauge $\xi$, a second-order operator $\tfrac12\partial_\xi^2 + \tfrac{\beta}{\xi}\partial_\xi$
with nonnegative leading coefficient satisfies the PMP, because at an interior nonnegative
maximum $g'(\xi_0) = 0$ and $g''(\xi_0) \le 0$, so $(Ag)(\xi_0) = \tfrac12 g''(\xi_0) \le 0$.

\emph{The homogeneity claim.} By Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case}, depth-homogeneous
members have $F(s) = c\,s^{\alpha}$; within the inverse-gamma family the exponent is a pure
power only for $a = \tfrac12$. Indeed, by Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}, $e^{-F(s)} =
\tfrac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma(a)}(\sqrt{2s})^a K_a(\sqrt{2s})$, and the large-argument asymptotic
$K_a(w) \sim \sqrt{\pi/(2w)}\,e^{-w}$ as $w \to \infty$ gives
\[
  F(s) \;=\; \sqrt{2s} \;-\; \Bigl(\tfrac a2 - \tfrac14\Bigr)\log s \;+\; O(1),
  \qquad s \to \infty,
\]
which is a pure power of $s$ only when the logarithmic coefficient vanishes, i.e.\
$a = \tfrac12$ --- where $K_{1/2}$ is elementary and $H(y) = e^{-\sqrt{2y}}$ exactly, the
$\tfrac12$-stable, i.e.\ heat, case. The moment statement is
Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:local-ladder
\begin{proposition}[the ladder: locality without the maximum principle]\label{prop:local-ladder}
Fix $n \ge 2$, $c' > 0$ and $a_1, \dots, a_{n-1} > 0$, and let
\[
  T_1 \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; \frac{1}{c'\,\gamma_{a_1}\gamma_{a_2}\cdots\gamma_{a_{n-1}}}
  \quad (\text{independent factors}), \qquad
  m(z) = c'^{\,z}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(a_i + z)}{\Gamma(a_i)} .
\]
Then the family is admissible in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} and
satisfies (H), and its inversion operator is \emph{local of order $n$}, with polynomial
symbol
\[
  B(-z) \;=\; c'\,z\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z + a_i);
\]
the kernels are Meijer $G$-function densities. Conversely, every local admissible family of
order $n$ has $m$ equal to the unique log-convex solution of $m(z+1) = Q(z)m(z)$ for its
polynomial $Q$; when the roots of $Q$ are real --- necessarily $\le 0$, giving
$Q(z) = c'\prod(z+a_i)$ with $a_i > 0$ --- this is the family above, and the laws are
identified from $m$ as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Admissibility.} Gamma densities are hyperbolically completely monotone ($\HCM$), and
the $\HCM$ class is closed under products of independent variables and under reciprocals,
while $\HCM$ laws are generalized gamma convolutions and hence self-decomposable
(\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}, Thm.~5.1.1, p.~69; Thm.~4.3.1, p.~57, whose regularity
hypotheses are removed on p.~68; and the inclusion $\GGC \subset \SDclass$, a remark on
p.~30). So $-\log \Ex e^{-sT_1}$ is of the form (7.1), and (H) holds
because all negative moments are finite and $\mathbb{P}(T_1 = 0) = 0$.

\emph{The symbol.} $B(-z) = z\,m(z+1)/m(z) = c'z\prod(z+a_i)$, a polynomial, so $A$ is local
of order $n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}.

\emph{The converse.} This is Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity} together with the Krull--Webster uniqueness theorem
(\sscite{webster1997log}, Thm.~3.1, p.~609), as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:locality} ---
the general theorem now, the recursion carrying $n-1$ Gamma factors, for which
Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}'s single-factor case is not enough. Two hypotheses
of Webster's theorem must be discharged, not assumed. The asymptotic condition is automatic:
$Q$ is a polynomial, so $Q(z+w)/Q(z) \to 1$ as $z \to \infty$ for each fixed $w > 0$. The
normalization is not automatic --- Webster pins the solution with $f(1) = 1$, where this
proposition's normalization is $m(\zp) = 1$ --- so apply Webster to $\hat m := m/m(1)$, which
satisfies $\hat m(1) = 1$ and the same recursion; this gives uniqueness of $\hat m$ and hence
of $m$, the constant $m(1) = Q(0)\,m(\zp) = Q(0)$ being read off the recursion at $z = 0$. A
real root $-a_i \ge 0$ of $Q$ would contradict $Q > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$ together with
$Q(0) = m(1) > 0$, so real roots give $a_i > 0$, and the Krull--Webster solution is the
displayed gamma product.

\emph{The Meijer $G$ identification.} The product $\gamma_{a_1}\cdots\gamma_{a_{n-1}}$ has a
$G^{\,n-1,0}_{\,0,n-1}$ density, for arbitrary distinct shapes, by
\sscite{springer1970distribution}, Thm.~1, p.~722; the reciprocal and the scale factor $c'$
are then absorbed by the $G$-function transformation rules (\sscite{samko1993fractional},
eqs.~(1.96) and (1.97), p.~22).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[complex roots]\label{rem:complex-roots}
Whether complex-conjugate root pairs can support an admissible family is left open; we
conjecture not. The question is taken up in a companion note, where spectrally leading
pairs are excluded and order three is settled.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the stable slice; Gauss multiplication]\label{rem:stable-slice}
Depth-homogeneity intersects the ladder at the equally spaced parameters $a_j = j/n$ and
$c' = n^n$: by the Gauss multiplication formula,
\[
  m(z) \;=\; n^{nz}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(j/n + z)}{\Gamma(j/n)}
  \;=\; n\,\frac{\Gamma(nz)}{\Gamma(z)}, \qquad
  \tilde H(z) = n\,\Gamma(nz), \qquad H(y) = e^{-y^{1/n}},
\]
the $\tfrac1n$-stable laws --- recovering the classical identity that represents the
$\tfrac1n$-stable subordinator as a scaled reciprocal product of $n-1$ gamma variables. Thus
the local cases $\alpha = 1/n$ of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} are precisely the
homogeneous slice of the ladder, and each unfolds under the hemigroup axioms into an
$(n-1)$-parameter inhomogeneous family of local media. The maximum principle eliminates
$n \ge 3$ in both settings; what remains at $n = 2$ is the single point $\alpha = \tfrac12$
there, and the Bessel line here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what the theorem settles]\label{rem:trilemma}
Theorem~\ref{thm:locality} completes the resolution of the practicality problem of
Remark~\ref{rem:heavy-tails} \emph{within the local class}: the drift weight
$\beta = \tfrac12 - a$ is a design parameter, and any prescribed number of finite kernel
moments is achievable by a local, positivity-respecting, continuously scale-covariant,
time-causal medium, namely $n < a$. The residual trade-off is the tail: inverse-gamma kernels
have polynomial tails $t^{-1-a}$ for every finite $a$, so \emph{all} moments --- exponential
localization --- still require leaving the local class for the Gamma-type corner of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family}.

Homogeneity, locality and moment control thus interact as follows: homogeneity together with
covariance forces stable kernels, which have no mean; locality together with covariance and
the PMP allows any finite number of moments but polynomial tails; full moment control
requires nonlocal delay accrual.
\end{remark}
