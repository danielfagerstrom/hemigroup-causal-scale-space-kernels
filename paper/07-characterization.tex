\section{Self-decomposability and the characterization theorem}\label{sec:characterization}

The two preceding sections reduce the family to one function. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity} the exponent of a kernel is a difference of accumulated
exponents, $g_{x,y} = G(y,\cdot) - G(x,\cdot)$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-gauge}
the accumulated exponent is a dilate of one function, $G(x,s) = F(\chi(x)s)$. So every kernel
is read off $F$ by stretching its argument and subtracting:
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) \;=\; F(bs) - F(as), \qquad a = \chi(x) \le b = \chi(y),
\]
and since $\chi$ is onto, $(a,b)$ ranges over all pairs $0 \le a \le b$. Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}
requires each of these differences to be a Bernstein function vanishing at the origin. That
is a condition on $F$ alone --- not that $F$ is Bernstein, which it is, but that
\emph{every stretched difference} $F(b\,\cdot) - F(a\,\cdot)$ is --- and it is exactly the
condition this section solves.

It has a probabilistic name. Let $T_1$ be the random delay whose law is the kernel at
canonical scale $1$, so that $\Ex\,e^{-sT_1} = e^{-F(s)}$; in the canonical gauge the kernel
at scale $x$ is then the law of $xT_1$. That $F(s) - F(cs)$ is a Laplace exponent for
$c \in (0,1)$ says that the law of $T_1$ is the convolution of the law of $cT_1$ with some
probability law: there is a random variable $R_c$, independent of $T_1$, with
\[
  T_1 \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; c\,T_1 + R_c \qquad \text{for every } c \in (0,1).
\]
A law with this property --- shrink it by any factor, and what was removed is itself a
random variable independent of the shrunk copy --- is called \emph{self-decomposable}, or of
class $L$ (\sscite{sato1999levy}, Def.~15.1, p.~90; \S\ref{sec:preliminaries}). It is the
hemigroup form of infinite divisibility: in the semigroup case the kernel at scale $\tau$ is
the $n$-fold convolution power of the kernel at $\tau/n$, all stages identical; here the
stages differ, but covariance turns a shrinking of the scale axis into a shrinking of the
delay, and the cascade requires what remains, the passage from scale $c$ to scale $1$, to be
a measurement in its own right --- that is $R_c$. The lemma below is the analytic form of
this statement: the classical characterization of self-decomposable laws by their Lévy
measure (\sscite{sato1999levy}, Cor.~15.11, p.~95), which is what makes $F$ concrete.

% shared with blueprint lem:selfdecomposable-exponents
\begin{lemma}[self-decomposable exponents]\label{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}
For $F \in \BFz$ the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F(b\,\cdot) - F(a\,\cdot) \in \BFz$ for all $0 < a \le b$;
\item $B(s) := s\,F'(s)$ is a Bernstein function;
\item $F$ has the representation
\[
  F(s) = b_0\, s + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\, \frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt,
  \qquad b_0 \ge 0, \tag{7.1}
\]
with $k : (0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ nonincreasing, $\int_0^1 k < \infty$ and
$\int_1^\infty k(t)/t\,dt < \infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(1) $\Rightarrow$ (2).} For $h > 0$ the difference quotient
$s \mapsto \bigl(F(e^h s) - F(s)\bigr)/h$ lies in $\BF$, being a positive multiple of an
element of $\BFz$. As $h \downarrow 0$ it converges pointwise to $sF'(s)$, since $F$ is smooth
on $(0,\infty)$; the limit is therefore Bernstein by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(4).

\emph{(2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3).} Write $F(s) = b_0 s + \int (1 - e^{-st})\,\nu(dt)$, with no
killing term because $F(\zp) = 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(3)), so that
\[
  B(s) = s F'(s) = b_0 s + \int_0^\infty s\,t\, e^{-st}\, \nu(dt).
\]
Note $B(\zp) = 0$, since $0 \le sF'(s) \le F(s)$ by concavity.

Suppose $\nu(dt) = k(t)\,t^{-1}dt$ with $k$ nonincreasing. Write
$k(t) = \rho\bigl((t,\infty)\bigr)$ for the positive measure $\rho = -dk$; right-continuity of
$k$ may be assumed, and $k(\infty) = 0$ is forced by $\int^\infty k(t)t^{-1}dt < \infty$.
Tonelli then gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty s\, e^{-st}\, k(t)\, dt
  = \int_0^\infty \rho(du) \int_0^u s\,e^{-st}\,dt
  = \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-su}\bigr)\,\rho(du),
\]
so $B(s) = b_0 s + \int (1 - e^{-su})\rho(du) \in \BFz$.

Conversely, if $B \in \BF$ then $B \in \BFz$ by the remark above, so
$B(s) = \beta s + \int (1 - e^{-su})\rho(du)$. Running the same computation backwards and
invoking uniqueness of the Lévy--Khintchine triple
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(3)) identifies $\beta = b_0$ and
$\nu(dt) = k(t)t^{-1}dt$ with $k(t) = \rho((t,\infty))$ nonincreasing.

\emph{(3) $\Rightarrow$ (1).} Write
$F(cs) = b_0 c s + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-cst}\bigr)\,k(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}$ for $c > 0$ and
substitute $u = ct$. The measure $dt/t$ is invariant under dilations, so
\[
  F(cs) = b_0 c\, s + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-su}\bigr)\, k(u/c)\,\frac{du}{u} ,
\]
i.e.\ dilating the argument by $c$ dilates the density and rescales the drift, and does
nothing else. Subtracting the identity at $c = a$ from the one at $c = b$,
\[
  F(bs) - F(as) = b_0 (b-a)\, s
    + \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-su}\bigr)\,\bigl(k(u/b) - k(u/a)\bigr)\,\frac{du}{u},
\]
which lies in $\BFz$: the drift $b_0(b-a)$ is nonnegative since $a \le b$, the density is
nonnegative since $u/b \le u/a$ for $u > 0$ and $k$ is nonincreasing, and both integrals are
finite, each being bounded by $F(bs) < \infty$. Vanishing at $\zp$ is monotone convergence.
\end{proof}

The three conditions are not equally deep, and the asymmetry organizes what follows. The
implication (3)~$\Rightarrow$~(1) is elementary --- a change of variables in the
representation, using no property of the $\CM$ class --- and it is all that the constructive
direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} consumes. The legs
(1)~$\Rightarrow$~(2)~$\Rightarrow$~(3) are different in kind: $B(s) = sF'(s)$ has no meaning
in the representation vocabulary until the representation is in hand, and both legs spend the
classical theory --- closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits, and uniqueness of the
Lévy--Khintchine triple. This is why the constructive direction of the main theorem is cheap
and the analytic one is not. The printed proofs take the classical route: the constructive
direction of the theorem reaches its measures by Bernstein--Widder, and the analysis
direction by the legs (1)~$\Rightarrow$~(2)~$\Rightarrow$~(3). The machine-checked proofs
reach the same two implications by the two cited facts of \S\ref{sec:machine-checked}, one
per half, and it is that separation --- existence of a subordinator for the construction,
self-decomposability for the analysis, neither borrowing the other --- that
\S\ref{sec:machine-checked} certifies.

Condition~(2) has a reading that the section's tail supplies. By Lemma~\ref{lem:memory-kernel}
below, $F'$ is the drift plus the Laplace transform of the nonincreasing function $k$, so
$B(s) = sF'(s)$ is $s$ times that transform; and since $\partial_x F(xs) = sF'(xs)$, it is the
symbol of the evolution of the family across scale. Condition~(2) says that this symbol is
itself a Bernstein function --- the evolution across scale is, at each scale, again a
subordination.

% Verbatim from the blueprint's rem:sato-reading.
\begin{remark}[the probabilistic reading]\label{rem:sato-reading}
Probabilistically, (7.1) together with the similarity structure
below says that the kernels are the marginal laws of a \emph{self-similar additive process},
a Sato subordinator: an increasing process $(T_{\tilde x})_{\tilde x \ge 0}$ with independent
--- but not stationary --- increments, satisfying
$T_{\sigma \tilde x} \stackrel{d}{=} \sigma\, T_{\tilde x}$.
\end{remark}

% shared with blueprint thm:main-characterization
\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main-characterization}
A family $(\Phi_{x,y})_{0 \le x \le y}$ satisfies (A1)--(A8) and (ND) \textbf{iff} there exist
an increasing bijection $\chi$ of $[0,\infty)$ --- the gauge --- and a function $F$ of the
form (7.1) with $F \not\equiv 0$ such that
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} f = \mu_{x,y} * f, \qquad
  \hat\mu_{x,y}(s) = \exp\Bigl[-\bigl(F(\chi(y)\,s) - F(\chi(x)\,s)\bigr)\Bigr].
\]
In particular, in the canonical gauge the scale-space kernels are
\[
  \phi_{x}(t) \;=\; \Lap^{-1}\Bigl[\, e^{-F(x\,s)} \,\Bigr](t),
  \qquad F \text{ as in } (7.1),
\]
and the pair $(\chi, F)$ is unique up to the normalization $\chi(1) = 1$.
\end{theorem}

Read against \S\ref{sec:measurement}: the apertures are the marginal laws $\mu_{0,x}$ of one
Sato subordinator, the kernel at canonical scale $x$ being the law of $xT_1$, and the whole
family is generated by one law and the gauge. What the axioms leave free is exactly the pair
$(b_0, k)$ in (7.1): a deterministic front, and a nonincreasing density.

\begin{proof}
$(\Rightarrow)$ Lemmas \ref{lem:convolution-representation}, \ref{lem:transform-continuity}
and \ref{lem:additivity}, Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein},
Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}, Lemmas \ref{lem:covariance-laplace} and
\ref{lem:action-rigidity}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-gauge} together give the
representation with $F = G(1,\cdot) \in \BFz$ and $F \not\equiv 0$. Every increment
$F(\chi(y)s) - F(\chi(x)s)$ lies in $\BFz$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}, and
$\chi$ is onto $(0,\infty)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-gauge}, so condition~(1) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} holds for every pair $0 < a \le b$, and the lemma
yields (7.1).

$(\Leftarrow)$ Let $F$ be of the form (7.1) with $F \not\equiv 0$; work in the canonical
gauge. For $0 \le a \le b$ the function $g_{a,b}(s) := F(bs) - F(as)$ lies in $\BFz$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, (3)~$\Rightarrow$~(1) (for $a = 0$ the increment is
$F(b\,\cdot)$ itself, which is in $\BFz$ by hypothesis), so $e^{-g_{a,b}}$ is
completely monotone with value $1$ at $\zp$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(2)), and
by the Bernstein--Widder criterion (Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(1)) it is the
Laplace transform of a unique probability measure $\mu_{a,b}$ on $[0,\infty)$. Define
$\Phi_{a,b} f := \mu_{a,b} * f$. Then:
(A1)--(A5) hold by the converse direction of Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation};
(A6) holds because exponents add, $g_{a,b} + g_{b,c} = g_{a,c}$, and Laplace transforms
determine measures (Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness});
(A8) holds with $\Sact_\sigma x = \sigma x$, since
$\hat\mu_{\sigma a, \sigma b}(s) = \hat\mu_{a,b}(\sigma s)$;
and (ND) holds because $F$ is strictly increasing, by Lemma~\ref{lem:vanishing} applied to the
increments of $F$ as in Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}.

For (A7): if $(a_n, b_n) \to (a,b)$ then $\hat\mu_{a_n,b_n} \to \hat\mu_{a,b}$ pointwise, by
continuity of $F$, so $\mu_{a_n,b_n} \to \mu_{a,b}$ weakly by the continuity theorem
(Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-continuity}). Weak convergence of probability measures implies
$\mu_{a_n,b_n} * f \to \mu_{a,b} * f$ in $\Lone$ for every $f \in X$: writing
$\mu * f = \int \shift_r f\, \mu(dr)$ as an $X$-valued Bochner integral, the map
$r \mapsto \shift_r f$ is bounded and continuous, and an $\varepsilon/3$ argument on a compact
carrying mass $1 - \varepsilon$ concludes, first for compactly supported continuous $f$ and
then for all of $X$ by density, the error not growing because each $\Phi_{a,b}$ is a
contraction.

Finally, for an arbitrary gauge $\chi$ set $\Phi'_{x,y} := \Phi_{\chi(x),\chi(y)}$: (A1)--(A6)
and (ND) hold verbatim, (A7) because an increasing bijection of $[0,\infty)$ is continuous, and
(A8) with $\Sact'_\sigma = \chi^{-1}(\sigma\,\chi(\cdot))$.

\emph{Uniqueness.} The exponents $g_{0,x}$ are determined by the family
(Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness}, with $\exp$ injective), and $\chi(1) = 1$ recovers
$F = g_{0,1}$ (without the normalization the pair is determined up to
$(\chi, F) \mapsto (c\,\chi,\ F(c^{-1}\,\cdot))$, $c > 0$, which is the freedom the
normalization removes). For each $x$, $\chi(x)$ is then the unique $c$ with $g_{0,x} = F(c\,\cdot)$:
such a $c$ exists by the representation, and if $F(cs) = F(c's)$ for all $s$ with
$\rho := c/c' \ne 1$, then iterating $F(s) = F(\rho^{\pm n} s)$ towards the origin and using
$F(\zp) = 0$ gives $F \equiv 0$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint cor:semigroup-case
\begin{corollary}[recovering the semigroup case]\label{cor:semigroup-case}
Assume in addition that the family is one-parameter: $\Phi_{x,y}$ depends only on $y - x$.
Then, after the normalization $g_{0,1}(1) = 1$,
\[
  F(s) = s^{\alpha} \quad \text{for some } 0 < \alpha \le 1,
\]
i.e.\ the kernels are exactly the extremal stable densities of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, Thm.~2, together with the boundary case $\alpha = 1$ --- the
pure delay $\mu_{0,x} = \delta_x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Homogeneity gives $g_{x,y} = (y - x)\,g$ with $g := g_{0,1}$, and (6.1) becomes
$(\Sact_\sigma y - \Sact_\sigma x)\, g(s) = (y - x)\, g(\sigma s)$. Hence $\Sact_\sigma$ is
affine, and fixing $0$ it is linear: $\Sact_\sigma x = c(\sigma) x$ with
$c(\sigma) g(s) = g(\sigma s)$.

Then $c$ is a continuous multiplicative homomorphism of $(0,\infty)$, so $c(\sigma) =
\sigma^\alpha$ by Cauchy's functional equation, as in \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal},
Lemma~2. Now $g(\sigma s) = \sigma^\alpha g(s)$ forces $g(s) = g(1) s^\alpha$, and the
normalization makes $g(1) = 1$. Membership of $\BFz$ restricts $\alpha$ to $(0,1]$. The value
$\alpha = 1$ gives $sF'(s) = s \in \BF$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} admits
it and it is not excluded by the present axioms.
\end{proof}

% Verbatim from the blueprint's rem:drift-boundary.
\begin{remark}[the boundary case $\alpha = 1$ and the drift term]\label{rem:drift-boundary}
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} excluded $\alpha = 1$ through the pointwise-continuity
requirement on the kernel functions, which the deterministic delay $\delta(t - x)$ violates.
Under Definition~\ref{def:cascade-family} the delay family --- and more generally the drift
term $b_0 s$ in (7.1), which shifts every kernel by $b_0 x$ --- is admissible.

If that is undesired it is removed by one additional axiom: absolute continuity of the kernels
for $x > 0$, or the requirement $\lim_{s\to\infty} F(s)/s = 0$, which says there is no
deterministic front.
\end{remark}

% shared with blueprint lem:admissible-cone
\begin{lemma}[the admissible exponents form a convex cone]\label{lem:admissible-cone}
If $F_1, F_2$ are admissible in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} --- of the
form (7.1) with drift $b_0 \ge 0$ and $k \ge 0$ nonincreasing --- then so are $F_1 + F_2$, with
data $(b_0^1 + b_0^2,\, k_1 + k_2)$, and $c\,F_1$ for $c \ge 0$, with data $(c\,b_0^1,\, c\,k_1)$.
So the admissible exponents form a convex cone in the pointwise order, and the correspondence
with the pairs $(b_0,k)$ is linear.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each of the six defining conditions is stable: nonnegativity and monotonicity of $k$ are
preserved by sums and by nonnegative multiples, the drift stays nonnegative, and the exponent of
a sum is the sum of the exponents by additivity of the integral --- so finiteness is inherited.
Linearity of $(b_0,k) \mapsto F$ is the same computation read forwards.
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint lem:dickman-superposition
\begin{lemma}[Dickman superposition]\label{lem:dickman-superposition}
Write $\mathrm{Ein}(z) := \int_0^z \bigl(1 - e^{-u}\bigr)\,u^{-1}du$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(The rays.)} For $\tau > 0$ the density $k = \mathbf{1}_{(0,\tau)}$ is admissible,
with drift $0$, and its exponent is $s \mapsto \mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)$.
\item \emph{(The superposition.)} Every admissible $F$, with drift $b_0$ and density $k$,
satisfies
\[
  F(s) \;=\; b_0\, s \;+\; \int_{(0,\infty)} \mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)\,\rho(d\tau)
  \qquad (s \ge 0),
\]
where $\rho$ is any positive measure on $(0,\infty)$ with $\rho\bigl((t,\infty)\bigr) = k(t)$ for
almost every $t > 0$; such a $\rho$ exists.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the substitution $u = st$ in $\int_0^\tau (1 - e^{-st})\,t^{-1}dt$; admissibility is the
integrability criterion of Proposition~\ref{prop:admissibility-criterion}, both halves being
immediate for a bounded, compactly supported $k$.

(2) $k$ is nonincreasing and nonnegative and $k(\infty) = 0$, the last forced by
$\int^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt < \infty$; so a positive measure with tail $k$ exists. Then Tonelli
over the region $\{0 < t < \tau\}$ gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt
  = \int_{(0,\infty)} \rho(d\tau) \int_0^\tau \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{dt}{t}
  = \int_{(0,\infty)} \mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)\,\rho(d\tau),
\]
the inner integral being $\mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)$ by (1).
\end{proof}

% shared with blueprint prop:extreme-rays
\begin{proposition}[Choquet structure of the admissible cone]\label{prop:extreme-rays}
The superposition map of Lemma~\ref{lem:dickman-superposition},
\[
  (b_0, \rho) \;\mapsto\; F, \qquad
  F(s) = b_0\,s + \int_{(0,\infty)} \mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)\,\rho(d\tau),
\]
is a linear \emph{bijection} from
$\bigl\{(b_0, \rho) : b_0 \ge 0,\ \rho \text{ a positive Borel measure on } (0,\infty),\
\int \bigl[\tau \wedge (1 + \log_+ \tau)\bigr]\,\rho(d\tau) < \infty \bigr\}$
onto the cone of Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible-cone}, with inverse $\rho = -dk$,
$k(t) = \rho\bigl((t,\infty)\bigr)$. Consequently the
extreme rays of the cone are the pure delay $s \mapsto s$ together with the one-parameter family
$\mathrm{Ein}(\tau\,\cdot)$, $\tau > 0$ --- the Laplace exponents, with L\'evy density
$t^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{(0,\tau)}(t)$, of the \emph{dilated Dickman subordinators}
(\sscite{caravenna2019dickman}, (1.1)--(1.2), p.~1) --- and every admissible family is a
\emph{unique} superposition of Dickman rays and drift.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} an admissible $F$ is exactly one of the form (7.1)
with $b_0 \ge 0$ and $k$ nonincreasing, and $k$ may be taken right-continuous with
$k(\infty) = 0$, the latter forced by $\int^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt < \infty$. So $\rho := -dk$
is a positive measure on $(0,\infty)$ with $k(t) = \rho((t,\infty))$, and Tonelli gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt
  = \int_{(0,\infty)} \rho(d\tau) \int_0^\tau \bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{dt}{t}
  = \int_{(0,\infty)} \mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)\,\rho(d\tau),
\]
which is the displayed representation. Since $\mathrm{Ein}(z) \le z$ and
$\mathrm{Ein}(z) = \log z + \gamma_E + o(1)$, $\gamma_E$ Euler's constant, as $z \to \infty$, the integral is finite for one
$s > 0$ iff it is finite for all, iff $\int [\tau \wedge (1 + \log_+\tau)]\,\rho(d\tau) <
\infty$; so the stated domain is exactly the set of pairs the representation accepts. The map
is linear in $(b_0, \rho)$ by construction and surjective by the above. It is injective because
the L\'evy--Khintchine triple of $F$ is unique (Proposition~\ref{prop:bernstein-toolbox}(3)):
the triple determines $b_0$ and the L\'evy measure $k(t)t^{-1}dt$, hence $k$ up to a null set,
hence $\rho = -dk$.

A linear bijection carries extreme rays to extreme rays, so it suffices to identify them in the
domain, which is the product of the ray $[0,\infty)$ with a cone of positive measures. Its
extreme rays are $(1, 0)$ and $(0, \delta_\tau)$, $\tau > 0$: any $(b_0, \rho)$ decomposes
uniquely as $b_0\,(1,0) + \int (0,\delta_\tau)\,\rho(d\tau)$, and a pair is extreme only if it
admits no nontrivial such splitting, which forces $\rho = 0$ or $\rho$ a single atom with
$b_0 = 0$. Under the map, $(1,0) \mapsto s$, the pure delay; and $(0, \delta_\tau) \mapsto
\mathrm{Ein}(\tau s)$, whose $k(t) = \delta_\tau((t,\infty)) = \mathbf{1}_{(0,\tau)}(t)$ gives
the L\'evy density $t^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{(0,\tau)}(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the three corners are slices, not extremes]\label{rem:extreme-rays}
None of the distinguished families of this paper is extreme; each is a slice cut by a different
principle --- the stable family by dilation symmetry (Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case},
Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-family}), the Gamma family as the atoms of the Thorin
representation (Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-family}), the Bessel family by locality
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bessel-family}, Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}). Since all
three have completely monotone $k$, the closed convex cone the three corners generate together
is exactly the Thorin subclass --- strictly smaller than the whole class, the difference being
the memory kernels that are monotone but not completely monotone (plateaus, kinks, hard cutoffs). The
Dickman rays are the paradigm of that difference: their kernels solve the delay equation
obtained from the Volterra identity of Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} with the step kernel
$k = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau)}$, and carry derivative singularities at the delays
$\tau, 2\tau, \dots$ --- echo signatures unattainable within the Thorin subclass.

Two bounds hold throughout the cone off the drift ray. If $k \not\equiv 0$, every kernel
$\mu_{0,x}$, $x > 0$, is a nondegenerate self-decomposable law, hence absolutely continuous
(\sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~27.13) and \emph{unimodal}, by Yamazato's theorem on class $L$
laws (\sscite{yamazato1978unimodality}, Thm.~1, p.~523); on the drift ray $k \equiv 0$ the
kernel is the point mass $\delta_{b_0 x}$. The axioms never produce multimodal kernels. And
every kernel with $k \not\equiv 0$ is infinitely divisible and carried by $[0,\infty)$, so its
tail is never lighter than $e^{-c\,t\log t}$ (\sscite{sato1999levy}, Thm.~26.1, p.~168): no
truncation of the Gaussian is an admissible kernel among these, which is the claim made in
\S\ref{sec:measurement}; the drift ray is the point mass above, whose tail is of course lighter
than any such bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[the kernel behind $F'$]\label{lem:memory-kernel}
Let $F$ be as in (7.1). Then
\[
  F'(s) \;=\; b_0 \;+\; \int_0^\infty e^{-st}\, k(t)\,dt, \qquad s > 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the integrand of (7.1) in $s$ gives $e^{-st}k(t)$, which for $s \ge s_0 > 0$ is
dominated by $e^{-s_0 t} k(t)$. That dominating function is integrable near $0$ because
$\int_0^1 k < \infty$, and at infinity because $k$ is bounded there, being nonincreasing; so
differentiation under the integral sign is justified.
\end{proof}

Thus $F'$ is, up to the drift, the Laplace transform of $k$. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory}
reads this as the statement that $k$ --- in $L^1$-normalized dilation to the current scale,
$\kappa^{(x)}(dt) := b_0\,\delta_0(dt) + \tfrac1x\,k(\tfrac tx)\,dt$ --- is the memory kernel
of the evolution across scale. The monotonicity of $k$, which entered abstractly as
condition~(3) of Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, is precisely the admissibility of
$\kappa^{(x)}$ as a Lévy tail. In the extremal stable case
$k(t) = \alpha t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)$, and the operator
$\partial_t(\kappa^{(x)} * \cdot)$ is the Riemann--Liouville derivative $D^{\alpha}_{t,+}$ of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, up to the scale factor $\alpha x^{\alpha-1}$ that
Remark~\ref{rem:recovering-thm3} absorbs into the semigroup's own parametrization; in the
Gamma family $k(t) = \gamma e^{-t}$, a bounded kernel.
