\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

This paper characterizes the time-causal scale spaces: the families of smoothing operators
through which an uncommitted observer can measure a temporal signal at all scales at once.
The axioms are those of causal, covariant measurement, with one change against the classical
axiomatics --- the cascade property is stated in its weakest defensible form, as a
two-parameter \emph{hemigroup}
\[
  \Phi_{y,z}\,\Phi_{x,y} \;=\; \Phi_{x,z}, \qquad 0 \le x \le y \le z,
\]
rather than a one-parameter semigroup $\Phi_\tau \Phi_{\tau'} = \Phi_{\tau+\tau'}$. Under
these axioms the admissible kernel families are completely characterized
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}): they are exactly
\[
  \phi_x(t) \;=\; \Lap^{-1}\bigl[\,e^{-F(xs)}\,\bigr](t), \qquad
  F(s) = b_0\,s + \int_0^\infty\bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt,
  \quad k \ \text{nonincreasing},
\]
the Laplace exponents of the \emph{self-decomposable} laws --- equivalently, the kernels are
the marginal densities of self-similar additive (Sato) subordinators. The class contains
members with all moments finite. The simplest of these, and the one the implementation half
of the paper builds on, is the Gamma family
\[
  \phi_x(t) \;=\; \frac{t^{\gamma-1}\,e^{-t/x}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\,x^{\gamma}},
\]
attractive on every practical axis at once: its mean delay is finite, $\gamma x$; its tails
are exponential; its scale covariance is exact and continuous; and it is computed by a
cascade of first-order recursive filters.

The classical constructions are recovered inside this class, and their limitations are
located by it. The Gaussian scale space that answers the corresponding spatial question
\sscite{iijima1962basic,lindeberg2011generalized} is unavailable in time --- the Gaussian
aperture is non-causal, and its causal truncations destroy the cascade structure --- and the
known time-causal constructions each concede one desideratum. The scale-time of
\sscite{koenderink1988scale} applies Gaussian smoothing to a logarithmically remapped past,
achieving causality but not time-recursivity. The Poisson scale space of
\sscite{lindeberg1996scalespace} is causal and time-recursive but carries no dilation covariance
of any span: its fixed elementary delay is a preferred time unit. The axiomatization of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} --- hereafter \emph{the semigroup theory} --- imposes full
continuous covariance together with the semigroup cascade and obtains a complete answer,
the kernels being exactly the extremal stable densities $\Lap^{-1}[e^{-\tau s^\alpha}]$,
$0 < \alpha < 1$; but every member has infinite mean delay. The time-causal limit kernel of
\sscite{lindeberg2016time} restores
finite moments through a recursive cascade of first-order integrators, with covariance
established there under the discrete subgroup of rescalings $\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$.

The single change of axiom is where all of this resolves. The semigroup form of the cascade
formalizes ``a measurement of a measurement is a measurement'' together with a second, tacit
assumption: that the composite depends only on the total \emph{amount} of scale --- that
measurement stages are interchangeable (\S\ref{sec:measurement-cascade}). The hemigroup form
keeps the first assumption and drops the second; every other axiom --- causality, linearity,
positivity, unit area, continuity, translation covariance, and \emph{full continuous} scale
covariance --- is retained verbatim, and the derivation of the semigroup theory is rerun in
the same order with the same tools: Laplace transforms, two functional equations, and the
Bernstein-function calculus. The analogues of its Theorems~3--5 are numbered $3'$--$5'$
below. The extremal stable family is precisely the sub-case recovered when
stage-interchangeability is reimposed (Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case}), and the heavy
tails are thereby exposed as the price of that tacit assumption, not of causality and
covariance (Remark~\ref{rem:heavy-tails}).

The second half of the paper develops the theory over the memory line, and the embodiment
principle of the semigroup theory, \S5 --- the observer cannot revisit its past, so whatever
of the signal's history is to influence the present output must be carried in the observer's
own state --- appears as theorem content. The signaling form, Theorem~$4'$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}), inverts the evolution across scale into an evolution in
present time over the memory half-line,
\[
  \partial_t u \;=\; A\,u, \qquad u(t, 0) = f(t),
\]
with the signal entering as boundary data at zero scale. The operator $A$ is constructed in
\S\ref{sec:signaling} by harmonic analysis in the scale variable, and it is the unique
operator of its kind; the construction comes with an exact account of what the memory holds.
At each instant, the memory line stores the analytic family of Riemann--Liouville integrals
of the past signal: the observer's embodiment of its past is fractional integration.

Two things follow. The temporal $N$-jet (\S\ref{sec:jet}), the causal analogue of the
derivative-of-Gaussian receptive fields, is an instantaneous readout of that same state,
\[
  \partial_t^n u \;=\; A^n u :
\]
differentiation is a re-indexing of the embodied record --- for the Gamma family, a binomial
subtraction of coexisting filter states --- and no second memory is needed to differentiate.
And the locality theorem, Theorem~$5'$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}), classifies the
memory-line media: locality of the operator alone admits a ladder of every differential
order, and the positive maximum principle closes the ladder at order two, where it leaves
the one-parameter Bessel family
\[
  A \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial_x^2 + \frac{\beta}{x}\,\partial_x ,
\]
unfolding the isolated heat case of the semigroup theory's Theorem~5, with the drift weight
$\beta$ buying any prescribed finite number of kernel moments. On the implementation side
(\S\ref{sec:implementation}) the hemigroup itself is the algorithm: the discrete cascade
over any set of scale knots reproduces the continuum family exactly at the knots, and finite
Gamma cascades approximate the entire completely-monotone-kernel subclass from inside the
axiom class. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} carries Theorem~$3'$, the scale-Cauchy
problem (Theorem~\ref{thm:scale-cauchy}) --- a fractional relaxation equation across scale
whose memory kernel is the function $k$ of the representation itself --- together with the
Sonine conservation identity, by which the infinitesimal smoothing applied at each scale is
exactly invertible: no step of the cascade destroys information.

One paragraph on the relation to the existing time-causal scale-space theory, since a
complete time-causal, time-recursive construction already exists in the literature
\sscite{lindeberg2016time}. The relation is containment, not competition
(Remark~\ref{rem:strata}): the axioms without any covariance give the Bernstein hemigroups,
which contain the Poisson scale space; covariance under the discrete subgroup $q^{\ZZ}$
gives the semi-selfdecomposable stratum; and full covariance gives the present class. The
time-causal limit kernel itself has a completely monotone memory kernel, so it is a fully
covariant member of the class characterized here --- in retrospect, the first-discovered
member: a construction that preceded the classification explaining it. What this paper adds
is the classification, the continuous-covariance guarantee for the whole class, and the
structure that comes with it --- the signaling form, the locality classification, and the
exactness of the cascade at arbitrary knots.

The paper is purely temporal; composition with a spatial scale space is orthogonal to
everything done here. The organization follows the derivation. \S\ref{sec:measurement}
states and motivates the axioms; \S\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and the
Bernstein-function toolbox; \S\S\ref{sec:representation}--\ref{sec:covariance} derive the
convolution representation, the infinite divisibility of the exponents, and the similarity
form forced by covariance; \S\ref{sec:characterization} proves the characterization
theorem; \S\ref{sec:examples} gives examples and moment formulas; \S\ref{sec:signaling} the
Mellin signaling form; \S\ref{sec:locality} the locality theorem;
\S\ref{sec:implementation} implementation; \S\ref{sec:jet} the temporal jet;
\S\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes, with four open problems.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} develops the memory-kernel and Sonine structure and the
scale-Cauchy problem.

\subsection{What is machine-checked}\label{sec:machine-checked}

The central results of this paper have been formalized in the Lean~4 proof assistant, on top
of its mathematical library Mathlib, and this subsection says exactly which, resting on
exactly what. It is self-contained: nothing below depends on reading the formal development.

\paragraph{What is proved.} Both main theorems are machine-checked in full.
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}, the characterization: a family
$(\Phi_{x,y})$ satisfies (A1)--(A8) and (ND) if and only if it is convolution by the kernels
of an exponent of the form (7.1), read through a gauge --- together with the uniqueness of
the pair (gauge, exponent). All three parts are verified: the construction, the analysis,
and the uniqueness.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}, the signaling form: the profiles $H(s\,\cdot)$ are
eigenfunctions of the inversion operator $A$ with eigenvalue $s$; the field solves the
signaling problem, in its Laplace and its Mellin form; and $A$ is the unique inversion in
the covariant Mellin class.
\end{itemize}

Everything \S\S\ref{sec:representation}--\ref{sec:covariance} contribute to the
characterization is verified with it: the convolution representation, the additivity and
infinite divisibility of the exponents (Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}), and the
similarity form forced by covariance. In the appendix, the memory kernel, its transform, the
Sonine conservation identity $\kappa^{(x)} * \ell^{(x)} = \mathrm{Leb}$ and the existence
and uniqueness of the potential kernel are verified. Of \S\ref{sec:signaling}, every
numbered item is. Of \S\ref{sec:locality}, the log-convexity of the moment function
(Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity}), the vanishing $B(0) = 0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1)) and the uniqueness of the log-convex solution of the
moment recursion in the order-2 case (Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}) are.

\paragraph{What it rests on.} Two analytic facts are taken from the literature rather than
reproved. They are the \emph{only} things the verified results assume beyond the logical
foundations of the proof assistant itself, and each is cited with a page reference checked
against the source.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Existence of a subordinator from its triple.} Given a drift $b_0 \ge 0$ and a
Lévy measure $\nu$ with $\int (1 \wedge t)\,\nu(dt) < \infty$, there is a probability
measure on $[0,\infty)$ whose Laplace transform is
$\exp[-(b_0 s + \int (1 - e^{-st})\nu(dt))]$ (\sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Thm.~5.2,
p.~49). This is what turns an admissible exponent into actual kernels, and it is the only
thing the constructive direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} needs that is
not proved here.
\item \emph{Self-decomposability, in the analysis direction.} If every dilation increment
$F(b\,\cdot) - F(a\,\cdot)$ of an exponent is again an exponent, then its Lévy measure has a
nonincreasing density $k(t)/t$ (\sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Prop.~5.17, p.~57). This is
the implication (1)~$\Rightarrow$~(3) of Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, and it
is the only thing the analysis direction needs that is not proved here.
\end{enumerate}

The two are used in \emph{different} halves and neither borrows the other's: the
construction and the uniqueness use only (1); the analysis direction uses only (2). That
separation is checked mechanically rather than claimed. The first is expected to be
temporary --- it is a construction, and carrying it out would make it a lemma; the second is
not, for the reason in the next paragraph.

\paragraph{Why these two and not more.} The classical route to these results passes through
the theory of completely monotone and Bernstein functions --- the Bernstein--Widder theorem,
closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits, uniqueness of the Lévy--Khintchine triple,
composition rules. Formalizing led us to replace five arguments by shorter ones that use the
\emph{representation} (7.1) directly, and those five are now the proofs printed in this
paper: Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein} extracts the limiting triple from a weak limit
instead of invoking closure; Lemma~\ref{lem:potential-kernel} constructs the potential
kernel as a subordinator's potential measure instead of representing it by
Bernstein--Widder; and three proofs in \S\ref{sec:signaling} lose an appeal to Stirling's
formula, to the semigroup property of the Riemann--Liouville family, and to Mellin
uniqueness respectively. What survives is exactly the place where the derivative-sign
definition is irreducible: $B(s) = sF'(s)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} has
no meaning until the representation is in hand, which is why fact~(2) above is expected to
be permanent. The same policy runs in the other direction: classical results of structural
rather than analytic character are proved, not cited. Thus the convolution representation of
\S\ref{sec:representation}, classically Wendel's theorem \sscite{wendel1952left}, is proved
from scratch rather than admitted as a third interface; the citation would buy little in any
case, since the sharpening the axioms force --- a probability measure carried by the
half-line --- is most of the argument (Remark~\ref{rem:wendel}).

\paragraph{What is not formalized, and why.} The scale-Cauchy problem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:scale-cauchy}) is not, for a reason external to this paper: it needs the
theory of $C_0$-semigroups and closed operators, which Mathlib does not yet carry. Two
results in the appendix --- the distributional scale-evolution identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-evolution}) and the exact-inversion corollary --- need
distributions paired with measures, likewise absent.
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-regularity}(2), the characterization of when $\kappa^{(x)}$ has a
completely monotone density, is a statement \emph{about} the CM class, which the formal
development deliberately does not define; it is cited, not verified.
\S\S\ref{sec:examples} and \ref{sec:implementation}--\ref{sec:jet} are examples and
implementation, formalized only where they were cheap.

\S\ref{sec:locality} is a mixed case and worth separating out, because the obstruction there
is narrower than it first appears. Three of its lemmas are verified --- the log-convexity of
the moment function, the vanishing $B(0) = 0$, and the uniqueness of the log-convex solution
of the moment recursion in the order-2 case --- and none of them needed anything Mathlib
lacks. That last one deserves a word, because it removes a citation the theorem was stated
to rest on: Theorem~\ref{thm:locality} applies the recursion $m(z{+}1) = Q(z)m(z)$ only
where $Q$ is linear, and there the Krull--Webster uniqueness theorem is the classical
Bohr--Mollerup theorem, which is available; the order-$n$ ladder of
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-ladder} has $n-1$ Gamma factors and does still need the general
result. What remains unverified in \S\ref{sec:locality} is therefore
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}, where an operator must be compared with a
differential expression on $C_c^\infty$ --- available but laborious; Courrège's
classification, that a local operator satisfying the positive maximum principle has order at
most two, which is cited; and the Bessel identifications, which need $K_\nu$. So the gap is
one citation, one laborious lemma, and one special function --- not an absent theory.
