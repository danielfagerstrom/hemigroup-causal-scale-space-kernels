\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

This paper characterizes the time-causal scale spaces: the families of smoothing operators
through which an uncommitted observer can measure a temporal signal at all scales at once.
A visual or auditory front end measures its input before anything is known about what the
signal will contain, so it must hold records at every scale simultaneously; and in time it
must do so causally --- the future is unavailable --- and out of its own state --- the past
cannot be revisited. The spatial form of this programme is Gaussian scale space; this paper
settles the temporal form. Four questions organize everything that follows: which
classical assumption has to give; what replaces the stable kernels once it does; what the
enlarged class contains, and at what price; and which classical properties survive the
enlargement, in what form.

\emph{The assumption.} The axioms are those of causal, covariant measurement, with one
change against the classical axiomatics. The semigroup form of the cascade property,
$\Phi_\tau \Phi_{\tau'} = \Phi_{\tau+\tau'}$, formalizes ``a measurement of a measurement
is a measurement'' together with a second, tacit assumption: that the composite depends
only on the total \emph{amount} of scale crossed --- that measurement stages are
interchangeable (\S\ref{sec:measurement-cascade}). This paper keeps the first assumption
and drops the second, stating the cascade in its weakest defensible form, as a
two-parameter \emph{hemigroup},
\[
  \Phi_{y,z}\,\Phi_{x,y} \;=\; \Phi_{x,z}, \qquad 0 \le x \le y \le z
\]
(Figure~\ref{fig:cascade}). Every other axiom --- causality, linearity, positivity, unit
area, continuity, translation covariance, and \emph{full continuous} scale covariance ---
is retained, restated only as the two-parameter setting requires: scale covariance with
the relabelling of the scale axis left free (\S\ref{sec:measurement-covariance}), and the
extended point as the diagonal condition $\Phi_{x,x} = \Id$. One further difference is
disclosed where it matters (Remarks~\ref{rem:axiom-provenance}
and~\ref{rem:drift-boundary}): the kernels are no longer required to be continuous
functions, which admits a degenerate pure-delay member. The derivation of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} --- hereafter \emph{the semigroup
theory} --- is then rerun in the same order with the same tools: Laplace transforms, two
functional equations, and the Bernstein-function calculus. A numbering convention, used
throughout and self-contained: a theorem numbered with a prime --- $3'$ (the scale-Cauchy
problem), $4'$ (the signaling form), $5'$ (locality) --- is the hemigroup analogue of the
correspondingly numbered theorem of the semigroup theory, and the text restates each
recovered classical statement at the point where it recovers it, so the correspondence
can be read without the 2005 paper at hand.

\emph{What replaces the stable family.} Under these axioms the admissible kernel families
are completely characterized (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}): in the canonical
scale parametrization fixed in \S\ref{sec:covariance}, they are exactly
\[
  \phi_x(t) \;=\; \Lap^{-1}\bigl[\,e^{-F(xs)}\,\bigr](t), \qquad
  F(s) = b_0\,s + \int_0^\infty\bigl(1 - e^{-st}\bigr)\,\frac{k(t)}{t}\,dt,
  \quad k \ \text{nonincreasing},
\]
the Laplace exponents of the \emph{self-decomposable} laws --- the delays $T$ that shrink
into themselves, $T \stackrel{d}{=} cT + R_c$ with $R_c$ independent, for every
$c \in (0,1)$ --- equivalently, the kernels are the marginal laws of self-similar additive
(Sato) subordinators. When stage-interchangeability is reimposed, the recovered sub-case
is the extremal stable family together with the pure delay at its boundary
(Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case}), and the heavy tails of the stable kernels are
thereby exposed as the price of that tacit assumption, not of causality and covariance
(Remark~\ref{rem:heavy-tails}).

\emph{What the class contains, and at what price.} The class contains members with all
moments finite. The simplest of these, and the one the implementation half of the paper
builds on, is the Gamma family
\[
  \phi_x(t) \;=\; \frac{t^{\gamma-1}\,e^{-t/x}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\,x^{\gamma}},
\]
whose mean delay is finite, $\gamma x$; whose tails are exponential; whose scale covariance
is exact and continuous; and which is computed, for integer $\gamma$, by a cascade of
first-order recursive filters. The price is stated before the consequences are: what the
Gamma family gives up is locality of its memory-line medium. Its signaling operator is
an integral operator, not a differential one. The trade-off between finite moments
and a local medium is not an accident of the example; it is classified by the locality
theorem (\S\ref{sec:locality}).

The classical constructions are recovered inside this class, and their limitations are
located by it. The Gaussian scale space that answers the corresponding spatial question
\sscite{iijima1962basic,lindeberg2011generalized} is unavailable in time: the Gaussian
aperture is non-causal, and its causal truncations destroy the cascade structure. The
known time-causal constructions each concede one desideratum. The scale-time of
\sscite{koenderink1988scale} applies Gaussian smoothing to a logarithmically remapped past,
achieving causality but not time-recursivity. The Poisson scale space of
\sscite{lindeberg1996scalespace} is causal and time-recursive but carries no dilation covariance
of any span: its fixed elementary delay is a preferred time unit. The semigroup theory
imposes full
continuous covariance together with the semigroup cascade and obtains a complete answer,
the kernels being exactly the extremal stable densities $\Lap^{-1}[e^{-\tau s^\alpha}]$,
$0 < \alpha < 1$; but every member has infinite mean delay. The time-causal limit kernel of
\sscite{lindeberg2016time} restores
finite moments through a recursive cascade of first-order integrators, with covariance
established there under the discrete subgroup of rescalings $\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$.

The second half of the paper develops the theory over the memory line. The embodiment
principle of the semigroup theory (\S5 of that paper; \S\ref{sec:measurement} motivates it)
--- the observer must carry its usable past in its own state --- appears as theorem
content. Under a mild standing hypothesis on the exponent, the signaling form,
Theorem~$4'$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}), inverts the evolution across scale into an
evolution in present time over the memory half-line,
\[
  \partial_t u \;=\; A\,u, \qquad u(t, 0) = f(t),
\]
with the signal entering as boundary data at zero scale. That hypothesis is (H),
Definition~\ref{def:standing-hypothesis} in \S\ref{sec:signaling}; every family of
\S\ref{sec:examples} satisfies it except the Gamma family at small shape. The operator $A$ is constructed in
\S\ref{sec:signaling} by harmonic analysis in the scale variable, and the problem is
well posed. $A$ is the unique inversion in its covariance class
(Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}(3)). The solution exists and is unique in an explicit
class, with the classical period-one ambiguity closed by a Bohr--Mollerup-style rigidity
(Corollary~\ref{cor:signaling-wellposed}). And it depends continuously on the data,
nonexpansively and uniformly over scales, and on the exponent itself
(Corollary~\ref{cor:signaling-hadamard}). The construction comes with an exact account of what the memory holds.
At each instant, the memory line stores the analytic family of Riemann--Liouville integrals
of the past signal: the observer's embodiment of its past is fractional integration.

Two things follow. The temporal $N$-jet (\S\ref{sec:jet}), the causal analogue of the
derivative-of-Gaussian receptive fields, is an instantaneous readout of that same state,
\[
  \partial_t^n u \;=\; A^n u :
\]
differentiation is a re-indexing of the embodied record, for the Gamma family a binomial
subtraction of coexisting filter states. No second memory is needed to differentiate,
up to an order bound set by the same hypothesis (H). And the locality theorem, Theorem~$5'$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}), classifies the memory-line media: locality of the operator
alone admits a ladder of every differential order, and the positive maximum principle closes
the ladder at order two, where it leaves a degenerate transport member and the
one-parameter Bessel family
\[
  A \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial_x^2 + \frac{\beta}{x}\,\partial_x ,
\]
unfolding the isolated heat case of the semigroup theory's Theorem~5, with the drift weight
$\beta$ buying any prescribed finite number of kernel moments.

The fourth question --- which classical properties survive, and in what form --- has its
sharpest instance in the oldest axiom of the subject, and there it is settled in both
directions (\S\ref{sec:examples}). Scale-monotone non-creation --- every coarser record
showing no more sign changes than every finer one --- is \emph{impossible}: an admissible
family whose increments are all variation-diminishing is the pure delay
(Proposition~\ref{prop:vd-impossibility}). What can exist is the endpoint form --- no
scale shows more sign changes than the signal itself --- and it is classified and
attained: the members with variation-diminishing kernels are exactly drift plus integer
Thorin atoms of finite mean, the Gamma family foremost. The classical obstruction,
that no causal convolution \emph{semigroup} can be variation-diminishing at all, is
thereby dissolved exactly as far as it can be. Discrete covariance retains more: along a
geometric ladder the limit kernel's rung increments are variation-diminishing, and there
the counts are monotone from scale to scale (Remark~\ref{rem:ladder-vd}). And along a
feature's own delay path the field is
non-enhancing at past-dominating extrema, with the echo of a preceding event as the sharp
failure mode (Corollary~\ref{cor:past-dominating}).

On the implementation side
(\S\ref{sec:implementation}) the hemigroup itself is the algorithm: the discrete cascade
over any set of scale knots reproduces the continuum family exactly at the knots. For the
Gamma family each increment is a pole--zero filter section whose zero moves with the finer
scale, and the zeros are exactly what the hemigroup adds. Finite Gamma cascades
approximate the entire completely-monotone-kernel subclass from inside the axiom class, with
rational cascade realizations at integer weights. The whole architecture --- cascade, jets,
and the qualitative predictions --- is exercised end to end in a numerical experiment
(Example~\ref{ex:numerical-experiment}).
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} carries Theorem~$3'$, the scale-Cauchy
problem (Theorem~\ref{thm:scale-cauchy}): a fractional relaxation equation across scale
whose memory kernel is the function $k$ of the representation itself. It also carries the
Sonine conservation identity, by which the memory operator applied at each scale has an
explicit inverse, the potential kernel; a Volterra equation that derives the kernels from
$k$ by forward integration, with no Laplace inversion; and the pointwise non-enhancement
corollary above.

One paragraph on the relation to the existing time-causal scale-space theory, since a
complete time-causal, time-recursive construction already exists in the literature
\sscite{lindeberg2016time}. The relation is containment, not competition
(Remark~\ref{rem:strata}): the axioms without any covariance give the Bernstein hemigroups,
which contain the Poisson scale space; covariance under the discrete subgroup $q^{\ZZ}$
gives the semi-selfdecomposable stratum; and full covariance gives the present class. The
time-causal limit kernel itself has a completely monotone memory kernel, so it is a fully
covariant member of the class characterized here, and of its variation-diminishing
subclass --- in
retrospect, the first-discovered
member: a construction that preceded the classification explaining it. Even the scale-time
aperture is contained at the kernel level: pulled back to the time axis it is the lognormal
density, a generalized gamma convolution by Thorin's theorem, hence an admissible
Thorin-subclass kernel --- only its log-domain composition structure lies outside the
axioms (Remark~\ref{rem:strata}).

The contributions, each with the section that carries it:
\begin{itemize}
\item the characterization of the time-causal scale spaces under the hemigroup axioms ---
  exactly the self-decomposable Laplace exponents, with the gauge and exponent unique
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}, \S\ref{sec:characterization});
\item continuous scale covariance for the whole class, with the discrete cascade exact at
  every set of scale knots (\S\ref{sec:implementation});
\item the signaling form: the scale evolution inverted into an evolution in present time,
  with the unique covariant inversion and the exact account of what the memory holds
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}, \S\ref{sec:signaling});
\item the classification of the local, positivity-respecting memory-line media --- the
  Bessel family, closing the ladder at order two (Theorem~\ref{thm:locality},
  \S\ref{sec:locality});
\item non-creation settled in both directions: the scale-monotone form impossible for
  every nondegenerate member, the endpoint form classified and attained by the
  variation-diminishing members, and non-enhancement along the feature's
  delay path (\S\ref{sec:examples}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory});
\item the temporal $N$-jet as an instantaneous readout of the embodied state
  (\S\ref{sec:jet});
\item machine-checked proofs of the central results, resting on two cited analytic facts
  (\S\ref{sec:machine-checked}).
\end{itemize}

The paper is purely temporal; composition with a spatial scale space is orthogonal to
everything done here. Its contribution is mathematical foundations together with an exact
implementation route: no empirical claims about natural signals are made, and the one
numerical experiment (\S\ref{sec:jet}) checks numbered results rather than applications.
The signal model is $L^1$, where the transform apparatus lives; the pointwise extension of
the smoothing to bounded measurable signals --- the experiment's persistent step among
them --- is recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:signal-model}.
The organization follows the derivation. \S\ref{sec:measurement}
states and motivates the axioms; \S\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and the
Bernstein-function toolbox; \S\S\ref{sec:representation}--\ref{sec:covariance} derive the
convolution representation, the infinite divisibility of the exponents, and the similarity
form forced by covariance; \S\ref{sec:characterization} proves the characterization
theorem; \S\ref{sec:examples} gives examples and moment formulas; \S\ref{sec:signaling} the
Mellin signaling form; \S\ref{sec:locality} the locality theorem;
\S\ref{sec:implementation} implementation; \S\ref{sec:jet} the temporal jet;
\S\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes, with four open problems.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} develops the memory-kernel and Sonine structure and the
scale-Cauchy problem.

Two fast tracks, for two readers. The practitioner can take \S\ref{sec:measurement} for
the axioms and the statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}, then go directly
to \S\S\ref{sec:implementation}--\ref{sec:jet} --- the exact cascade, the Thorin banks,
the jets, and the numerical experiment --- accepting the intermediate theory on faith. The
reader here for the mathematics can take
\S\S\ref{sec:representation}--\ref{sec:characterization} for the characterization,
\S\ref{sec:signaling} for the Mellin solution theory, and \S\ref{sec:locality} with
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} for the media and the scale-Cauchy problem, skipping
the implementation sections entirely. \S\ref{sec:examples} serves both.

\subsection{What is machine-checked}\label{sec:machine-checked}

The central results of this paper have been formalized in the Lean~4 proof assistant, on top
of its mathematical library Mathlib, and this subsection says exactly which, resting on
exactly what. It is self-contained: nothing below depends on reading the formal development.

\paragraph{What is proved.} Both main theorems are machine-checked in full.
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}, the characterization: a family
$(\Phi_{x,y})$ satisfies (A1)--(A8) and (ND) if and only if it is convolution by the kernels
of an exponent of the form (7.1) --- the representation displayed at the start of this
introduction --- read through a gauge, an increasing reparametrization of the scale axis
(\S\ref{sec:covariance}); together with the uniqueness of the pair (gauge, exponent). All three parts are verified: the construction, the analysis,
and the uniqueness.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}, the signaling form: the profiles $H(s\,\cdot)$ are
eigenfunctions of the inversion operator $A$ with eigenvalue $s$; the field solves the
signaling problem, in its Laplace and its Mellin form; and $A$ is the unique inversion in
the covariant Mellin class.
\end{itemize}

Everything \S\S\ref{sec:representation}--\ref{sec:covariance} contribute to the
characterization is verified with it: the convolution representation, the additivity and
infinite divisibility of the exponents (Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}), and the
similarity form forced by covariance. In the appendix, the memory kernel, its transform, the
Sonine conservation identity $\kappa^{(x)} * \ell^{(x)} = \mathrm{Leb}$, the existence,
uniqueness and scaling of the potential kernel, the Volterra equation (V.1) with its
uniqueness clause, the delay-derivative lemma, and the Phillips form of the generator with
all five clauses of Lemma~\ref{lem:generator-properties} --- the last interface-free ---
are verified. Of \S\ref{sec:signaling}, every
numbered result through the signaling theorem is; the well-posedness arc that follows it
--- Definition~\ref{def:mellin-solution} through
Corollary~\ref{cor:signaling-hadamard} --- and the boundary-growth
Lemma~\ref{lem:zstar-log-growth} are paper-only. Of \S\ref{sec:locality}, the classification step
(Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}, both directions), the log-convexity of the moment
function (Lemma~\ref{lem:log-convexity}), the vanishing $B(0) = 0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-recursion}(1)) and the uniqueness of the log-convex solution of the
moment recursion in the order-2 case (Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma-recursion-uniqueness}) are.

\paragraph{What it rests on.} Two analytic facts are taken from the literature rather than
reproved. They are the \emph{only} things the verified results assume beyond the logical
foundations of the proof assistant itself, and each is cited with a page reference checked
against the source.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Existence of a subordinator from its triple.} Given a drift $b_0 \ge 0$ and a
Lévy measure $\nu$ with $\int (1 \wedge t)\,\nu(dt) < \infty$, there is a probability
measure on $[0,\infty)$ whose Laplace transform is
$\exp[-(b_0 s + \int (1 - e^{-st})\nu(dt))]$ (\sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Thm.~5.2,
p.~49). This is what turns an admissible exponent into actual kernels, and it is the only
thing the constructive direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} needs that is
not proved here.
\item \emph{Self-decomposability, in the analysis direction.} If every dilation increment
$F(b\,\cdot) - F(a\,\cdot)$ of an exponent is again an exponent, then its Lévy measure has a
nonincreasing density $k(t)/t$ (\sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Prop.~5.17, p.~57). This is
the implication (1)~$\Rightarrow$~(3) of Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, and it
is the only thing the analysis direction needs that is not proved here.
\end{enumerate}

The two are used in \emph{different} halves and neither borrows the other's: the
construction and the uniqueness use only (1); the analysis direction uses only (2). That
separation is checked mechanically rather than claimed. The first is expected to be
temporary --- it is a construction, and carrying it out would make it a lemma; the second is
not, for the reason in the next paragraph.

\paragraph{Why these two and not more.} The classical route to these results passes through
the theory of completely monotone and Bernstein functions --- the Bernstein--Widder theorem,
closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits, uniqueness of the Lévy--Khintchine triple,
composition rules. Formalizing led us to replace five arguments by shorter ones that use the
\emph{representation} (7.1) directly, and those five are now the proofs printed in this
paper; \S\ref{sec:conclusions} names them. What survives is exactly the place where the derivative-sign
definition is irreducible: $B(s) = sF'(s)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} has
no meaning until the representation is in hand, which is why fact~(2) above is expected to
be permanent. The same policy runs in the other direction: classical results of structural
rather than analytic character are proved, not cited. Thus the convolution representation of
\S\ref{sec:representation}, classically Wendel's theorem \sscite{wendel1952left}, is proved
from scratch rather than admitted as a third interface; the citation would buy little in any
case, since the sharpening the axioms force --- a probability measure carried by the
half-line --- is most of the argument (Remark~\ref{rem:wendel}).

\paragraph{What is not formalized, and why.} The scale-Cauchy problem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:scale-cauchy}) is not, though the obstruction is narrower than it was
once thought to be: its proof is a scalar ODE per Laplace frequency and invokes no
$C_0$-semigroup results --- that theory, absent from Mathlib, is needed only by the
fixed-scale reading (Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-scale-semigroup}), which is cited. What
the theorem still needs is the distributional scale-evolution identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-evolution}), which --- like the exact-inversion corollary ---
requires distributions paired with measures, absent from Mathlib, together with the
vector-valued fundamental-theorem step.
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-regularity}(2), the characterization of when $\kappa^{(x)}$ has a
completely monotone density, is a statement \emph{about} the CM class, which the formal
development deliberately does not define; it is cited, not verified.
\S\S\ref{sec:examples} and \ref{sec:implementation}--\ref{sec:jet} are examples and
implementation, formalized only where they were cheap.

\S\ref{sec:locality} is a mixed case and worth separating out, because the obstruction there
is narrower than it first appears. Its structural spine is verified: the classification step
--- Lemma~\ref{lem:local-polynomial-symbol}, local operators are exactly the polynomial
symbols, in both directions --- together with the log-convexity of the moment function, the
vanishing $B(0) = 0$, and the uniqueness of the log-convex solution of the moment recursion
in the order-2 case. That last one deserves a word, because it removes a citation the
theorem was stated to rest on: Theorem~\ref{thm:locality} applies the recursion
$m(z{+}1) = Q(z)m(z)$ only where $Q$ is linear, and there the Krull--Webster uniqueness
theorem is the classical Bohr--Mollerup theorem, which is available; the order-$n$ ladder of
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-ladder} has $n-1$ Gamma factors and does still need the general
result. What remains cited in \S\ref{sec:locality} are the analytic interfaces --- Widder's
singularity theorem (behind $z_* = \infty$), Courrège's order bound, Krull--Webster for the
general ladder, and Bondesson's theory for the ladder's admissibility --- and the Bessel
identifications, which need $K_\nu$. So the gap is four citations and one special function
--- not an absent theory.
