\section{Convolution representation}\label{sec:representation}

The first step of the derivation uses no covariance and no cascade: the axioms
(A1)--(A5) alone force the measurement operators to be convolutions by probability
measures on $[0,\infty)$ --- the apertures promised in \S\ref{sec:measurement}, as
measures; where they have densities, and which, is settled once covariance and (ND) are
imposed (\S\ref{sec:characterization}). The lemma is an equivalence, and its converse is
what the constructive half of the characterization will later run backwards through.
Continuity (A7) is added only for the second lemma, which extracts from the measures the
one function the rest of the paper works with.

One device serves both lemmas. The exponential $e^{-st}$ is unbounded on $\RR$, so it is
not a functional on $X$; the \emph{clamped exponential} $w_s(t) := e^{-s\max(t,0)}$ is
bounded, hence in $X^*$, and agrees with $e^{-st}$ exactly where causal mass lives. For a
measure $\mu$ carried by $[0,\infty)$ and $f = 1_{(0,1)}$, one has
$\langle w_s, f(\cdot - r)\rangle = e^{-sr}\langle w_s, f\rangle$ for $r \ge 0$, so Fubini
collapses the pairing to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clamped}
  \langle w_s, \mu * f\rangle = \langle w_s, f\rangle\,\hat\mu(s),
  \qquad \langle w_s, f\rangle \ge e^{-s} > 0 :
\end{equation}
a single bounded functional reads off the Laplace transform of the kernel.

% shared with blueprint lem:convolution-representation
\begin{lemma}[representation]\label{lem:convolution-representation}
Under (A1)--(A5), for each pair $0 \le x \le y$ there is a unique probability measure
$\mu_{x,y}$ on $[0,\infty)$ with
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} f = \mu_{x,y} * f, \qquad f \in X,
\]
and conversely every such measure defines an operator satisfying (A1)--(A5).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(x,y)$ with $0 \le x \le y$ and write $\Phi = \Phi_{x,y}$. The plan is classical:
the representing measure ought to be $\Phi$ applied to a unit point mass, but $\delta_0$
is not in $L^1$, so $\Phi$ is applied to an approximate identity instead, a limit measure
is extracted by compactness, and the limit is then checked to represent $\Phi$; the named
steps follow that plan.

\emph{Convolution as a vector-valued integral.} For $f \in L^1$ and $g \in X$,
\[
  f * g = \int_\RR f(r)\,\shift_r g\,dr
\]
as a Bochner integral \emph{in $X$}: the integrand is continuous in $r$, because translation
acts continuously on $L^1$, and is dominated by $|f(r)|\,\|g\|_1$. An $X$-valued integral
commutes with every bounded operator, so (A1) and (A2) give the interchange identity
\[
  \Phi(f * g) = f * \Phi g, \qquad f \in L^1,\ g \in X,
\]
which carries the existence argument; the remaining axioms enter where named.

\emph{The approximants.} Let $\rho_\varepsilon = \varepsilon^{-1} 1_{(0,\varepsilon)}$ and
$h_\varepsilon := \Phi \rho_\varepsilon$. By (A4) and (A5), $h_\varepsilon \ge 0$ with
$\int h_\varepsilon = 1$; by (A3) it vanishes a.e.\ on $(-\infty,0)$. So
$\nu_\varepsilon := h_\varepsilon\,dt$ is a probability measure carried by $[0,\infty)$.

\emph{Tightness.} By the interchange identity
$\rho_1 * h_\varepsilon = \Phi(\rho_1 * \rho_\varepsilon)$, and
$\rho_1 * \rho_\varepsilon \to \rho_1$ in $L^1$, so $\rho_1 * h_\varepsilon$ converges in $X$
as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ --- and a convergent sequence in $L^1$ has uniformly small tails.
Since $\rho_1 \ge 0$ is carried by $(0,1)$ and $h_\varepsilon \ge 0$, convolving can only move
mass to the right:
\[
  \int_T^\infty h_\varepsilon \le \int_T^\infty \rho_1 * h_\varepsilon .
\]
Along $\varepsilon_n = 1/(n+1)$ the right tails of $\nu_{\varepsilon_n}$ are therefore small
uniformly in $n$, and (A3) kills the left tail outright, so
$\{\nu_{\varepsilon_n}\}$ is tight, with compacts $[0,T]$.

\emph{The limit.} By Prokhorov's theorem some subsequence $\nu_{\varepsilon_{n_k}}$ converges
weakly to a probability measure $\mu$; and $(-\infty,0)$ being open, the portmanteau theorem
bounds $\mu((-\infty,0))$ by the lower limit of $\nu_{\varepsilon_{n_k}}((-\infty,0)) = 0$, so
$\supp \mu \subseteq [0,\infty)$. Both the mass and the support clause are thus part of the
extraction rather than separate arguments.

\emph{Identification.} Let $\Psi \in X^*$ and $f \in X$. The map $r \mapsto \Psi(\shift_r f)$
is continuous --- again because translation acts continuously on $L^1$ --- and bounded by
$\|\Psi\|\,\|f\|_1$, so it is a legitimate test function for weak convergence. Reading
$f * h_\varepsilon$ with $h_\varepsilon$ in the density slot and applying $\Psi$,
\[
  \Psi(f * h_\varepsilon) = \int_\RR \Psi(\shift_r f)\,\nu_\varepsilon(dr)
  \xrightarrow{\ k \to \infty\ } \int_\RR \Psi(\shift_r f)\,\mu(dr) = \Psi(\mu * f),
\]
the last equality because $\mu * f = \int \shift_r f\,\mu(dr)$ is again an $X$-valued Bochner
integral. On the other hand $f * h_\varepsilon = \Phi(f * \rho_\varepsilon) \to \Phi f$ in $X$,
by the interchange identity and (A1). Hence $\Psi(\Phi f) = \Psi(\mu * f)$ for every
$\Psi \in X^*$, and so $\Phi f = \mu * f$. Note that this holds for \emph{every} $f \in X$ at
once: nothing in the display is pointwise, so no restriction to $C_c$ and no density step is
needed.

\emph{Uniqueness.} Take $f = 1_{(0,1)}$ and pair $\mu * f$ against the clamped
exponential $w_s(t) = e^{-s\max(t,0)}$: by the factorisation~\eqref{eq:clamped},
$\langle w_s, \mu * f\rangle = \langle w_s, f\rangle\,\hat\mu(s)$ with
$\langle w_s, f\rangle > 0$. So $\Phi$ determines $\hat\mu$ on $[0,\infty)$, and a finite
measure on $[0,\infty)$ is determined by its Laplace transform
(Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness}). One test function suffices; no separating
family is needed.

\emph{Converse.} A probability measure $\mu$ on $[0,\infty)$ makes $f \mapsto \mu * f$ bounded
with norm $\le 1$ by Young's inequality (A1), translation-commuting because convolution is
(A2), support-preserving on $[t_0,\infty)$ because $\supp\mu \subseteq [0,\infty)$ (A3),
positive (A4), and mass-preserving by Tonelli (A5).
\end{proof}

The rest of the paper works not with the measures but with one function of them, their
\emph{exponent} $g_{x,y}(s) := -\log\hat\mu_{x,y}(s)$. The reason is the cascade: Laplace
transforms turn convolution into multiplication, so $-\log$ turns the composition of two
smoothings into the \emph{sum} of their exponents, and (A6) will read as additivity in
\S\ref{sec:cascade}. That section's argument also needs the exponent to depend continuously
on the pair of scales, so that a fine partition of $[x,y]$ gives a null array; this is what
the second lemma supplies, from (A7).

% shared with blueprint lem:transform-continuity
\begin{lemma}[continuity of transforms]\label{lem:transform-continuity}
Under (A1)--(A5) and (A7), the function
\[
  g_{x,y}(s) := -\log \hat\mu_{x,y}(s) \in [0,\infty), \qquad s \ge 0,
\]
is well defined, and $(x,y) \mapsto g_{x,y}(s)$ is continuous on $\{0 \le x \le y\}$ for each
$s \ge 0$; moreover $g_{x,y}(0) = 0$ and $s \mapsto g_{x,y}(s)$ is continuous on
$[0,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\hat\mu(s) \in (0, 1]$ for a probability measure on $[0,\infty)$ and $s \ge 0$: positive
because the integrand is, and at most $1$ because $e^{-st} \le 1$ where the mass sits. So $g$
is well defined and nonnegative, and $g_{x,y}(0) = -\log 1 = 0$. Continuity of
$s \mapsto \hat\mu(s)$ on $[0,\infty)$ is dominated convergence with the constant bound $1$
on the integrand. This is where causality is needed: $e^{-st}$ is bounded on the support of
$\mu$ only because that support lies in $[0,\infty)$.

For continuity in $(x,y)$, take $f = 1_{(0,1)}$ and pair against the clamped exponential
$w_s$; by~\eqref{eq:clamped},
\[
  \langle w_s, \Phi_{x,y} f\rangle
  = \langle w_s, \mu_{x,y} * f\rangle = \langle w_s, f\rangle\,\hat\mu_{x,y}(s).
\]
The left side is a bounded functional applied to $\Phi_{x,y} f$, which is continuous in $(x,y)$
on $\{0 \le x \le y\}$ by (A7); dividing by the constant $\langle w_s, f\rangle > 0$ gives
continuity of $\hat\mu_{x,y}(s)$, and composing with $-\log$, legitimate because the transform
never vanishes, gives it for $g_{x,y}(s)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the representation is, and what it is not]\label{rem:wendel}
Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation} is the Wendel step
(\sscite{wendel1952left}: the translation-invariant operators on $L^1$ of a group are exactly
convolution by a measure), specialised to $\RR$ and sharpened by (A3)--(A5) to a probability
measure carried by $[0,\infty)$. It is proved here rather than cited; the only imported
fact in this section is the uniqueness theorem for Laplace transforms,
Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-uniqueness}.

\emph{On the two routes.} The classical argument regards $h_\varepsilon\,dt$ as a bounded
family in $M(\RR) = C_0(\RR)^*$ and extracts a weak-$*$ subnet by Banach--Alaoglu, recovering
$\|\mu\| = 1$ from (A5) afterwards and reading the limit as a measure by Riesz--Markov. The
proof given above instead makes the family \emph{tight} --- which costs one extra observation,
that convolution with a density carried by $(0,1)$ only moves mass to the right --- and then
Prokhorov returns a probability measure carried by $[0,\infty)$ outright. The mass and support
clauses are then part of the extraction rather than separate arguments, no dual space appears,
and the weak-$*$ selection apparatus --- Banach--Alaoglu and Riesz--Markov --- is
bypassed. The second route is the one the formalisation follows, because it needs no dual
space: the library has Banach--Alaoglu but not the identification of $M(\RR)$ with
$C_0(\RR)^*$.

The identification step likewise has two forms. Testing against $C_0$ functions makes it
pointwise, which forces the restriction to $f \in C_c$ and a density argument afterwards.
Testing against $X^* = L^\infty$ makes it an identity in $X$, valid for every $f \in X$ at
once, and the price is only that $f * g$ must be read as an $X$-valued Bochner integral --- which
the proof needs anyway, for the interchange identity $\Phi(f * g) = f * \Phi g$.

The multiplicative-group counterpart of this step, for the scale variable rather than time,
is what \S\ref{sec:signaling} has to build by hand; there is no Wendel theorem to appeal to
once the group is $(0,\infty)$ under multiplication and the operator is required only to be
covariant.
\end{remark}
