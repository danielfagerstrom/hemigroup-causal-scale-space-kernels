\section{Implementation}\label{sec:implementation}

This section records how the scale spaces of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} are
computed, and compares the three practically distinguished corners --- Gamma, Bessel, stable
--- from the implementer's point of view. The story is a three-step reduction: the scale
direction can be sampled \emph{exactly}, for any family
(Proposition~\ref{prop:knot-exactness}); for the Gamma family the time direction then needs
only $\gamma$ first-order states per increment (Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-cascade}); and
the whole Thorin subclass --- every admissible family with completely monotone memory
kernel, the Bessel and stable corners included --- reduces to banks of such sections
(Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass}), leaving only the genuinely non-Thorin members to
direct convolution. The structural fact that organizes everything is the first step:
the \emph{scale direction never needs to be discretized approximately}:

\begin{proposition}[exactness at the knots]\label{prop:knot-exactness}
Let $0 = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_M$ be any finite set of scale knots and define the discrete
cascade
\[
  u_0 := f, \qquad u_{k+1} := \mu_{x_k,\,x_{k+1}} * u_k .
\]
Then $u_k = \Phi_{0, x_k} f$ \emph{exactly}, for every knot set; and refining the knot set
changes nothing at the old knots.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate induction from the hemigroup (A6): $\mu_{0,x_{k+1}} = \mu_{x_k, x_{k+1}} * \mu_{0,x_k}$.
\end{proof}

So the only discretization error in a digital implementation is the time discretization of
the increment filters, and intermediate scales can be added at any time without
recomputation error. This is a genuine advantage over discretizing the memory-line evolution
of \S\ref{sec:signaling}, where the scale direction carries truncation error; the cascade is
not an approximation scheme for the family but the family itself, sampled.

For an implementer the property to want next is \emph{rationality} of the increment
transfer: a rational transfer function is realized by finitely many first-order states,
time-recursively, with no stored history. The Gamma family has it
(Figure~\ref{fig:filterbank}).

\begin{proposition}[the Gamma cascade]\label{prop:gamma-cascade}
For the Gamma family with integer $\gamma$, the increment filter is rational:
\[
  \widehat{\mu}_{x_k, x_{k+1}}(s) \;=\; \left(\frac{1 + x_k\, s}{1 + x_{k+1}\, s}\right)^{\gamma},
\]
a cascade of $\gamma$ \emph{identical} first-order pole--zero sections (pole at $-1/x_{k+1}$,
zero at $-1/x_k$). Each section admits the one-state realization, free of input differentiation,
\[
  \dot w = \frac{y - w}{x_{k+1}}, \qquad
  v \;=\; \frac{x_k}{x_{k+1}}\, y \;+\; \Bigl(1 - \frac{x_k}{x_{k+1}}\Bigr)\, w,
\]
($y$ input, $v$ output), whose transfer is $(1 + x_k s)/(1 + x_{k+1} s)$. The output is a
\emph{convex combination} of input and state.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The increment transfer is the ratio of the endpoint transforms: by the hemigroup (A6),
$\widehat{\mu}_{0,x_{k+1}} = \widehat{\mu}_{x_k,x_{k+1}}\,\widehat{\mu}_{0,x_k}$, and
$\widehat{\mu}_{0,x}(s) = (1 + x s)^{-\gamma}$ is nonvanishing for $\operatorname{Re} s \ge 0$,
so dividing gives the display. The transfer identity of the section:
$v = \tfrac{x_k}{x_{k+1}} y + \bigl(1 - \tfrac{x_k}{x_{k+1}}\bigr)\tfrac{y}{1 + x_{k+1}s}
= y\,\tfrac{1 + x_k s}{1 + x_{k+1} s}$; the convex-combination clause is
$0 < x_k/x_{k+1} < 1$.
\end{proof}

The convex combination is what makes positivity and unit mass manifest at the realization
level. In discrete time, write $\Delta$ for the sample interval and $y_n := y(n\Delta)$; with
sampled-and-held input each section admits the exact exponential-integrator update
\[
  w_{n+1} = e^{-\Delta/x_{k+1}}\, w_n + \bigl(1 - e^{-\Delta/x_{k+1}}\bigr)\, y_n ,
\]
unconditionally stable (real pole in $(0,1)$) and free of overshoot and ringing. Each
\emph{section} is exact for zero-order-held input; the cascade is not exact end-to-end, the
input to the next section being the previous section's response rather than a held signal,
so time discretization is a genuine approximation --- what it preserves exactly is
positivity, unit mass and stability, the update being a convex combination; what it does not
preserve is the end-to-end kernel and covariance under dilations not commensurate with
$\Delta$. A full scale space over knots $x_1 < \cdots < x_M$ costs $\gamma M$ first-order
states and $O(\gamma M)$ multiply-adds per sample.

\begin{remark}[covariance in practice]\label{rem:covariance-practice}
With geometric knots $x_k = q^k x_0$ the \emph{continuum} cascade is carried to itself by
every rescaling $\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$, as a shift of the ladder; in the sampled system the
time constants are tied to the sample interval $\Delta$, so the shift is exact on the knot
set but not on the sampled filters. Because the underlying family exists at \emph{every}
scale, an arbitrary $\sigma$ is handled by instantiating the same family at the shifted
knots --- a new filter bank with new time constants and re-initialized state, not a
relabelling of the running one; the operation is principled exactly because the kernels
between knots belong to the family. This is the operational content of continuous
covariance, and the point of the pole--zero structure: a pure-pole cascade (truncated
exponentials with distinct time constants), the finite form of the time-causal limit-kernel
construction of \sscite{lindeberg2016time}, also marches a hemigroup, but its kernels are
hypoexponential laws whose family is \emph{not} dilation-closed, so only the discrete
covariance of the ladder itself survives. The zeros are what the hemigroup increments of the
Sato--Gamma process add, and they are exactly what buys the continuum.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[implementing the local corner]\label{rem:local-implementation}
The Bessel/inverse-gamma increments have transcendental transforms (ratios of Bessel-K
profiles), hence \emph{no} finite-dimensional time-recursive realization; the same holds for
the stable family ($e^{-(x_{k+1}^{\,\alpha} - x_k^{\,\alpha})\,s^\alpha}$ in the homogeneous
gauge). The implementation options are: (i) direct convolution against tabulated increment
kernels per knot --- burdened by the polynomial tails $t^{-1-a}$, so truncation error decays
only polynomially in the window length; (ii) marching the memory-line PDE, in the parabolic
gauge $\xi$ of Remark~\ref{rem:gauge-freedom},
$\partial_t u = \tfrac12 u_{\xi\xi} + \tfrac{\beta}{\xi}u_\xi$ in time (implicit in $\xi$, with
care at the degenerate boundary), which reintroduces scale-direction discretization error; or
(iii)
rational approximation of the irrational symbol, in the manner standard for
fractional-order elements in filter and control design (\sscite{oustaloup2000frequency}), with state count growing logarithmically in the required bandwidth ---
made systematic within the axioms in Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass} below. All three
are workable; none matches the cascade of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-cascade} in simplicity
or exactness. This is the practical face of Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}: locality of the
medium is bought at the price of heavy-tailed, non-rational kernels.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[finite differences on the memory line]\label{rem:finite-differences}
The explicit finite-difference scheme of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S\S6--7, for the
heat signaling equation extends verbatim to the Bessel family. Working in the parabolic gauge
$\xi$ of Remark~\ref{rem:gauge-freedom}, on the logarithmic grid $\eta = \log\xi$ the operator
is constant-coefficient up to a rate factor,
\[
  \tfrac12\,\partial_\xi^2 + \frac{\beta}{\xi}\,\partial_\xi
  \;=\; e^{-2\eta}\Bigl[\tfrac12\,\partial_\eta^2 + \bigl(\beta - \tfrac12\bigr)\,\partial_\eta\Bigr],
\]
so the stencil is uniform along the scale ladder and only the update rate varies with
position --- the discrete form of the Lamperti time change (\sscite{lamperti1972semi}). With
the first-order term upwinded, the explicit update matrix is sub-stochastic, so positivity
and non-amplification are automatic, and the discrete scheme is itself a hemigroup of
sub-probability kernels: a random-walk medium whose absorption-time laws are discrete
analogues of the inverse-gamma kernels. We record this for continuity with the 2005 paper but
do not recommend it: the explicit step is constrained by the finest cell,
$\Delta t \lesssim \xi_{\min}^2\,\Delta\eta^2$, while the coarse-scale dynamics live on
horizons $\sim \xi_{\max}^2$, so the cost of covering the ladder grows like
$(\xi_{\max}/\xi_{\min})^2$ (an implicit march trades this for per-sample band solves plus
additional discretization error);
the finite-moment regime $a > 1$, i.e.\ $\beta < -\tfrac12$ --- the very reason to prefer the
Bessel family --- is advection-dominated near the singular boundary and numerically the least
pleasant; and, decisively, any marching of the signaling equation approximates the scale
direction, which Proposition~\ref{prop:knot-exactness} shows can be sampled exactly. The
principled numerics for the local corner is instead the following.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[the Thorin subclass; gamma cascades approximate the local corner]\label{prop:thorin-subclass}
For an admissible $F$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}) the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the memory kernel $k$ is completely monotone --- the classical Sonine case of
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-regularity}(2);
\item $F$ has a \emph{Thorin representation}
\[
  F(s) \;=\; b_0\,s \;+\; \int_{(0,\infty)} \log\Bigl(1 + \frac{s}{\tau}\Bigr)\,U(d\tau),
  \qquad U \ge 0,\ \
  \int_{(0,1]} \log\tfrac{1}{\tau}\,U(d\tau) + \int_{(1,\infty)} \frac{U(d\tau)}{\tau} < \infty;
\]
\item the kernels are generalized gamma convolutions (GGC).
\end{enumerate}
In that case, for any atomic $U_N = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i\,\delta_{\tau_i}$ such that
$F_N(s) := b_0 s + \sum_i w_i \log(1 + s/\tau_i)$ converges to $F$ pointwise --- as it does
for the standard quadratures of $U$, the weights carrying the two integrability conditions
--- the exponents $F_N$ are \emph{themselves admissible}, finite multi-Gamma members of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}, and the kernel families converge weakly at every
scale; positivity and unit mass hold \emph{exactly at every truncation level}, the
approximation staying inside the axiom class rather than merely near it. For integer weights
the increments over any knot ladder are rational of order $\sum_i w_i$ up to the pure delay
$e^{-b_0(x_{k+1}-x_k)s}$ contributed by the drift --- itself exactly implementable as a
sample delay --- realized by the cascade of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-cascade} applied per
Thorin node, with time constants $x/\tau_i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2): by Bernstein's theorem (\sscite{schilling2012bernstein},
Thm.~1.4, p.~3), $k$ completely monotone means $k(t) = \int e^{-\tau t}\,U(d\tau)$ for a
positive measure $U$ on $[0,\infty)$, finite on compacts since the integral converges; an
atom of $U$ at $\tau = 0$ would put a constant component into $k$, making
$\int_1^\infty k(t)\,t^{-1}dt$ --- hence $F$ --- infinite, so $U$ lives on $(0,\infty)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:memory-kernel},
$F'(s) = b_0 + \int U(d\tau)/(s + \tau)$, and integrating in $s$ from $0$ gives the Thorin
form, the integrability condition being finiteness of $F(1)$ --- three regimes:
$\log(1+1/\tau) \asymp \log(1/\tau)$ as $\tau \downarrow 0$, $\asymp 1/\tau$ as
$\tau \to \infty$, and bounded on the compact middle, where $U$ is finite; conversely
differentiate.
(2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3) is Thorin's characterization of the GGC class
(\sscite{thorin1977pareto}, \sscite{thorin1977lognormal}). In the L\'evy-data form --- GGC
iff the L\'evy density $\ell$ has $y\,\ell(y)$ completely monotone, which is clause (1)
verbatim, $y\,\ell(y)$ being this paper's $k$ --- it is \sscite{bondesson1992generalized},
Thm.~3.1.1, p.~30; the exponent-side form is the Thorin--Bernstein class of
\sscite{schilling2012bernstein}, Thm.~8.2, p.~109.
Admissibility of $F_N$: $sF_N'(s) = b_0 s + \sum_i w_i\, s/(s + \tau_i)$ is a Bernstein
function, so Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents} applies and $F_N$ is of the form
(7.1); $F_N(\zp) = 0$, the integrability conditions are trivial for a finite sum, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization} makes the family admissible. Pointwise
convergence of $F_N$ gives convergence of the transforms $e^{-F_N(xs)}$, hence weak
convergence of the kernels by the continuity theorem
(Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace-continuity}).
\end{proof}

The four working families of this paper --- Gamma, Bessel, stable, and the gamma-product
ladder --- all lie in the Thorin subclass. The Gamma family is its
\emph{single-atom} member, $U = \gamma\,\delta_1$ --- the cascade of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-cascade} is literally the atomic case of
Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass}. The stable family has the explicit Thorin density
$U(d\tau) = \tfrac{\alpha \sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\,\tau^{\alpha - 1}\,d\tau$. The
inverse-gamma/Bessel family is GGC (\sscite{halgreen1979self};
\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}), with Thorin measure supported on the spectral cut of the
Bessel operator and density expressible through the Bessel functions of the first and second
kind, $u(\tau) \propto \bigl[\tau\,\bigl(J_a^2 + Y_a^2\bigr)\bigl(\sqrt{2\tau}\bigr)\bigr]^{-1}$
(\sscite{ismail1979special}), decaying like $\tau^{-1/2}$ at infinity in accordance with
$F(s) \sim \sqrt{2s}$. And the gamma-product ladder of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-ladder} is
GGC since $\HCM \subset \GGC$ (\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}). The conclusion deserves stating plainly: \textbf{the Gamma family is the implementation
substrate for the entire Thorin subclass} --- all four working families --- even when
Bessel semantics are wanted, the machine one builds is a bank of first-order Gamma sections.
And by Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}, the substrate is essentially the regularity
class of \S\ref{sec:examples}: both select drift plus integer Thorin atoms of finite mean,
the variation-diminishing property admitting countably many atoms where rationality keeps
finitely many.
Outside the subclass --- the non-completely-monotone kernels, the Dickman rays with their
echo singularities foremost --- no Gamma bank converges from inside the class, the GGC
class being closed under weak limits (\sscite{bondesson1992generalized}, p.~29), and an
implementer is left with direct convolution.

As a design heuristic: the Bessel Thorin
density is asymptotically a power law, hence scale-invariant over the covered band, so
logarithmically spaced nodes --- a fixed number per decade of bandwidth --- are the natural
quadrature; we state this as a heuristic, with no error bound claimed, which is the honest
form of the state-count claim of Remark~\ref{rem:local-implementation}(iii). In practice the
node data $(\tau_i, w_i)$ come either from quadrature of $U$ or from direct rational fitting
of the increment transfer functions; the Thorin structure guarantees that a fit with
\emph{positive} weights exists.

\begin{example}[numerical illustration]\label{ex:tail-contrast}
Figure~\ref{fig:kernels} compares the kernels of the three corners at matched delay: Gamma
kernels with $\gamma \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ and scale $x = 1/\gamma$ (mean delay $1$); the
Bessel/inverse-gamma kernel with $a = 2$ and inverse-gamma scale parameter $\vartheta = a - 1$ (mean delay $1$); and the
$\tfrac12$-stable kernel of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} normalized to median delay $1$,
its mean being infinite.


Panel (a) shows the bodies: at matched mean the Gamma kernels are the most concentrated,
sharpening with $\gamma$ (mode $(\gamma - 1)/\gamma \to$ mean), while the inverse-gamma
kernel buys its early sharp peak by exporting mass into the tail, and the stable kernel does
so to the point of losing the mean altogether; beyond $t \approx 2$--$3$ the ordering
reverses and the heavy-tailed members dominate. Panel (b) shows the mechanism: the Gamma
tails fall exponentially, the inverse-gamma and stable tails follow the powers $t^{-1-a}$ and
$t^{-3/2}$ of Remark~\ref{rem:trilemma}. The practical consequence is quantified by the
window length $T_p$ capturing a fraction $p$ of the kernel mass ($\int_0^{T_p}\phi = p$), the
quantity governing the truncation error of route (i) in
Remark~\ref{rem:local-implementation} (Table~\ref{tab:windows-mean}):

For the Gamma family each extra decade of captured mass costs a fixed additive window
increment (exponential tails); for the inverse-gamma with $a = 2$ it costs a factor
$10^{1/a} \approx 3.2$; for the $\tfrac12$-stable, of index $\alpha = \tfrac12$, a factor
$10^{1/\alpha} = 100$ --- capturing
$99.9\%$ of its mass requires a window five orders of magnitude longer than its median
delay. This is the quantitative content of the statement that the 2005 kernels are too
heavy-tailed for practical measurement, and of its resolution within the hemigroup class.

Since the stable mean is infinite, the delay proxy used in the influence-curve discussion of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, \S8, is the \emph{mode}, and Figure~\ref{fig:kernels-mode}
repeats the experiment in that normalization: matched mode delay $1$, i.e.\
$x = 1/(\gamma - 1)$ for the Gamma kernels ($\gamma = 1$ is omitted, its mode sitting at the
onset), $\vartheta = a + 1 = 3$ for the inverse-gamma, and $\vartheta = 3/2$ for the
$\tfrac12$-stable.
