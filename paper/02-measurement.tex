\section{Temporal measurement}\label{sec:measurement}

This section assembles the axiom system of the paper: a list of requirements on temporal
measurement, each motivated in turn and collected at the end in
Definition~\ref{def:cascade-family}. The substantive change from
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} is one axiom, the cascade property of
\S\ref{sec:measurement-cascade}, and it is the subject of this paper; the two smaller
differences are recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:axiom-provenance}.

It is impossible to measure the value of a signal at a point: a measurement must collect
input from a region of non-vanishing extent --- an \emph{extended point}
\sscite{florack1997image,fagerstrom2011spatiotemporal}.
What an observer holds is therefore a record of the signal at some resolution, never the
signal itself, and a multi-scale front end maintains a family of such records, indexed by
a scale parameter.

The front end is \emph{uncommitted}: it is built before anything is known about what the
signals will contain, so the family of records cannot be derived from a signal model. It
must be derived from requirements on the measurement process itself. Those requirements
are what this section states.

Two asymmetries distinguish time from space. The observer cannot access the future:
measurement must be causal. And the observer cannot return to the past: whatever of the
signal's history is to influence the present output must be carried in the observer's own
state --- the observer must \emph{embody} its past. Causality is a condition on the
measured values and becomes an axiom below (\S\ref{sec:measurement-causality}).
Embodiment is a condition on how a measurement can be realized, and it is not an axiom:
no requirement below mentions the observer's state. That the axioms nevertheless admit an
embodied realization is a result of the theory: under a mild standing hypothesis on the
family, stated in \S\ref{sec:signaling}, that section constructs the state a covariant
observer carries, and \S\ref{sec:jet} shows that the temporal derivatives of the signal
can be read off that state.

A temporal measurement is an operator that smooths the signal. Signals are integrable
transients, $X = \Lone$, with positive cone $X_+$ and $\int f := \int_{\RR} f(t)\,dt$; this
and the other notation used informally in this section is collected in
\S\ref{sec:preliminaries}.
Because a record is itself a signal, and is measured further in the cascade of
\S\ref{sec:measurement-cascade}, the measurement operators are indexed by ordered pairs
of scales,
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} : X \to X, \qquad 0 \le x \le y < \infty,
\]
with $\Phi_{x,y}$ carrying the record at scale $x$ to the record at the coarser scale
$y$, and the signal itself as the record at scale $0$. In the classical theory a single
scale index suffices; why two are needed here is explained in
\S\ref{sec:measurement-cascade}. The apertures --- the measures realizing these
operators --- are not postulated: that the operators are convolutions is recovered from
the axioms in \S\ref{sec:representation}, and which convolutions in
\S\ref{sec:characterization}.

One point of formulation is settled here, because the rest of the paper depends on it.
The extended point says that no record is the signal and no measurement stage is the
identity. It is more than a statement about what can be built: in the duality of state space and
device space of \sscite{florack1997image}, followed in \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}
with the signal as the state, the device is the part of the measurement one designs, and
designing it with an extended aperture is what makes the space of records differentiable
when the space of signals is not --- one reason to take the extended kernels, and not
the pure delays, as the objects of the theory. The formal theory is nevertheless set on the \emph{closed} half-line
$[0,\infty)$ of scales, with the signal as the record at scale $0$ and
$\Phi_{x,x} = \Id$ on the diagonal. Neither is a realizable measurement; both enter as
the limits the continuity requirement of \S\ref{sec:measurement-continuity} refers to,
in the same way that the Dirac measure enters the extended point axiom of
\sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, $\lim_{\tau \to 0}\phi_\tau = \delta$ with $\phi_\tau$
the kernel at scale $\tau$: as a limit the family approaches and never attains. Nothing
is measured \emph{at} scale $0$; every statement below is about the family and its
limits. The alternative --- taking ``a measurement of a measurement is a measurement''
as a formal requirement, every stage strictly smoothing --- leads to a discrete ladder of
scales and would still need the diagonal in its closure to state continuity at all; it is
not pursued. The same stance covers one degenerate member the axioms admit, the pure
delay, exhibited after Definition~\ref{def:cascade-family} and kept separate in the text
wherever a property fails on it.

\subsection{Linearity, positivity, and mass}\label{sec:measurement-linearity}

An uncommitted front end treats all intensity levels alike and prefers no signal feature
over another. Superposition is the operational form of that indifference: a device that
does not respect it responds to some combinations of inputs differently from the sum of
its responses, and has thereby singled those combinations out before anything is known
about the signals. So each $\Phi_{x,y}$ is linear. This is the assumption on which the
convolution route of \S\ref{sec:representation} rests; the nonlinear scale-space
axiomatics \sscite{alvarez1993axioms}, which give it up, are a different theory and
outside the scope of this paper. Measurement is also stable --- a small signal leaves a small record --- which on $X$ is
boundedness of each $\Phi_{x,y}$ (A1).

An intensity signal is nonnegative, and a measurement of nonnegative input that reports
negative values has created structure of its own --- structure of the apparatus rather
than of the signal. Measurement preserves the positive cone (A4).

In the spatial theory the corresponding normalization is \emph{gray-level invariance}:
smoothing an image may not change its average intensity, so the smoothing kernel has unit
area and the smoothing operator is conservative --- it redistributes intensity and
neither creates nor absorbs any. Read on integrable transients the invariant quantity is
the total mass $\int f$. This is (A5). A device that loses mass
models absorption rather than measurement; the passive variant is recorded in
Remark~\ref{rem:axiom-provenance} and not pursued.

\subsection{Causality}\label{sec:measurement-causality}

The record at time $t$ may depend on the signal only through its restriction to
$(-\infty, t]$: if the signal vanishes before some time $t_0$, so does every record of it
(A3). This is the one requirement with no spatial counterpart, and it removes the
Gaussian: the Gaussian aperture reads the future, and no truncation of it is compatible
with the cascade structure discussed below --- the characterization of
\S\ref{sec:characterization} shows that every admissible kernel is infinitely divisible,
which no truncated Gaussian is.

\subsection{Covariance}\label{sec:measurement-covariance}

A front end built before its signals has no preferred origin of time and no preferred
unit of time.

No preferred origin: each $\Phi_{x,y}$ commutes with the translations
\[
  (\shift_a f)(t) = f(t-a), \qquad a \in \RR,
\]
which is (A2).

No preferred unit: rescaling the time axis must carry the family into itself. The
mass-preserving dilations are
\[
  (\dil_\sigma f)(t) = \sigma^{-1} f(t/\sigma), \qquad \sigma > 0 .
\]
One point requires care before the requirement can be stated. Nothing so far ties the scale
index $x$ to a unit of time: the scale axis is, at this stage, only an ordered
parametrization of the records --- which is finer, which is coarser --- and the labels
themselves have no metric meaning. A rescaling of the time axis can therefore not be
asked to fix each operator; it can only be asked to map the family onto itself, with the
scale labels rearranged. Formally: there is a family $(\Sact_\sigma)_{\sigma>0}$ of
increasing bijections of $[0,\infty)$ with
\[
  \dil_\sigma\,\Phi_{x,y} \;=\; \Phi_{\Sact_\sigma x,\,\Sact_\sigma y}\,\dil_\sigma
  \qquad \text{for all } 0 \le x \le y,\ \sigma > 0,
\]
which is (A8) (Figure~\ref{fig:covariance-square}). The axioms, with the nondegeneracy
of \S\ref{sec:measurement-continuity}, determine each $\Sact_\sigma$ uniquely, and
\S\ref{sec:covariance} recovers a canonical scale parametrization from the action. We
demand \emph{full} covariance, every $\sigma > 0$. The discrete form
$\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$, a fixed ratio between successive scales, is a genuinely weaker
requirement with a theory of its own; \S\ref{sec:conclusions} places it.

\subsection{The cascade property}\label{sec:measurement-cascade}

A record is itself a signal and can be measured further. A record at a coarser scale must
then be computable from the record at any finer scale, without returning to the raw
signal: a measurement of a measurement is a measurement, and the family closes under
composition,
\[
  \Phi_{y,z}\,\Phi_{x,y} \;=\; \Phi_{x,z}, \qquad x \le y \le z,
  \qquad\qquad \Phi_{x,x} = \Id,
\]
which is (A6).

\input{fig-cascade}

This is the axiom the paper changes. In \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}, as throughout
the scale-space literature, the cascade is a one-parameter \emph{semigroup},
\[
  \Phi_\tau\,\Phi_{\tau'} \;=\; \Phi_{\tau + \tau'},
\]
and this single equation asserts two things at once (Figure~\ref{fig:cascade}). The
first is the closure just motivated: composites of measurements are measurements. The
second is tacit: the composite depends only on the total \emph{amount} of scale crossed.
Equivalently, the elementary smoothing applied between fine scales equals the one applied
between coarse scales --- measurement stages are interchangeable. Nothing in the
measurement picture supports the second assertion. A stage that carries a fine record to
a slightly coarser one and a stage that does the same deep in the hierarchy act on
different records, and there is no a priori reason the two operations should coincide.

The hemigroup form (A6) keeps the first assertion and drops the second, and nothing else:
scale becomes an ordered interval structure rather than an additive quantity. Two
consequences follow immediately. The family must be indexed by pairs, since an increment
$\Phi_{x,y}$ is no longer determined by the difference $y - x$. And the scale axis
acquires a gauge freedom --- any increasing reparametrization of $[0,\infty)$ yields the
same family relabelled --- which is why (A8) was stated with the relabelling
$\Sact_\sigma$ left free. \S\ref{sec:covariance} resolves the first: covariance
determines the gauge up to normalization. And the semigroup case reappears when
stage-interchangeability is reimposed: Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case} in
\S\ref{sec:characterization} returns the stable family of the semigroup theory together
with the pure delay.

\subsection{Continuity and nondegeneracy}\label{sec:measurement-continuity}

Measurements at nearby scales must be comparable: the record depends continuously on the
pair of scales, jointly, in the norm of $X$ (A7). Together with the diagonal condition
$\Phi_{x,x} = \Id$, continuity is the extended point in its two-parameter form: as
$y \downarrow x$ the record at scale $y$ recovers the record at scale $x$.

Finally, the scale axis carries no idle stretches: strictly coarser means strictly more
smoothed, $\Phi_{x,y} \ne \Id$ for $x < y$ (ND). Nondegeneracy is a normalization rather
than a restriction --- a family that stalls on an interval of scales is the same family
on a smaller scale axis.

\subsection{The axiom system}\label{sec:measurement-axioms}

% shared with blueprint def:cascade-family
\begin{definition}[causal cascade measurement family]\label{def:cascade-family}
A family of linear operators
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} : X \to X, \qquad 0 \le x \le y < \infty,
\]
is a \emph{causal cascade measurement family} if it satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(A1) Boundedness.} Each $\Phi_{x,y}$ is a bounded operator on $X$.
\item \textbf{(A2) Time-translation covariance.} $\Phi_{x,y}\,\shift_a = \shift_a\,\Phi_{x,y}$
for all $a \in \RR$.
\item \textbf{(A3) Causality.} If $f \in X$ vanishes a.e.\ on $(-\infty, t_0)$, then so does
$\Phi_{x,y} f$.
\item \textbf{(A4) Positivity.} $\Phi_{x,y} X_+ \subseteq X_+$.
\item \textbf{(A5) Unit area.} $\int \Phi_{x,y} f = \int f$ for all $f \in X_+$.
\item \textbf{(A6) Cascade (hemigroup).} $\Phi_{x,x} = \Id$, and
$\Phi_{y,z}\,\Phi_{x,y} = \Phi_{x,z}$ for all $x \le y \le z$.
\item \textbf{(A7) Continuity.} For every $f \in X$, the map $(x,y) \mapsto \Phi_{x,y} f$ is
continuous from $\{0 \le x \le y\}$ to $X$.
\item \textbf{(A8) Scale covariance.} There is a family $(\Sact_\sigma)_{\sigma > 0}$ of
increasing bijections of $[0,\infty)$ such that
\[
  \dil_\sigma\,\Phi_{x,y} = \Phi_{\Sact_\sigma x,\,\Sact_\sigma y}\,\dil_\sigma
  \qquad \text{for all } 0 \le x \le y,\ \sigma > 0.
\]
\item \textbf{(ND) Nondegeneracy.} $\Phi_{x,y} \ne \Id$ whenever $x < y$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Two members, at the two ends of what the axioms admit.

The \emph{pure delay},
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} = \shift_{y-x}, \qquad \Sact_\sigma x = \sigma x,
\]
satisfies every axiom at sight. It smooths nothing, and it is admitted for the reason the
identity is: it is the boundary the class closes on (Remark~\ref{rem:drift-boundary}).

The \emph{Gamma family} of \S\ref{sec:introduction},
\[
  \Phi_{x,y} f = \rho_{x,y} * f, \qquad
  \hat\rho_{x,y}(s) = \Bigl(\frac{1+xs}{1+ys}\Bigr)^{\gamma},
\]
with $\hat\rho$ the Laplace transform, satisfies (A6) because the transforms multiply,
(A8) with $\Sact_\sigma x = \sigma x$ because
$\hat\rho_{\sigma x,\sigma y}(s) = \hat\rho_{x,y}(\sigma s)$, and (A7) because the
transform is continuous in $(x,y)$. That $\rho_{x,y}$ is a probability measure on
$[0,\infty)$, which gives (A1)--(A5), is the one non-obvious point; it is the
self-decomposability of the Gamma law (\S\ref{sec:examples}).

Between these two the theory has to fit; \S\ref{sec:characterization} says exactly what
fits.

\begin{remark}[provenance of the axioms]\label{rem:axiom-provenance}
(A1)--(A5) and (A7) are as in \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal}; the diagonal condition in (A6)
together with (A7) is the two-parameter form of the \emph{extended point} axiom
$\lim_{\tau\to 0}\phi_\tau = \delta$, in the notation of that paper, $\phi_\tau$ the kernel
at scale $\tau$.

The substantive change is (A6). In \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} the cascade reads
$\Phi_\tau \Phi_{\tau'} = \Phi_{\tau + \tau'}$, which additionally assumes that the composite
measurement depends only on the total amount of scale --- that measurement stages are
interchangeable. (A6) drops that assumption, and every result that follows is a
consequence of dropping it. Two smaller differences: \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} also
required the kernels to be continuous functions, which the pure delay violates, and
dropping that requirement is what admits the drift term (Remark~\ref{rem:drift-boundary});
and (ND) is a normalization not stated there.

(A8) is the two-parameter form of scaling covariance; note that an increasing bijection of
$[0,\infty)$ automatically fixes $0$. (ND) excludes idle stretches of the scale axis.

A \emph{passive} variant replaces (A5) by $\int \Phi_{x,y} f \le \int f$ on $X_+$; all results
below hold with sub-probability kernels and an extra killing term, and we do not pursue it.
\end{remark}
