\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusions}

One axiom of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} was weakened --- the one-parameter semigroup
cascade to the two-parameter hemigroup, i.e.\ the assumption that measurement stages are
interchangeable --- and the entire derivation was rerun in the same order, with the same
analytic apparatus. What the uncommitted observer of \S\ref{sec:measurement} can be is now
settled: the admissible measurement families are exactly the self-decomposable Laplace
exponents, one nonincreasing memory density $k$ and one drift per family
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}). The class contains members with every moment
finite, and the heavy tails of the stable kernels are thereby exposed as the price of the
semigroup axiom, not of causality or covariance. The observer's embodiment of its past is
made exact: the memory line carries the analytic family of fractional integrals of the
signal, the scale evolution inverts into an evolution in present time under the unique
covariant inversion (Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}), and the temporal jet is an
instantaneous readout of that same state (\S\ref{sec:jet}). The local,
positivity-respecting media among these are classified --- the Bessel family, unfolding the
isolated heat case of the semigroup theory (Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}). Non-creation ---
the oldest axiom of the subject --- is recovered inside the class rather than assumed: the
variation-diminishing members are classified (Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-regularity}), the
semigroup obstruction that forbade causal non-creation dissolves in the hemigroup
(Remark~\ref{rem:pf-hemigroup}), and along the delay path the field is non-enhancing at
past-dominating extrema (Corollary~\ref{cor:past-dominating}). And the hemigroup
is itself the algorithm: the discrete cascade is exact at every set of scale knots
(Proposition~\ref{prop:knot-exactness}), with the memory-kernel and Sonine structure of
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} --- the scale-Cauchy problem, and the exact inverse of
each scale's memory operator --- reading the same function $k$ as the physical memory
kernel.

One methodological remark, since the formalization changed the paper rather than merely
certifying it. Five of the proofs printed above are not the ones first written. In each case
the original argument invoked a named theorem about a \emph{class} of functions ---
Bernstein--Widder, closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits, the semigroup property of the
Riemann--Liouville family, the asymptotics of $\Gamma$ on vertical lines, uniqueness of the
Mellin transform --- where an elementary argument about the \emph{object} suffices, and is no
longer. The formal development, which has no notion of complete monotonicity at all, could
not take the classical route and so found the elementary one; having found it, there was no
reason to keep the classical route on paper. The net effect is that the machine-checked results rest on
two cited facts where the classical route would have spent six or seven, and that the one
irreducible appeal to the derivative-sign definition ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, where $sF'(s)$ is the object of study --- is now
visible as the single place where that vocabulary is doing work rather than providing
convenience. We record this because the direction of influence is the opposite of the usual
expectation: verification is generally described as a check on a finished argument, and here
it shortened the argument.

The limits of what is done here should be stated as plainly as the results. The theory is
purely temporal; composition with a spatial scale space is untouched. The local corner is
bought at a price the implementation section makes exact: the Bessel family admits no
finite-dimensional time-recursive realization
(Remark~\ref{rem:local-implementation}), and --- recorded without proof in
Remark~\ref{rem:markov-media} --- the homogeneous members with $\alpha < \tfrac12$ admit no
Markov medium at all. The memory-line theory holds under the standing hypothesis (H), which
the extreme rays with $b_0 = 0$ sit on the boundary of and the Gamma family with
$\gamma \le 1$ fails. And the paper's numerical component
(Example~\ref{ex:numerical-experiment}) exercises the implementation on synthetic signals
only: what is delivered is a characterization and an exact implementation route, with no
validation on natural data.

Four problems remain. One is genuinely open here: the relaxation of hypothesis (H) by
meromorphic continuation, which would admit the slowly growing exponents --- for instance
the Gamma family with $\gamma \le 1$ --- into
\S\S\ref{sec:signaling}--\ref{sec:locality} and \S\ref{sec:jet}. The others are open in
this paper and taken up elsewhere. The memory-line inverse problem --- which admissible
exponents $F$ are realized by genuine Markov media, i.e.\ a correspondence of Krein type
(\sscite{kotani1982krein}; \sscite{dym1976gaussian}) admitting jumps, together with the
question of what surface data determine the medium --- is settled here only in its local,
positivity-respecting corner (Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}, discharging the promise of
Remark~\ref{rem:markov-media}) and is otherwise the subject of a separate article. The
complex-root cases of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-ladder}
(Remark~\ref{rem:complex-roots}) are largely resolved in a companion note, where
spectrally leading pairs are excluded and order three is settled, with the subordinate-pair
configuration remaining. And the discrete-covariance branch, whose place in the axiom
system is identified in Remark~\ref{rem:strata} below, has its structure theorem, together
with the canonical-selection question for the time-causal limit kernel, in the same
companion note.

\begin{remark}[the discrete-covariance stratum and the previous literature]\label{rem:strata}
Where do the earlier time-causal constructions sit relative to these axioms? The instrument
that answers the question is a weakening of (A8) to the subgroup $\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$,
$q > 1$. Most of \S\ref{sec:covariance} survives verbatim: uniqueness of $\Sact_q$, the
group law along the subgroup, and the absence of interior fixed points, the orbit argument
needing only the powers $q^{\pm n}$. The similarity solution generalizes: in the variables
$(\log(xs),\, \log(x/s))$ the covariance relation $G(qx, s) = G(x, qs)$ says precisely that
\[
  G(x, s) \;=\; \mathfrak{F}\bigl(x s;\ \log(x/s)\bigr),
  \qquad \mathfrak{F}(\,\cdot\,;\ v + 2\log q) = \mathfrak{F}(\,\cdot\,;\ v),
\]
with $\mathfrak{F}$ a profile, log-periodic in the second, \emph{chirp} variable --- a
modulation of the continuous case's $F(xs)$. The probabilistic identification is exact:
applying covariance once, the marginal exponents satisfy
\[
  \Psi_x(qs) - \Psi_x(s) \;=\; g_{x,\,qx}(s) \;\in\; \BFz
\]
--- a Bernstein function vanishing at the origin, \S\ref{sec:preliminaries} --- which is the
definition of a \emph{semi-selfdecomposable} law of span $q$
(\sscite{maejima1998semi}), and the two-parameter families are the \emph{semi-selfsimilar
additive} subordinators of \sscite{maejima1999semi}. The axiom system thus carries a
three-level stratification, and the earlier time-causal constructions occupy its strata
rather than lying outside it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(A1)--(A7) alone: Bernstein hemigroups.} This stratum contains the Poisson scale
space of \sscite{lindeberg1996scalespace}, which belongs to no higher stratum: with exponent
$\lambda(1 - e^{-\delta s})$ the $q$-step increment $\lambda(e^{-\delta s} - e^{-q\delta s})$
has a sign-changing derivative for every $q > 1$, so the fixed jump size $\delta$ is
incompatible with covariance under any dilation subgroup.
\item \emph{(A8) for $q^{\ZZ}$: semi-Sato families.} This stratum contains the time-causal
limit kernel of \sscite{lindeberg2016time}: its exponent
$F(s) = \sum_{i \ge 1} \log(1 + c\,q^{-i}s)$ telescopes under one covariance step,
\[
  F(qs) - F(s) \;=\; \log(1 + c\,s) \;\in\; \BFz,
\]
one $q$-step of scale per pole section. But more is true: the Lévy tail of $F$ is
$k(t) = \sum_{i \ge 1} e^{-t q^{i}/c}$, a sum of decreasing exponentials --- nonincreasing,
indeed completely monotone --- so $F$ is itself \emph{self-decomposable}, and the similarity
family $\{F(x s)\}_{x > 0}$ generated by its exponent is a fully covariant member of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}: the Thorin member of
Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass} whose measure is the geometric atom train
$\sum_i \delta_{q^i/c}$ in place of the Gamma family's single atom. The atoms carry unit
weight with finite mean, $\sum_i c\,q^{-i} = c/(q-1)$, so the family lies in the
variation-diminishing subclass of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-regularity} as well: its
kernels are P\'olya frequency and create no extrema. That similarity family
is thus, in retrospect, the first-discovered member of the class characterized in this
paper, and of its non-creation subclass --- the published construction itself is its
$q^{\ZZ}$-sampled form. To it the
knot-exactness of Proposition~\ref{prop:knot-exactness}, the Sonine-pair structure of
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory}, and the jets of \S\ref{sec:jet} (under (H), which
holds: $z_* = \infty$) transfer exactly; the finite \emph{cascade} of
\S\ref{sec:implementation} does not --- the atom train is infinite, so the rational
realizations of Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass} reach it only approximately, and the
finite truncations are hypoexponential and not dilation-closed
(Remark~\ref{rem:covariance-practice}). What is intrinsically discrete about it is the
cascade construction and the role of $q$ as the log-spacing of its Thorin atoms. Strict members of the middle stratum,
semi-selfdecomposable but not self-decomposable, exist and are natural --- geometric jump
ladders refining the Poisson scale space; the companion note constructs them and proves the
stratum's structure theorem.
\item \emph{(A8) in full: the Sato class of the present paper.}
\end{enumerate}

For the limit kernel the extension question has a clean answer, and the argument is short
enough to record here: among all $q^{\ZZ}$-covariant families with its $q$-step increments
$g_{x,qx}(s) = \log(1 + c x s)$, \emph{exactly one} extends to full continuous covariance
--- full covariance forces the similarity form $\Psi_x = F(x\,\cdot)$, the $q$-difference
equation $F(qs) - F(s) = \log(1+cs)$ determines $F$ up to a log-periodic summand, and a
continuous log-periodic function with limit $0$ at the origin vanishes --- namely the
limit-kernel family itself. Whether the limit kernel is distinguished \emph{within} its modulation orbit
by an extremality principle that does not invoke the continuum extension is the selection
question taken up in the companion note. The comparison with the previous literature is
thereby one of containment rather than difference: the Poisson scale space lies strictly in
the first stratum, the limit kernel in the third, and the strict middle stratum is populated
by their common refinement (Figure~\ref{fig:strata}).
\end{remark}
