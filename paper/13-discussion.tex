\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusions}

One axiom of \sscite{fagerstrom2005temporal} was weakened --- the one-parameter semigroup
cascade to the two-parameter hemigroup, i.e.\ the assumption that measurement stages are
interchangeable --- and the entire derivation was rerun in the same order with the same tools.
The kernel characterization (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}) enlarges the extremal
stable class to the self-decomposable (Sato) class; the Mellin signaling form
(Theorem~\ref{thm:signaling-form}) and the locality theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:locality}) are
the analogues of Theorems~4 and~5, with the isolated heat case unfolding into the Bessel
family and with the heavy tails of the stable kernels exposed as the price of the semigroup
axiom rather than of causality or covariance;
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory} carries the analogue of Theorem~3 --- the scale-Cauchy
problem --- together with the memory-kernel and Sonine structure, which identifies the
self-decomposability function $k$ as the physical memory kernel and losslessness as an exact
conservation identity.

One methodological remark, since the formalization changed the paper rather than merely
certifying it. Five of the proofs printed above are not the ones first written. In each case
the original argument invoked a named theorem about a \emph{class} of functions ---
Bernstein--Widder, closure of $\BF$ under pointwise limits, the semigroup property of the
Riemann--Liouville family, the asymptotics of $\Gamma$ on vertical lines, uniqueness of the
Mellin transform --- where an elementary argument about the \emph{object} suffices, and is no
longer. The formal development, which has no notion of complete monotonicity at all, could
not take the classical route and so found the elementary one; having found it, there was no
reason to keep the classical route on paper. The net effect is that the results here rest on
two cited facts rather than the six or seven a reader would expect, and that the one
irreducible appeal to the derivative-sign definition ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:selfdecomposable-exponents}, where $sF'(s)$ is the object of study --- is now
visible as the single place where that vocabulary is doing work rather than providing
convenience. We record this because the direction of influence is the opposite of the usual
expectation: verification is generally described as a check on a finished argument, and here
it shortened the argument.

Four problems remain open. First, the memory-line inverse problem: which admissible exponents
$F$ are realized by genuine Markov media (Remark~\ref{rem:markov-media}), i.e.\ a
correspondence of Krein type (\sscite{kotani1982krein}; \sscite{dym1976gaussian}) admitting
jumps, together with the question of what surface data determine the medium; this is the
subject of a separate article. Second, the complex-root cases of
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-ladder} --- largely resolved in a companion note, where
spectrally leading pairs are excluded and order three is settled, with the subordinate-pair
configuration remaining. Third, the relaxation of hypothesis (H) by meromorphic continuation,
which would admit the slowly growing exponents --- for instance the Gamma family with
$\gamma \le 1$ --- into \S\S\ref{sec:signaling}--\ref{sec:locality}. Fourth, the
discrete-covariance branch, whose place in the axiom system is identified in
Remark~\ref{rem:strata} below; its structure theorem, together with the canonical-selection
question for the time-causal limit kernel, is the subject of the same companion note.

\begin{remark}[the discrete-covariance stratum and the previous literature]\label{rem:strata}
Weaken (A8) to the subgroup $\sigma \in q^{\ZZ}$, $q > 1$. Most of \S\ref{sec:covariance}
survives verbatim: uniqueness of $\Sact_q$, the group law along the subgroup, and the absence
of interior fixed points, the orbit argument needing only the powers $q^{\pm n}$. The
similarity solution generalizes: in the variables $(\log(xs),\, \log(x/s))$ the covariance
relation $G(qx, s) = G(x, qs)$ says precisely that
\[
  G(x, s) \;=\; \mathfrak{F}\bigl(x s;\ \log(x/s)\bigr),
  \qquad \mathfrak{F}(\,\cdot\,;\ v + 2\log q) = \mathfrak{F}(\,\cdot\,;\ v),
\]
a log-periodic modulation of the continuous case's $F(xs)$ in the chirp variable
$\log(x/s)$. The probabilistic identification is exact: applying covariance once, the
marginal exponents satisfy
\[
  \Psi_x(qs) - \Psi_x(s) \;=\; g_{x,\,qx}(s) \;\in\; \BFz,
\]
which is the definition of a \emph{semi-selfdecomposable} law of span $q$
(\sscite{maejima1998semi}), and the two-parameter families are the \emph{semi-selfsimilar
additive} subordinators of \sscite{maejima1999semi}. The axiom system thus carries a
three-level stratification, and the earlier time-causal constructions occupy its strata
rather than lying outside it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(A1)--(A7) alone: Bernstein hemigroups.} This stratum contains the Poisson scale
space of \sscite{lindeberg1996scalespace}, which belongs to no higher stratum: with exponent
$\lambda(1 - e^{-\delta s})$ the $q$-step increment $\lambda(e^{-\delta s} - e^{-q\delta s})$
has a sign-changing derivative for every $q > 1$, so the fixed jump size $\delta$ is
incompatible with covariance under any dilation subgroup.
\item \emph{(A8) for $q^{\ZZ}$: semi-Sato families.} This stratum contains the time-causal
limit kernel of \sscite{lindeberg2016time}: its exponent
$F(s) = \sum_{i \ge 1} \log(1 + c\,q^{-i}s)$ telescopes under one covariance step,
\[
  F(qs) - F(s) \;=\; \log(1 + c\,s) \;\in\; \BFz,
\]
one $q$-step of scale per pole section. But more is true: the Lévy tail of $F$ is
$k(t) = \sum_{i \ge 1} e^{-t q^{i}/c}$, a sum of decreasing exponentials --- nonincreasing,
indeed completely monotone --- so $F$ is itself \emph{self-decomposable}, and the similarity
family $\{F(x s)\}_{x > 0}$ is a fully covariant member of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-characterization}: the Thorin member of
Proposition~\ref{prop:thorin-subclass} whose measure is the geometric atom train
$\sum_i \delta_{q^i/c}$ in place of the Gamma family's single atom. The limit kernel is thus,
in retrospect, the first-discovered member of the class characterized in this paper, and the
Sonine-pair structure of Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-memory}, the cascade implementation of
\S\ref{sec:implementation}, and the jets of \S\ref{sec:jet} apply to it verbatim. What is
intrinsically discrete about it is the cascade construction --- the finite truncations are
hypoexponential and not dilation-closed (Remark~\ref{rem:covariance-practice}) --- and the
role of $q$ as the log-spacing of its Thorin atoms. Strict members of the middle stratum,
semi-selfdecomposable but not self-decomposable, exist and are natural --- geometric jump
ladders refining the Poisson scale space; the companion note constructs them and proves the
stratum's structure theorem.
\item \emph{(A8) in full: the Sato class of the present paper.}
\end{enumerate}

For the limit kernel the extension question has a clean answer: among all
$q^{\ZZ}$-covariant families with its $q$-step increments $g_{x,qx}(s) = \log(1 + c x s)$,
\emph{exactly one} extends to full continuous covariance --- full covariance forces the
similarity form $\Psi_x = F(x\,\cdot)$, the $q$-difference equation
$F(qs) - F(s) = \log(1+cs)$ determines $F$ up to a log-periodic summand, and a continuous
log-periodic function with limit $0$ at the origin vanishes --- namely the limit-kernel
family itself. Whether the limit kernel is distinguished \emph{within} its modulation orbit
by an extremality principle that does not invoke the continuum extension is the selection
question taken up in the companion note. The comparison with the previous literature is
thereby one of containment rather than difference: the Poisson scale space lies strictly in
the first stratum, the limit kernel in the third, and the strict middle stratum is populated
by their common refinement.
\end{remark}
