\section{Scale covariance: the similarity form}\label{sec:covariance}

Covariance now turns the free relabelling $\Sact_\sigma$ of (A8) into a coordinate: the
action is rigid, its orbit through $1$ parametrizes the scale axis, and in the resulting
gauge the accumulated exponent is a dilate of a single function, $G(x,s) = F(\chi(x)s)$ ---
so that the exponent of any kernel is a difference of two dilates of $F$, which is the form
\S\ref{sec:characterization} characterizes. In the optical reading of
Remark~\ref{rem:optical-depth}, the section says: the covariance equation makes the medium
\emph{self-similar} --- descending the scale axis is indistinguishable from probing faster
--- and the rigidity lemma says a self-similar medium has no marked layer. The gauge is the
coordinate in which the self-similarity becomes homogeneity, one absorption spectrum $F$
read through the dimensionless product $\chi(x)\,s$. The first lemma only translates (A8)
into the language of kernels and exponents.

% shared with blueprint lem:covariance-laplace
\begin{lemma}[Laplace form of covariance]\label{lem:covariance-laplace}
Under (A1)--(A5), the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item axiom (A8);
\item $\dil_\sigma\,\mu_{x,y} = \mu_{\Sact_\sigma x,\,\Sact_\sigma y}$ for all
$0 \le x \le y$, $\sigma > 0$;
\item $g_{\Sact_\sigma x,\,\Sact_\sigma y}(s) = g_{x,y}(\sigma s)$ for all
$0 \le x \le y$, $\sigma, s > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, under (A8),
\[
  G(\Sact_\sigma x,\, s) = G(x,\, \sigma s)
  \qquad \text{for all } x \ge 0,\ \sigma, s > 0. \tag{6.1}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The compatibility $\dil_\sigma(\mu * f) = (\dil_\sigma\mu) * (\dil_\sigma f)$ turns (A8) into
the statement that $\dil_\sigma\mu_{x,y}$ and $\mu_{\Sact_\sigma x, \Sact_\sigma y}$ represent
the same operator, and the uniqueness clause of Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation}
makes that an identity of measures; this is (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2). For (2)$\Leftrightarrow$(3),
$\widehat{\dil_\sigma\mu}(s) = \hat\mu(\sigma s)$, so taking $-\log$ gives
$g_{\Sact_\sigma x, \Sact_\sigma y}(s) = g_{x,y}(\sigma s)$. Equation (6.1) is the case
$x = 0$ of that identity together with $\Sact_\sigma 0 = 0$, which holds because an increasing
bijection of $[0,\infty)$ fixes $0$; here $G(x,s) = g_{0,x}(s)$, which needs only
Lemma~\ref{lem:convolution-representation}.
\end{proof}

What the translated axiom buys is rigidity. Nothing in (A8) says that the relabellings
$\Sact_\sigma$ are unique, compose, or move every point; the next lemma says all three, and
its engine is one fact --- for fixed $s$ the accumulated exponent $G(\cdot, s)$ is injective
(Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}) --- so that (6.1) reads $\Sact_\sigma$ off $G$.
Clause~(1) is the claim made in \S\ref{sec:measurement-covariance}, that the axioms with (ND)
determine each $\Sact_\sigma$.

% shared with blueprint lem:action-rigidity
\begin{lemma}[rigidity of the action]\label{lem:action-rigidity}
Assume (A1)--(A8) and (ND). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each $\sigma$, $\Sact_\sigma$ is uniquely determined by (6.1).
\item $\Sact_\sigma \Sact_\tau = \Sact_{\sigma\tau}$ for all $\sigma, \tau > 0$, and
$\Sact_1 = \Id$.
\item $\sigma \mapsto \Sact_\sigma x$ is continuous and strictly increasing for each $x > 0$.
\item The action has no fixed point in $(0,\infty)$: if $\Sact_\sigma x^* = x^*$ for all
$\sigma$, then $x^* = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(1) Uniqueness.} If $\Sact$ and $\Sact'$ both satisfy (6.1) then
$G(\Sact_\sigma x, s) = G(x,\sigma s) = G(\Sact'_\sigma x, s)$ for every $s > 0$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}, $G(\cdot, s)$ is strictly increasing, hence
injective, so $\Sact_\sigma x = \Sact'_\sigma x$.

\emph{(2) The group law.} Applying (6.1) twice,
\[
  G(\Sact_\sigma \Sact_\tau x, s) = G(\Sact_\tau x, \sigma s) = G(x, \sigma\tau s)
  = G(\Sact_{\sigma\tau} x, s),
\]
and injectivity of $G(\cdot,s)$ gives $\Sact_\sigma \Sact_\tau = \Sact_{\sigma\tau}$. Taking
$\sigma = 1$ in (6.1) gives $G(\Sact_1 x, s) = G(x,s)$, whence $\Sact_1 = \Id$.

\emph{(3) Continuity and strict monotonicity in $\sigma$.} Fix $x > 0$ and any $s_0 > 0$. By
(6.1), $G(\Sact_\sigma x, s_0) = G(x, \sigma s_0) = g_{0,x}(\sigma s_0)$.

The map $\sigma \mapsto g_{0,x}(\sigma s_0)$ is continuous, and it is \emph{strictly}
increasing: $g_{0,x}$ is a nonzero member of $\BFz$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity},
and a nonzero member of $\BFz$ is strictly increasing, because its derivative is completely
monotone and not identically zero, hence strictly positive on $(0,\infty)$ --- a completely
monotone function vanishing at one point vanishes beyond it, and vanishing on an interval
forces the representing measure, and so the function, to be zero.

The map $G(\cdot, s_0)$ is continuous (Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity}) and strictly increasing
(Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}), so it has a continuous strictly increasing inverse
on its range. Composing,
$\Sact_\sigma x = G(\cdot,s_0)^{-1}\bigl(g_{0,x}(\sigma s_0)\bigr)$ is continuous and strictly
increasing in $\sigma$.

\emph{(4) No interior fixed point.} Suppose $\Sact_\sigma x^* = x^*$ for all $\sigma > 0$.
Then (6.1) gives $G(x^*, s) = G(x^*, \sigma s)$ for all $\sigma, s > 0$, so $G(x^*, \cdot)$ is
constant on $(0,\infty)$; its value is $G(x^*, \zp) = 0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity}. Hence
$g_{0,x^*} \equiv 0$, i.e.\ $\mu_{0,x^*} = \delta_0$ and $\Phi_{0,x^*} = \Id$. By (ND) this
forces $x^* = 0$.
\end{proof}

Three of the four clauses are injectivity of $G(\cdot, s)$ and nothing else; (3) is the one
that needs more, namely that $g_{0,x}$ is strictly increasing in $s$. Clause~(4) is where
(ND) bites in this section: a fixed point $x^*$ of the action would be a scale at which
$\Phi_{0,x^*} = \Id$, an idle stretch. Without (ND) the orbit below could stall there
and there would be no coordinate at all. \S\ref{sec:measurement-continuity} called (ND) a
normalization; this is what the normalization is for.

The coordinate itself is the orbit. Fix the point $1$ on the scale axis and follow it under the
action: $\sigma \mapsto \Sact_\sigma 1$ is continuous, strictly increasing, and --- having no
fixed point to stall at --- sweeps the whole axis. So every scale $x > 0$ is reached by exactly
one $\sigma$, and labelling $x$ by that $\sigma$ straightens the action into multiplication
(Figure~\ref{fig:orbit-gauge}).

\begin{proposition}[orbit coordinate; the gauge]\label{prop:canonical-gauge}
Assume (A1)--(A8) and (ND). Then $c(\sigma) := \Sact_\sigma 1$ is a continuous strictly
increasing bijection of $(0,\infty)$ onto $(0,\infty)$ with $c(1) = 1$. Consequently
\[
  \chi := c^{-1} : (0,\infty) \to (0,\infty), \qquad \chi(0) := 0,
\]
is an increasing bijection --- \emph{the canonical gauge} --- satisfying
$\chi(\Sact_\sigma x) = \sigma\,\chi(x)$, and
\[
  G(x, s) = F\bigl(\chi(x)\, s\bigr), \qquad
  F := G(1, \cdot) \in \BFz,\ F \not\equiv 0. \tag{6.2}
\]
Equivalently $F \in \LE$, with a drift $b_0 \ge 0$ and a Lévy measure $\nu$ on $(0,\infty)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$c$ is continuous} by Lemma~\ref{lem:action-rigidity}(3), and $c(1) = \Sact_1 1 = 1$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:action-rigidity}(2).

\emph{$c$ is injective.} Suppose $c(\sigma) = c(\tau)$ with $\rho := \sigma/\tau \ne 1$. The
group law \ref{lem:action-rigidity}(2) gives $\Sact_\rho 1 = 1$, so by (6.1)
\[
  F(s) = G(1,s) = G(\Sact_\rho 1, s) = G(1, \rho s) = F(\rho s)
  \qquad \text{for all } s > 0 .
\]
Iterating along $\rho^{\pm n}$ gives $F(s) = F(\rho^n s)$ for every $n \in \ZZ$. Replacing
$\rho$ by $\rho^{-1}$ if necessary we may take $\rho < 1$, and then $\rho^n s \to 0$, so
$F(s) = F(\zp) = 0$ for every $s$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity}. Thus $F \equiv 0$, which
contradicts Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity} under (ND).

\emph{$c$ is increasing.} Being continuous and injective on the connected set $(0,\infty)$,
$c$ is strictly monotone, so it suffices to exclude the decreasing case. Suppose $c$ were
decreasing and fix $\sigma > 1$, so that $\Sact_\sigma 1 < 1$. Since $G(\cdot, s)$ is strictly
increasing (Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}),
\[
  G(\Sact_\sigma 1, s) < G(1,s) = F(s) \qquad \text{for every } s > 0 .
\]
But (6.1) evaluates the left-hand side as $G(1,\sigma s) = F(\sigma s)$, and $F$ is
nondecreasing, being a Bernstein function, so $F(\sigma s) \ge F(s)$. The two are
incompatible, so $c$ is increasing.

\emph{$c$ is onto $(0,\infty)$.} Its limits as $\sigma \to \zp$ and $\sigma \to \infty$ exist
in $[0,\infty]$ by monotonicity, and each is a fixed point of the action or a boundary point;
by Lemma~\ref{lem:action-rigidity}(4) there is no fixed point in $(0,\infty)$, so the limits
are $0$ and $\infty$. With continuity, $c$ is a bijection onto $(0,\infty)$.

\emph{The gauge relation.} By the group law, $\Sact_\sigma x = \Sact_\sigma \Sact_{\chi(x)} 1
= \Sact_{\sigma\chi(x)} 1$, so $\chi(\Sact_\sigma x) = \sigma\chi(x)$.

\emph{The similarity form.} For $x > 0$, writing $x = \Sact_{\chi(x)} 1$ and using (6.1),
\[
  G(x, s) = G\bigl(\Sact_{\chi(x)} 1,\, s\bigr) = G\bigl(1,\, \chi(x)\, s\bigr)
  = F\bigl(\chi(x)\,s\bigr),
\]
and $F = g_{0,1} \in \BFz$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:increments-bernstein}, with $F \not\equiv 0$ by
(ND) through Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-monotonicity}. The case $x = 0$ is
$G(0,\cdot) = 0 = F(0)$.
\end{proof}

In the semigroup case the straightening is the classical reparametrization: in the
semigroup's own additive parameter $\tau$ the accumulated exponent is
$G(\tau, s) = \tau s^{\alpha}$, so the action is $\Sact_\sigma \tau = \sigma^{\alpha}\tau$
and the gauge is $\chi(\tau) = \tau^{1/\alpha}$; in the straightened coordinate
$x = \tau^{1/\alpha}$ the action is $\Sact_\sigma x = \sigma x$ and $G = F(xs)$ with
$F(s) = s^{\alpha}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:semigroup-case};
Remark~\ref{rem:recovering-thm3} makes the identification $\tau = x^{\alpha}$ explicit),
the two coordinates coinciding only for the pure delay $\alpha = 1$. The proposition says
that the hemigroup case differs from this only by the generality of the reparametrization
and the freedom in $F$.

\begin{remark}[gauge freedom]\label{rem:gauge-freedom}
In the hemigroup setting the scale coordinate carries no intrinsic parametrization: replacing
$x$ by any increasing bijection of $[0,\infty)$ yields an equivalent family.
Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-gauge} fixes the \emph{canonical gauge} $\tilde x := \chi(x)$,
in which $\Sact_\sigma$ acts by multiplication and $G(x,s) = F(\tilde x\, s)$. In it, scale
carries the dimension of time: $s$ is dual to time, and $\tilde x s$ must be dimensionless for
$F(\tilde x s)$ to make sense. The canonical gauge is the working gauge of the rest of the
paper.

The other homogeneous gauges are $c\,\tilde x^{\,\alpha}$, $c, \alpha > 0$ (the
\emph{$\alpha$-gauge}). One of them
recurs, for the Bessel family of \S\ref{sec:examples}--\ref{sec:locality}: the
\emph{parabolic gauge} $\xi$, defined by
\[
  \tilde x = \xi^2 ,
\]
under which that family's memory-line evolution is exactly the Bessel operator
$\tfrac12\,\partial_\xi^2 + \tfrac{\beta}{\xi}\,\partial_\xi$ --- the heat-equation form; the
factor $\tfrac12$ arises from the substitution itself, as for Brownian motion at $a = \tfrac12$.
It is named at each use and never written $\tilde x$.
\end{remark}
